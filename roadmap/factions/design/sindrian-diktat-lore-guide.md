## Canon anchors

The Sindrian Diktat is a military dictatorship founded after Admiral Philip Andrada's
Hegemony intervention in the Askonia Crisis. Its self-image centers on unity, strength,
clarity of purpose, and rejection of weak parliamentary division. Canon locations and
dialogue depict propaganda, rationing, secret police, officer-bureaucrats, labor
camps, factional elites, and the omnipresent image of the Lion of Sindria.

The Diktat's power rests heavily on Askonia's antimatter-fuel industry and a compact
territorial base. Current doctrine is aggressive, rigid, warship-heavy, non-phase,
and deliberately non-randomized, mixing ballistic, energy, and missile systems. Heavy
armaments are illegal for civilians. Lion's Guard has its own faction id and superior
hull quality, size, aggression, and prestige equipment, but remains an elite Diktat
institution rather than a separate polity.

## Strong ground-war inferences

- Diktat ground power combines state-security troops, regular forces, industrial
  guards, conscripts or coerced labor controls, and politically privileged formations.
- Doctrine prizes rehearsed concentration, visible force, loyalty, and execution of a
  central plan. Initiative is safest when it can be presented as fulfilling orders.
- Fuel works, ports, propaganda systems, police archives, and regime command nodes are
  both practical and political centers of gravity.
- Ordinary formations receive serviceable mixed technology; the best equipment and
  presentation concentrate around the Lion's Guard and politically important sites.
- Internal rivalries, succession anxiety, and fear of disgrace can drive contradictory
  orders beneath the public image of unity.

## Marine Operations direction

### Units and armor

Use conscript or regular line troops, state-security squads, industrial guards,
military police, riot-control units, engineers, political officers, and Lion's Guard
shock formations. Baseline armor is standardized and imposing but may show rationed
maintenance. Guard armor should be better finished, aggressively branded, and paired
with privileged support rather than merely recolored.

Heavy battlesuits fit refinery security, palace or command defense, and selected
Guard assault units. Keep the prestige concentration visible: elite equipment should
create objectives, resentment, and salvage interest.

### Weapons and equipment

Prefer a controlled mix of slug rifles, squad automatics, grenade launchers, energy
carbines, heavy ballistic support, and missiles. Diktat prototypes or Tri-Tachyon-
assisted systems fit Guard or test formations, while ordinary troops retain reliable
midline and low-tech arms. Crowd-control gear, surveillance, encrypted command nets,
and fuel-facility hazard equipment broaden the roster beyond battlefield weapons.

The civilian ban on heavy armaments makes caches, stolen weapons, and seized police
arsenals politically meaningful objectives.

### Tactics and command

Favor rigid strongpoints, concentrated response, decisive counterattack, visible
command posts, and low tolerance for unauthorized withdrawal. Lion's Guard units may
be aggressive and lavishly supported without being universally competent; patronage
can coexist with real training. Ordinary officers should become brittle when the plan,
communications, or political supervision collapses.

### Facilities and objectives

Good sites include fuel refineries, pipeline junctions, lobster-export facilities,
labor compounds, police ministries, broadcast centers, ration depots, parade grounds,
palace approaches, prototype ranges, and Guard arsenals. Architecture should layer
industrial necessity, Hegemony inheritance, improvised post-crisis repair, and regime
spectacle.

Capturing refinery controls, safety interlocks, command archives, broadcast authority,
or secret-police records can avert industrial catastrophe, fracture loyalty, expose a
faction, or unlock orbital support. The Diktat normally preserves its own fuel plant;
an order to destroy it implies desperate denial, a purge, false flag, succession
struggle, or willingness to punish Askonia for disobedience.

### Contracts and voice

Credible offers include protection of fuel production, suppression of rebellion,
recovery of state property, Guard prestige operations, dissident capture, evidence
removal, prototype security, and factional jobs disguised as service to the Diktat.
Briefings invoke order, unity, quotas, loyalty, the Great Plan, and personal authority.
Always decide whether the true patron is the state, Lion's Guard, a ministry, an
industrial combine, or an ambitious insider.

## Anti-caricature rules

- Do not make every Diktat soldier a zealot for Andrada. Fear, career, local identity,
  nationalism, privilege, and survival all sustain the regime.
- Do not make the Lion's Guard simply “better Diktat.” It is politically privileged,
  prestigious, and doctrinally rigid, with its own institutional distortions.
- Do not destroy fuel infrastructure casually; it is the regime's strategic base.
- Do not confuse rigid planning with passive defense. Canon doctrine is highly
  aggressive.
