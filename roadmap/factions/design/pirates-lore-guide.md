## Canon anchors

“Pirates” are not one government. The label covers bandits, brigands, deserters,
looters, mercenaries, free captains, and people pushed out of mainstream Sector
society. Many captains understand themselves as free challengers, avengers, or
audacious survivors rather than criminals. Pirate ports sustain trade and tense local
order through reputation, bravado, bargains, and force even while pirate fleets prey
on outsiders.

Current doctrine favors many low-quality, highly aggressive, highly randomized ships,
converted freighters, broad scavenged technology, and willingness to fight to the
last. Pirate markets trade under hostility and transponder ambiguity, form temporary
bases, and operate through decentralized leadership. Canon also shows substantial
off-book economic activity linking pirates, smugglers, undocumented populations, and
otherwise legitimate trade.

## Strong ground-war inferences

- A pirate force reflects its captain, port, trade, and recent prizes. Organization
  can range from desperate raiders to a stable warlord's veteran household.
- Equipment is acquired through salvage, theft, black markets, conversion, local
  workshops, and defection. Maintenance quality varies more than technological origin.
- Loot access, escape, leverage, and reputation are common tactical priorities.
  Holding empty ground for its own sake is unusual.
- Skilled pirates exploit service tunnels, cargo systems, civilian traffic, false
  identification, ambush, and knowledge bought from insiders.
- Pirate communities still need food, air, water, repair, markets, and rules. A base
  that destroys every useful asset cannot remain a base.

## Marine Operations direction

### Units and armor

Use pressed or desperate crew, raiders, veteran boarders, smugglers, enforcers,
demolition specialists, technical scavengers, bodyguards, and a captain's distinctive
elite. Armor may combine civilian pressure gear, load-bearing frames, stolen plates,
industrial exoskeletons, suit components from several factions, and visibly dangerous
heavy-rig modifications.

Quality should vary by formation and patron. A famous free captain's boarders can be
excellent without making the entire pirate faction elite.

### Weapons and equipment

Prefer common slug weapons, shredder or flechette carbines, squad automatics, stolen
gauss or energy arms, improvised launchers, breaching tools, combat stims, carried
charges, and jury-rigged support weapons. Mixed magazines, hand-marked controls,
cut-down stocks, safety bypasses, and repaired power cells convey provenance.

Improvised IEDs and stims need explicit risk and finite issue. Rare martyr-style rigs
may appear through Pather contact or a particular extremist crew, but are not generic
pirate identity.

### Tactics and command

Favor ambush, opportunistic massing, flank access, suppression followed by a rush,
loot security, hostage leverage, and disengagement when the prize no longer justifies
the fight. Trapped or fanatically loyal crews may fight to destruction; other bands
should break when leadership, escape craft, or promised loot is lost.

### Facilities and objectives

Good sites include hidden docks, stripped industrial stations, cargo warrens,
salvage yards, illicit clinics, fuel dumps, fortified bars, chop shops, jury-rigged
shield plants, captured batteries, and mixed civilian habitation. Layouts should show
successive owners and shortcuts rather than one coherent construction standard.

Capturing the loot vault, docking control, escape craft, captain, workshop, black-
market ledger, hostages, or power plant can end a fight more credibly than clearing
every room. Pirates may destroy infrastructure for ransom, revenge, terror, denial,
or because they cannot carry it—but profitable capture and stripping are often better.

### Contracts and voice

Credible offers include theft, rescue of captured crew, recovery of a prize, attack on
a rival captain, ransom leverage, sabotage, smuggling access, defense of a port, and
work against an authority that cannot admit dealing with pirates. Briefings speak in
shares, grudges, reputation, opportunity, risk, crew loyalty, and practical detail.
Always identify the band or patron; “the pirates” do not issue one collective policy.

## Anti-caricature rules

- Do not make all pirates incoherent murderers. Predation can coexist with trade,
  loyalty, grievance, and local governance.
- Do not romanticize them into harmless freedom fighters. Their freedom often rests
  on violence and costs outsiders dearly.
- Do not make scavenged equipment automatically weak; condition, integration, and
  training decide whether a captured system works.
- Do not use randomness as a substitute for a named crew's doctrine.
