## Canon anchors

Tri-Tachyon is the Sector's most powerful post-Collapse megacorporation. Its core
competencies include AI, high-power devices, force fields, energy conversion, and
advanced armaments; its products and investments reach even hostile societies. Its
corporate charter is itself described as algorithmically self-modifying, making the
company less a conventional state than a durable machine of ownership, contracts,
departments, and human corporate officers.

Current doctrine favors a small number of high-quality high-tech hulls, energy
weapons, shields, phase ships, electronic warfare, flux expertise, and cybernetic
augmentation. Canon locations contrast glittering arcologies, executive privilege,
security drones, black sites, indebted labor, proprietary life support, hidden
arsenals, and aggressive protection of trade secrets. The Phantom and Revenant make
covert troop and logistics operations a direct faction capability.

## Strong ground-war inferences

- Tri-Tachyon prefers compact, well-equipped security, response, and black-operations
  teams over mass conscript formations.
- Corporate security protects assets, data, personnel categories, and continuity of
  profitable operation; humanitarian outcomes are often contractual or reputational.
- Surveillance, access control, remote systems, drones, electronic warfare, and
  proprietary interfaces shape the battlefield before direct fire begins.
- Contractors and mercenaries provide scale and deniability. Their gear may be mixed,
  while trusted internal teams receive the best integrated systems.
- Withdrawal from a bad trade is rational when data, specialists, or prototypes can
  be saved. A site is not sacred merely because the company owns it.

## Marine Operations direction

### Units and armor

Use corporate security, facility response teams, technical specialists, contract
troops, drone controllers, executive-protection cadres, black-operations infiltrators,
and rare heavy breach assets. Light and line armor should use sleek composite shells,
sealed seams, sensor clusters, proprietary connectors, and low-profile assistance.
Heavy suits fit hazardous prototype recovery and decisive breach work, not a massed
walking-wall doctrine.

Optical camouflage, neural/HUD uplinks, cybernetic interfaces, and drone pairing are
explicitly issued capabilities when implemented. They are not invisible bonuses on
every employee with a blue uniform.

### Weapons and equipment

Prefer pulse and laser carbines, precision gauss weapons, smart micro-missiles,
compact breaching tools, electronic countermeasures, sensor packages, and autonomous
or teleoperated support. Battery logistics should be visible rather than hand-waved:
charging racks, thermal management, capacitors, and proprietary cells make good
objectives and failure points.

Slug weapons remain plausible for contractors, cost-controlled facilities, and jobs
where reliability or local supply wins. Combat stims fit performance-managed security
or deniable teams when their risks and finite issue remain visible.

### Tactics and command

Favor information advantage, selective commitment, crossfires, remote observation,
displacement from poor trades, local counterattack around high-value assets, and
deliberate extraction. Tri-Tachyon should feel precise and unfair when its systems are
working, brittle or ruthlessly pragmatic when networks and proprietary support fail.

### Facilities and objectives

Good locations include arcology security levels, research campuses, prototype vaults,
data centers, drone-control rooms, clean fabrication cells, executive shelters,
subscription-locked utility plants, sensor arrays, shield generators, and hidden
surface arsenals. Public-facing areas can be immaculate while service levels expose
debt, subcontracting, and improvised maintenance.

Capturing network authority, encryption stores, drone control, shield regulation,
prototype records, or power conditioning can disable defenses without destroying the
investment. Tri-Tachyon may authorize destruction of a compromised black site or
competitor facility, but normally prefers theft, foreclosure, disruption, or evidence
removal when those produce a cleaner balance sheet.

### Contracts and voice

Credible offers include asset recovery, executive extraction, industrial espionage,
prototype field trials, data sanitization, union or pirate suppression, competitor
sabotage, deniable raids, and restoring a revenue-critical facility. Briefings speak
in risk, deliverables, proprietary rights, liability, continuity, incentives, and
carefully limited disclosure. Individual patrons can be idealistic researchers or
frightened middle managers; the company still treats the operation as a transaction.

## Anti-caricature rules

- Do not make every Tri-Tachyon force an army of autonomous AI. The company uses
  humans, contractors, drones, and taboo systems in uneven and politically constrained
  combinations.
- Do not make high technology flawless. It is scarce, thermally demanding,
  proprietary, and dependent on infrastructure.
- Do not confuse corporate rationality with perfect coordination; departments,
  careers, secrecy, and incentives can work at cross-purposes.
- Do not make every mission gratuitously evil. Exploitation, ambition, innovation,
  and genuine employee loyalty can coexist.
