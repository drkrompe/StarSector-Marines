## Canon anchors

The Church of Galactic Redemption is a hierarchical, syncretic faith inspired by
Ludd's martyrdom. It offers an agrarian, humanistic, prayerful life against the
violence, alienation, transhumanism, and perceived moral corruption of post-Domain
technology. That position is not a rejection of every machine: the Church fields
starships, heavy industry, weapons, armor, carriers, and some enormous Domain-era
warships while debating what technology is righteous, necessary, or corrupting.

Current doctrine favors numerous, aggressive, low-quality low-tech forces with armor,
ballistics, unguided or simple missiles, converted civilian hulls, mining weapons,
and old designs. Canon worlds emphasize farming, pilgrimage, shrines, monasteries,
local hierarchy, and the Church's uneasy coexistence with secular power and modern
war. The Knights of Ludd are its distinct military branch and receive their own guide.

## Strong ground-war inferences

- Most Church ground defense begins with local militia, constables, retainers, and
  workers who know the land, reinforced by better-equipped faithful and clergy-led
  formations.
- Maintenance culture values longevity, repair, craft knowledge, and moral sanction.
  An old weapon can be prestigious because generations kept it serviceable.
- Defense of homes, farms, water, shrines, and community infrastructure is more
  characteristic than aggressive pursuit for abstract operational gain.
- The Church can employ sophisticated defensive technology while controlling who may
  use it and explaining its use through doctrine, ritual, and necessity.
- Large formations and social cohesion compensate for uneven standardization and
  limited access to pristine equipment.

## Marine Operations direction

### Units and armor

Use parish militia, settlement defenders, household or temple guards, veteran
regulars, combat engineers, shrine wardens, and rare sanctioned heavy-armor warriors.
Line suits should look maintained rather than factory-fresh: replacement seals,
hand-fitted plates, devotional markings, and locally made covers over legacy powered
frames. Heavy battlesuits may be treated as consecrated burdens entrusted after
training and spiritual scrutiny.

Keep Knights separate when the fiction calls for the Church's professional military
arm. A Church militia squad should not become a Knight squad merely because it is
elite in gameplay terms.

### Weapons and equipment

Prefer rugged slug rifles, gauss weapons where maintained, squad automatics, simple
grenade and missile systems, crew-served ballistic guns, and industrial tools adapted
for defense. Arc cutters and thermal breachers fit miners, engineers, and sanctioned
breach teams. Rare energy carbines may appear as inherited, captured, or specially
approved tools; they should carry social context rather than a blanket taboo exemption.

Avoid giving ordinary Church units Pather martyr rigs or ubiquitous illicit stims.
Shared faith does not collapse the institutional divide between Church and Path.

### Tactics and command

Favor tenacious defense of inhabited and sacred places, massed local response,
prepared firing positions, short counterattacks, and limited pursuit beyond the
community's protective perimeter. Veteran or Knight-led units can organize a strong
line without turning every militia formation into professional shock infantry.

### Facilities and objectives

Good locations include monasteries, shrines, grain stores, water and soil systems,
communal workshops, pilgrim ports, old arsenals, shelter tunnels, batteries concealed
near settlements, and estates whose secular and religious authority overlap. Spaces
should show repaired inheritance, human labor, communal ritual, and selective high
technology embedded in older fabric.

Capturing a shrine, reliquary, water plant, granary, shelter, battery fire-control
room, or sanctioned archive carries moral and political weight beyond its raw utility.
Church patrons strongly prefer preserving their own food, habitation, and sacred
sites. Destruction is credible against an abomination, desecrated site, invading
war-machine, or irrecoverably corrupted cache—not as generic anti-technology policy.

### Contracts and voice

Credible offers include settlement defense, pilgrim rescue, recovery of a shrine,
protection of harvest or water, suppression of raiders, recovery or destruction of a
dangerous relic, and intervention where the Church cannot openly deploy Knights.
Briefings invoke stewardship, community, duty, temptation, redemption, and the moral
character of the objective. Patrons may be compassionate, paternalistic, political,
scholarly, or severe.

## Anti-caricature rules

- Do not make the Church technologically helpless. Its distinction is governed and
  morally framed use, not the absence of advanced machinery.
- Do not make all believers Pathers-in-waiting. The Church is hierarchical, settled,
  and invested in preserving ordinary life.
- Do not make agrarian worlds empty medieval villages; they remain interstellar
  societies with ports, industry, environmental systems, and planetary defenses.
- Do not use faith as a universal morale or combat-stat bonus.
