# Luddic Path lore guide

Status: ACTIVE — implementation reference for Luddic Path ground-combat content.

Written: 2026-08-26

## Canon anchors

The Luddic Path is a decentralized category of radical apocalyptic sects that claim a
truer Message than the settled Church. Pathers see the present as Armageddon and
violent action against humanity's technological hubris as redemptive. They are at
odds with nearly every established faction, but do not form one clean hierarchy.

Current doctrine has maximum aggression, numerous low-quality ships, heavy use of
converted civilian hulls, broad refit variation, and no concern for conventional
fleet preservation. Canon mechanics and text associate Pather cells with worlds using
advanced technology, covert watchers, tithes, sabotage, improvised conversion, and
violent escalation. Their fleets still require real logistics, weapons, crews, and
support networks.

## Strong ground-war inferences

- Ground forces are cells, sympathizers, infiltrators, local converts, veteran
  militants, and a smaller number of charismatic or technically skilled organizers.
- Surprise, concealment among civilian life, sabotage, propaganda, target familiarity,
  and willingness to accept loss compensate for weak conventional supply.
- Industrial and civilian tools are converted aggressively because they are available
  and deniable, not because Pathers are incapable of using military technology.
- Technological objectives have symbolic as well as military value. A server vault,
  AI installation, nanoforge component, or shield plant may be attacked to send a
  message even when holding it is impossible.
- Cells vary. Some are strategic and patient; others are millenarian, coercive, or
  reckless. Decentralization should create scenario variety.

## Marine Operations direction

### Units and armor

Use concealed sympathizers, light infiltrators, armed workers, assault cells,
demolition specialists, veteran “chosen” cadres, technical saboteurs, and rare
industrial heavy rigs. Armor may combine civilian load frames, pressure suits,
salvaged plates, repurposed mining protection, and stolen military components.

Ordinary Pathers are not secretly immune to fear, pain, or blast. If combat stims,
martyr rigs, or coercive conditioning affect play, issue an explicit item or unit
capability with visible cost and counterplay.

### Weapons and equipment

Prefer common slugthrowers, machine weapons, shredder/flechette carbines, simple
rockets, flamers where appropriate, industrial cutters, stolen service arms, illicit
stims, carried IEDs, and cell-built demolition. Expensive energy or gauss weapons fit
captured specialists and caches, not a standardized baseline.

Martyr rigs are rare explicit mission pieces or specialist loadouts. They should be
dangerous to allies, detectable through honest information, and consequential when
lost—not a visual synonym for every Pather marine.

### Tactics and command

Favor infiltration, ambush, rapid closing, demolition, attacks on technological
nodes, local massing, and low ordinary disengage preference. A competent cell still
uses cover, crossfire, escape routes, decoys, and timing. Make ideological aggression
shape objectives and risk tolerance; do not replace tactical AI with a straight line
toward the nearest target.

### Facilities and objectives

Good sites include hidden cells beneath ordinary districts, converted workshops,
fuel or mining caches, clandestine shrines, tunnel routes, safe houses, captured
utility plants, propaganda transmitters, and demolition-prepared industrial sites.
Their defenses should exploit the host environment rather than resemble a standardized
Pather fortress everywhere.

High-value targets include AI cores, research data, fabrication controllers, shield
plants, power relays, orbital fire-control, executive shelters, and symbols of elite
technological privilege. Destruction is more credible for the Path than for most
patrons, but mission variety still includes theft, hostage-taking, exposure, rescue,
conversion, defense of a cell, or securing a device for later judgment.

### Contracts and voice

Credible offers include sabotage, destruction of a named technological target,
recovery of a prophet or cell, seizure of an artifact, punishment of a collaborator,
smuggling arms, or a deniable strike against a common enemy. Briefings speak in
testimony, sin, corruption, sacrifice, signs, and personal trust rather than one
central bureaucracy. Patrons may conceal the Pather motive until the player's contact
or intelligence exposes it.

## Anti-caricature rules

- Do not make every Pather a suicide attacker.
- Do not make the Path technologically innocent; it uses the tools required to wage
  war against technology and lives with that contradiction.
- Do not make decentralization mean randomness. A local cell can prepare deeply and
  act coherently.
- Do not erase civilians, coercion, or sympathetic networks by spawning attackers
  from a cultureless “terrorist base.”
