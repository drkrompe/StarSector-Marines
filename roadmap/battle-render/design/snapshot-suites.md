# Snapshot suites

Status: ACTIVE

Written: 2026-10-16

Updated: 2026-10-25 — look recordings are MP4 by default, with an opt-in soundtrack

A guide to the notable `createSnapshots` suites: what each one shows and where it writes.
How to run them is below (Running the catalog); the preview rule is in `CLAUDE.md`. This table is not the catalog. Suites register through `META-INF/services`,
and `tools/authoring.sh snapshot_list_suites` lists every registered id; many registered
suites have no row here. Add a row when a suite is evidence someone will want to find
by name.

**Look frames are what the owner is shown.** A behaviour scene's loops are verdict evidence: a bare arena with
no ground art, faction dots, labels and a caption band, and by default no smoke, contrails or shadows. A scene suite
that overrides `lookShots()` also writes `look/<shot>-<view>.mp4` and `look/<shot>-<view>-NN.png`: its subject stood
on real generated ground (`LookGround`, a production map of a small colony on a chosen world, with its doodads,
buildings and nature cover handed to the simulation) and drawn through the look preset
(`BattleReviewFrameRenderer.look`, the presentation mode `asPresentation()` plus: shots, launch, trail, contrail, smoke, impact and aftermath effects, unit
shadows and roofs on, no markers, annotations or caption), at play zoom (60 cells across, 32 px a cell) and close,
1920x1080. The stills are each view's busiest moments. `-Dsnapshot.lookOnly=true` skips the verdict loops and
writes only the look frames. Any other suite calls `LookReview.record`.

**Look recordings are MP4.** The frames stream into `ffmpeg` as they render (raw BGR in, H.264 `yuv420p` with
`+faststart` out, written bit-exact so the same frames give the same file; `FfmpegVideo`), and only the frames that
can still become stills are held. A 30 s shot at 30 fps is tens of megabytes where its full-colour GIF was near a
gigabyte. `ffmpeg` comes from `PATH` or `$FFMPEG`, as for `processAudio`; a machine without one writes GIFs and says
so once. `-Dsnapshot.video=gif` asks for the GIF. `-Dsnapshot.lookSound=true` gives each MP4 a soundtrack: every
sound the battle screen would start, heard from the first view's centre, is logged (`BattleSoundLog`,
`build/look-sound/<shot>.jsonl`, `-Dsnapshot.lookSound.logDir` moves it) and mixed (`BattleSoundMix`): each take
decoded once, at its tick, volume and pitch (pitch resampled, so speed moves with it, as the game plays it), panned
across the view, the takes of an id played in turn, the whole scaled down if it would clip. The mix is
deterministic; the engine's distance roll-off is not modelled. The flag makes `createSnapshots` run
`:asset-pipeline:processAudio` first, since the mix reads the generated Oggs in `mod/sounds`. Scenes with look shots: `fire-support-apc`
(`apc-shelling`), `mech-duel-range` (`mech-duel`), `field-mortar` (`mortar-section`), `tank-undergrowth` (`young-wood`, `dead-wood`); the LRM carrier's look is its
own suite, `lrm-carrier-look`.

A suite can be **opt-in**: `all` leaves it out and only `-Psnapshot=<id>` runs it
(`SnapshotSuite.inAll`). That is for slow look studies that matter only while their
art is being changed; `deck-props` and `elevation-look` are opt-in.

## Running the catalog

`gradlew.bat createSnapshots` renders every deterministic visual-evidence suite under `build/snapshots/` without
launching Starsector. It is the only top-level task for visual evidence: a new kind of picture is a `SnapshotSuite`
provider registered through `META-INF/services`, never a new Gradle task, so automation and the `layerAuthoring`
workbench's Snapshots tab share one catalog.

- `-Psnapshot=<id>[,<id>...]` selects suites (default `all`, which leaves out opt-in suites); quote the whole
  property in PowerShell (`'-Psnapshot=layers,turrets'`). `-PsnapshotDir=<path>` moves the output root. A run
  replaces matching files without prompting and never removes stale ones, so a renamed artifact leaves the old
  name behind.
- A `-Dbattle.*` switch on the command line is forwarded into the fork, so a control run is one command:
  `.\gradlew.bat createSnapshots '-Psnapshot=killing-ground' '-Dbattle.pathfinding.casualtyRouteCost=false'`.
- Art is read from `mod/` first and the installed game second, the game's own order, so vanilla sprites such as
  aircraft hulls appear; a suite stops drawing them if the install is missing.
- **Review frames are drawn on the GPU by default**, through the shipping `BattleRenderer` into a `HeadlessGl`
  pbuffer (`ReviewSceneBackend`), with surface relief, sun shadows and event lights (`gpu-review-drain.md`).
  `-Dsnapshot.backend=java2d` is the reference drain; `-Dsnapshot.gl.relief=false` drops the relief composite. On
  the Mac the task runs on LWJGL 3 through a windowless CGL context (`-PsnapshotGl=lwjgl3` does the same on
  Windows); a machine with no context says so once and uses Java2D. GL pixels depend on the driver, so compare raw
  frames only between runs on one backend and machine.
- The pixel work runs on workers (`SnapshotWorkers`, half the processors; `-Dsnapshot.threads=1` is serial): frames
  drain in fixed 32-row bands, so every frame is the same at any worker count (`-Dsnapshot.bandRows=0` drains each
  frame whole). The simulation steps on one thread. `-Dsnapshot.profile=true` prints where a frame's time goes.
- **Accept a renderer change on raw frames, never on GIFs or MP4s**, whose palette or codec hides and invents
  differences: `-PsnapshotRawFrames=<dir>` keeps every frame losslessly (a look recording's too) and
  `RawFrameDump.main` compares two runs.
- **`-Dsnapshot.layoutLint=<dir>` lints every retained document the selected suites paint** (`LayoutLint`): each is
  laid out again at 1744x938, 1920x1080 and 2560x1440 through the host's relative viewport (a panel painted at its
  own size only at that size), checked for clipped text, boxes spilling a clipping ancestor and zero-size hides that
  still take a `gap` slot, and restored. It writes `layout-lint.md` (spots and patterns per screen, worst rows),
  `layout-lint.csv` and `marked/<screen>.png` (each screen's worst 1920x1080 frame, faults outlined) into `<dir>`.
  Pages drawn under a `HeadlessStrings` translation overlay count as separate `[translated]` screens. Pass absolute
  paths, and leave out suites that play battles: they cost minutes and add no screens. It lints the page the suite
  built, so a suite that leaves out a sheet the game installs lints a different page.
- A suite artifact is a PNG, an animated GIF for a suite whose evidence is a played battle, or an MP4 the suite
  has encoded to a scratch file under `build/tmp` (`SnapshotArtifact.video`). The runner writes or moves each into
  place; a suite never writes into the output tree itself. Animated review frames come from `BattleReviewFrameRenderer`, shared
  with commander evidence. Frames are a pure function of simulation state: the effect feed's jitter is seeded and
  the animation clock pinned (`REVIEW_CLOCK_SECONDS`).
- Snapshot code never enters the shipped jar: catalog and runner code lives in `:layer-authoring`, mod-specific
  providers in the root test source set, and `verifyModJarBoundary` (part of `check`) fails if any tool-only class
  reaches `StarsectorMarines.jar`.

## What a Java2D review frame draws, against the live screen

`BattleReviewFrameRenderer` collects the production `BattleRenderer` layers. On the Java2D drain
(`HeadlessBattleSceneRenderer`), what is not in the draw list is missing: GL custom passes, and services the
screen ticks every frame, which a feed (`ReviewImpactFx.feed`, once per simulation tick) stands in for.

| Live layer / feature | Review frame |
|----------------------|--------------|
| Ground, decals, doodads, parked vehicles | Drawn. Terrain relief, sun shading and normal-mapped lighting are the GL composite and are not drawn; `sun-shadows` holds the CPU model |
| Units, bodies, health bars, medic and satchel markers | Drawn |
| Unit shadows, roofs and fort building art | Drawn when asked for (`withUnitShadows`, `withRoofs`); off by default |
| Aircraft hulls, turrets and shadows, warships, convoys, drones, engine plumes | Drawn |
| Projectiles, tracers, bolts, beams, delivered-ordnance traces | Drawn (`SHOTS`) |
| Contrails, including warship missiles' ribbons | Drawn as flat-coloured ribbon segments (`RibbonBatch`); additive plumes draw as ordinary blends |
| Weapon `fx` blocks, blasts, wall dust, wreck smoke and fire, point-defence bursts | Drawn from the real `ImpactFx` fed per tick |
| Smoke clouds, shield domes, point-defence marks, demolition packs, tender sites | Drawn (shield domes by the CPU model of their shader) |
| Night lighting pools | Drawn; muzzle, impact and fire lights (`GroundLightService`) are not fed |
| Capture-point rings, objective annuli | Drawn |
| Objective, compound and fort text labels | Not drawn; reviews name places with `ReviewAnnotations` |
| Fog of war | Not drawn: a review is a neutral observer (`BattleRenderHostProfile.OBSERVER_SCENE`) |
| Highlights, selection rings, order lines, debug overlays | Not drawn: UI and debug |
| Ship-deck lighting and deck scenes | Not drawn here; the ship-deck suites have their own renderer |

Scenes built over a bare grid get no doodads unless they hand the generator's `ctx.doodads` to the simulation; the
fort scenes do not (`render-parity` does).

## Warship fights: the duel camera

Hulls fight at hull-fight range, a good part of a map apart, so a whole-map frame shows two specks. The warship
suites above (`warship-mirrors`, `warship-tactics`, `frigate-duel`, `greyhook-duel`, and the duel GIF of
`black-sluice`) record through `WarshipFollowRecording` instead, a camera over the same `BattleReviewFrameRenderer`
close view:

- **Framing.** The bounding box of the hulls in the fight, padded so the space between them and the rounds crossing
  it stay in shot. With more than two hulls up it frames one engagement: the closest hostile pair and every hull near
  them, kept until another pair is clearly closer. A destroyed hull stays framed for five seconds where its wreck
  falls. The view is held between a closest width (five hull lengths, at least 70 cells) and the whole map, and stays
  inside the map.
- **Motion.** Pan and zoom follow a critically damped spring toward a target that moves only past a dead zone (pan)
  or a hysteresis band (zoom out at 6%, in at 15%), so a hull yawing in place does not shake the frame; after the
  spring, the view widens or shifts just enough to hold every framed hull, so a hull is never lost. Centres are
  snapped to whole pixels at the drawn zoom (whole pixels per cell). It is a pure function of the simulation and the
  recording's own history (`DuelCamera`).
- **Cadence.** A frame every 6 ticks (five a simulated second, played at 100 ms: twice real time) while two hostile
  hulls are up and rounds are flying or have flown in the last five seconds; the scene's own cadence on the approach
  and between passes; a five-second tail after the last hostile pair breaks, then nothing. A long fight halves its
  rate after every 240 frames, down to the scene's own cadence, but never while a kill is framed.
- **Insets.** A locked-off close-up, about twice the main view's scale, of the hull whose structure just dropped (two
  seconds) or that was just destroyed (five seconds), labelled `HIT` or `KILL`, in a corner no framed hull is in; none when
  the main view already draws that hull nearly as large. A locator map in the lower right shows every hull and the
  view's rectangle.

Switches (forwarded `snapshot.*` properties): `-Dsnapshot.duelCamera=false` restores the whole-map frames at the
scene's own cadence, which is the way to get the whole-map review of any of these suites; `-Dsnapshot.duelCamera.everyTicks=N`
sets the dense cadence (3 is real time); `-Dsnapshot.duelCamera.inset=false` drops the close-up;
`-Dsnapshot.duelCamera.trace=true` prints each frame's view.

What it costs: a frame is no slower to draw than a whole-map one, but there are more of them, and a moving close
view defeats the GIF's frame-to-frame delta, so files are far larger. Measured on 2026-10-18 (hull wear at a
durability of three, so duels run long), whole map against duel camera:

| Loop | Whole map (scene cadence) | Duel camera |
|------|---------------------------|-------------|
| `wolf-11` (decided at about 2 minutes) | 300 frames, 0.4 MB, 30 s | 356 frames, 27 MB, about 35 s |
| `eagle-11` (decided at about 15 minutes) | 900 frames, 2.7 MB, 205 s | 1028 frames, 144 MB, about 85 s |
| `greyhook-duel` seed 15305 (fought to the finish, decided about 4.4 minutes after the order) | not measured | 45 MB at `everyTicks=12`; 272 frames, 29 MB at `everyTicks=30`, about 20 s |

The duel camera is quicker overall because it stops recording once the fight is decided. A long duel is the
expensive case: `-Dsnapshot.duelCamera.everyTicks=12` halves it, and `-Dsnapshot.duelCamera=false` gives the old
whole-map recording.
