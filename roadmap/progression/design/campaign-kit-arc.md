## The story the kit should tell

The authored campaign is a climb in legitimacy. An unaffiliated company protects
freight drivers on Jangala, chases a pirate crew across Corvus, stands beside the
Hegemony at Garnir and learns what real military power looks like, earns a working
ship, and graduates into sponsored paramilitary work on Agreus. Its armoury should
read the same way, in four registers:

| Register | What the company fields | Where it comes from | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Scrounged** | Mongrel kit: surplus rifles, security shells, whatever the last fight left behind | Starter issue, open markets, recovery | Watchman's Kit, Blackforge Raider Lot |
| **Issued** | Coherent local kit a patron hands out for the job | Story rewards, Common and Advanced licences | A Proper Issue, Trench-Clearing Issue |
| **Recovered** | Worn military patterns, real but lesser, rebuilt from wrecks | Introduced by the Agreus story; afterwards ordinary Advanced cards | Recovered Specter, Recovered Arbalest |
| **Approved** | Proper military issue: whole powered sections, current doctrine | Only by a military warrant from the tradition that builds it | XIV Shock, Bulwark, Holdfast, Lion's Mantle |

Where it suits a mission, the player meets a register **across the line** before
holding it: Agreus puts Greyhook's crude powered rigs in the field before the
company wears recovered versions of the same machines. This is a habit, not a rule.
A register may arrive simply as a new licence or a new listing in the market
stalls, with nobody on either side having worn it first, and a mission does not
need to show new kit at all.

## What is wrong today

Measured from the catalog (`loadoutCardSheet`), not from intent:

- **Approved kit is not reserved.** Every full powered sheet is Prestige, and every
  one of them sits in the Independent `recovery` pool, so fifteen high-risk victories
  can hand a mercenary a Lion's Guard Mantle or a Knights' Holdfast section from a
  random wreck. That is the "military patterns first" gap `campaign-learning-curve.md`
  and `a-small-war.md` already record. (The Hegemony Arbalest Section is built on the
  *recovered* Arbalest, a tier-3 Advanced pattern, and is not part of this problem.)
- **The newest toys have no card.** The Arrestor pack and Palisade pod are
  learnable templates, but a squad is equipped by one weapon card and
  one armour sheet, and no card carries any of them. A player can own the pack and
  never field it.
- **Story beats award money where they could award a way of fighting.** Corvus and
  Broken Supply Route pay only credits; their tactical lessons (defended positions,
  convoy escort, counter-battery) have no kit that names them.

## Proposed access model

Keep Common, Advanced and Prestige as they are, and add a fourth tier above them.

**Restricted**: equipment that no market, licence, patron roll or recovery roll
offers on its own authority. It becomes acquirable only through a **military
warrant**, a named, recorded authorisation from one tradition. A warrant is granted
either by a story beat or by close standing with that faction at the Prestige band,
and it names exactly which Restricted loadout cards it opens. A military warrant is not
Dormer's *civil-security procurement warrant* on Agreus, which is a contract term and
grants no military status.

- The six approved tier-4 power suits become Restricted. Five are shock suits: the
  Hegemony XIV `armor.heavy`, Tri-Tachyon Specter, League Bulwark, Knights' Holdfast
  and Lion's Guard Mantle. The sixth is the Hegemony military `armor.arbalest`
  support suit. The tier-3 recovered Specter and Arbalest stay Advanced.
- **Outlaw Foundry-Breaker stays Prestige and recoverable.** Pirates and the Path
  issue no warrants, which is the point: the dirty road to powered armour is open
  earlier, and it is cruder, louder and breaks first. Choosing it over waiting for an
  approved section is a real decision about what kind of company this is.
- **Some kit is nearly never the player's.** Martyr rigs already carry a non-player
  reason. The Lion's Guard Mantle and Knights' Holdfast need that faction's own
  standing warrant, so most companies only ever see them across the line. A warrant
  rule is the acquisition owner the stranded-asset audit needs.

This is the growth rule applied: approved kit consumes Standing with one tradition,
and its counter-pressure is the obligations that come with the warrant (and, for the
Hegemony, the scrutiny of being a licensed military auxiliary).

## The arc map

What each arc should hand the player, see across the line, and hold back.

| Arc | Shows the player (enemy or ally) | Fun in the player's hands | Held back |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Wayline opening** — Relief, Counterattack | Militia holding a checkpoint behind revetments | Watchman's Kit; A Proper Issue's choice (Steady Line or Forward Screen). **Checkpoint Issue**: service rifles, three satchel-charge sappers, smoke, on sale at the broker's market once Relief settles, beside the free choice rather than as a third path. It teaches holding a road by clearing what overlooks it | Everything above Common |
| **Broken Supply Route** — Junction, Thunder Run | Raiders with rocket tubes against the convoy | **Trench-Clearing Issue** as the Junction reward (opening a defended junction is exactly its job); **Convoy Escort Issue**: rockets plus one Palisade pod, on sale at the employer's market once Thunder Run settles | Powered anything |
| **The Corvus Account** — Dust Ledger, Blind Watch, Cold Account | Sable mortars and missiles at Blind Watch; at Garnir, Resolute's guns rather than Hegemony infantry: Kord lands no one | **Counter-Battery Issue** (Hegemony): service rifles, two Arrestor packs, one anti-materiel rifle. On sale at Hegemony markets once Cold Account settles, an old counter-mortar table back on the shelves | XIV Shock, the military Arbalest: the company has now seen what it cannot have |
| **A Working Ship** — Salvage Rights, Cold Start, Working Passage | Builders working under fire | **Builder's Guard**: revetments and Palisade pods from Tamsin Vale's yard stores, on sale at Asharu once Working Passage settles, for holding a construction site | — |
| **A Small War** — Agreus | Greyhook's crude boarding rigs and mortar fire on the commissioning work | Recovered Specter and Recovered Arbalest (as shipped), the Salvage-assisted Section, and the planned Boarding Jack. Counter-Battery Issue pairs naturally with the mixed section | Approved sections. The arc's planned procurement lead becomes the **first warrant** |
| **Beyond Agreus** — the procurement lead | — | The **Hegemony auxiliary warrant** (`progression-nouns.md`, Kit and armory): the military Arbalest first, XIV shock armour after the first honoured obligation period | Other traditions' approved kit, which needs their own warrants |

Two things this keeps true:

- **Every arc adds a way of fighting, not only a better number.** Dig in, open a
  position, escort, counter-battery, hold a site, mix powered and regular troops.
  Each is a card a player can feel in the first battle they field it.
- **Recovered before approved, always.** No ordinary channel can deliver a military
  powered section before the story's recovered ones, so the Agreus beat lands.

## What later stays to unlock

Even after Agreus, most of the catalog stays ahead of the company:

- Approved sections from every tradition except the one that warranted it.
- The Tri-Tachyon point-defence laser (planned: shoulder-wrap emitter), as
  Tri-Tachyon Restricted kit. It is the energy counterpart to the Arrestor, and the
  natural reward for a Tri-Tachyon warrant.
- Masterwork grades and faction Prestige line sheets, through MRB 20 and patrons as
  today.
- Whatever the Foundry-Breaker road leads to: pirate and Path powered kit is the
  alternative to warrants, not a stage before them.

## Why the arc cards are shaped as they are

Each was settled on the `loadoutMatchup` ladder (field fatigues, three seeds) as an
uncarded kit before it was authored, against two controls: Dockside Issue and
Trench-Clearing Issue. Trench-Clearing is the fair comparison, because it also
issues service rifles, so the difference from it is the specials alone.

- **Counter-Battery Issue** earns its place where it was meant to: holding a trench
  under a field-mortar attack it won 3/3 with seven left, where Trench-Clearing lost
  2/3.
- **Builder's Guard** held the same trench 2/3 with nine left, and held open ground
  against a Trench-Clearing assault 2/3, which Trench-Clearing's mirror never did.
- **Convoy Escort Issue** took the garrisoned building 3/3 from Trench-Clearing
  defenders, against 2/3 for the mirror.
- **Checkpoint Issue** has the thinnest edge: against Trench-Clearing attackers in
  open ground it held where the mirror stalled. That is acceptable for a Common card
  bought after the first contract, and the place to revisit if revetments change.

Rows move with behaviour tuning; rerun them before reshaping a card rather than
trusting these numbers.
