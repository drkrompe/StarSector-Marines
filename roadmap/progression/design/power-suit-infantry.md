# Power-suit infantry

Status: ACTIVE — equipment-category direction; field balance and distinct art require acceptance.
Written: 2026-10-13
Updated: 2026-10-13 — per-marine mixed issue and planned Vesper Reach recovered-suit progression.

## Purpose

The company should grow from fragile regular squads into a smaller number of
capable formations whose named people remain worth recognizing. Power-suit
infantry occupies the space between ordinary marines and mech chassis: a person
inside a wearable mechanized shell, carrying infantry weapons and fighting
through infantry cover, with substantially greater armor endurance and the
bounded capabilities of that particular suit.

An initial design target is for one upgraded squad to tackle the work of about
two regular squads in concentrated fights. This is a target for field evidence,
not a tested equivalence or a force-budget multiplier. Eight to twelve active
infantry squads is a proposed late-campaign management guideline, not a cap or
settled requirement. Better formations should reduce the need for repetitive
squad administration while preserving a reason to bring regular specialists.

## Class, role, quality, and person

The pattern explicitly declares STANDARD or POWER_SUIT armor class. STANDARD
includes ordinary powered assistance and advanced regular armor. Power suits
are not inferred from tier, armor role, a screen, color, or faction. A Kestrel
riot shell is STANDARD ASSAULT equipment; an Arbalest is POWER_SUIT SUPPORT.
Redoubt line plate, Outrider recon equipment, and Corpsman equipment remain
STANDARD. Regular recon and support do not vanish when the company improves.

Class describes the equipment carrier. Role describes the job, tier describes
issued experience, grade describes manufacture, and provenance describes source.
The person remains the same named marine. Refitting preserves identity, career,
status, billet, squad, officer relationships, twelve-person capacity, and the
three derived four-person teams. Each wearer still occupies one ordinary lift
seat. A POWER_SUIT adds neither a roster nor a battle unit type.

Equipment is individual: squadmates may wear different armor patterns and carry
different weapons. A mixed squad can combine regular and powered wearers in
different roles. A sheet's class requirement constrains the billets it describes;
it does not require every member to use the same pattern or equipment set.
The eight-regular/four-suit section below is one authored composition, not the
definition or only possible ratio of a mixed squad.

Deployment freezes the resolved class with the marine's issued equipment and
plain combat values. Battle fixtures and hover presentation preserve that same
fact rather than rediscovering it from a suit name, tier, or appearance. The
class is descriptive authority for issue and legibility; it never applies a
second hidden durability or damage multiplier.

The six heavy faction tactic sheets request POWER_SUIT equipment. Regular sheets
request STANDARD equipment. Issue searches and role fallback stay inside the
requested class: an unavailable class never silently becomes another formation.
A sheet without any acquired pattern in its class is unavailable and is not
advertised as an attainable refit. Role and tradition preferences still operate
within the class. Explicit class on collected patterns means that upgrading
company stock does not silently mechanize every regular sheet.

An explicitly mixed tactic sheet may declare class per billet. The
Salvage-assisted Section keeps eight STANDARD billets and requires two
POWER_SUIT assault billets plus two POWER_SUIT support billets. Each suit role
must actually be owned; a missing support shell cannot become an assault shell
or ordinary equipment. Better acquired patterns improve those same jobs when
the player refits. Existing regular and full-suit sheets retain their uniform
class requirement.

Missing class on older external pattern data means STANDARD. Existing named kit
keeps its concrete pattern identity; classification is a property of the catalog
pattern rather than a second saved identity. Live save/reload acceptance must
confirm those relationships rather than assume them from a unit test.

## Protection and counterplay

The planned first introduction belongs after the Shepherd has completed its
recovery and joined the player's fleet. `a-small-war.md` sends the company to
Brackwater in Vesper Reach, where Baronet Rusk's bounded procurement warrant and
the company's construction capability make recovered suit technology obtainable.
`vesper-reach.md` owns that setting and cast. Completing A Cold Account, securing
the grounded hull, or finishing Cold Start repairs is not this milestone.
Working Passage and the Vesper arc remain separate unfinished story work; no
suit reward is attached to those earlier victories.

Recovered Specter and Arbalest patterns form the initial tier-III step, with
lower protection and weaker or shorter-lived systems than the tier-IV military
patterns. A mixed section is an available transition before a full suit section;
it is a composition choice, not a finite inventory of four shells. Collected
technology still permits ordinary free fitting. The planned arc rewards recovered
Specter, recovered Arbalest and then a proposed pirate Boarding Jack sidegrade;
its story owns that sequence and calibration targets. The two existing recovered
templates declare that future story owner and remain outside random faction acquisition.
Explicit development controls can teach them for campaign-loop testing without
completing a story or automatically changing anyone's issued kit.

Existing military suit sources can still precede that story through patron and
victory thresholds. A strict story-first progression needs the later obtainable
military procurement route and shared acquisition gate specified in
`a-small-war.md`, preserving equipment already acquired in existing saves.

Suit armor supplies the endurance step. Infantry biological structure stays
unchanged, and armor break exposes the same wearer rather than ejecting or
spawning another actor. Capacity and rating remain separate. Finite directional
screens, movement assists, and payload systems continue through their existing
authorities; their activation does not restore armor or add permanent capacity.

The first calibration uses the following armor capacities. These are starting
numbers, not enduring balance laws; ratings, movement, incoming accuracy, systems,
and issued tiers retain their existing tradeoffs.

| Pattern | Initial capacity | Distinct promise |
| --- | --- | --- |
| XIV heavy battlesuit | 60 | Reliable armored breach and shock infantry |
| Specter composite battlesuit | 55 | Mobile specialist shell with less plate endurance |
| Bulwark coalition battlesuit | 70 | Balanced modular protection and interlock advance |
| Reliquary consecrated battlesuit | 60 | Resistant legacy shell with a severe movement cost |
| Lion's Mantle guard battlesuit | 60 | Conspicuous prestige shell and a long bounded advance |
| Foundry-breaker industrial rig | 85 | Huge crude reserve with poor material resistance |
| Arbalest support battlesuit | 50 | Mechanized support carrying finite shoulder ordnance |
| Recovered Specter frame | 36 | Early recovered assault shell with a slower, weaker breach system |
| Recovered Arbalest frame | 34 | Early support shell with two salvos and slower movement |

Concentrated high-penetration fire must remain an answer. A suit is slower or
more conspicuous according to its authored pattern, and it still needs cover,
weapons discipline, and supporting movement. Greater armor endurance must not
be confused with permanent immunity, hidden structure, free special equipment,
or a guarantee of superior performance on every map.

Acquired templates and ordinary free fitting remain the progression route.
No new upkeep, repair bill, pilot recruitment, fabrication inventory, or casualty
economy is introduced by this category. Mechs retain chassis, hardpoints, heat,
component inventory, lances, Mech Lab, and Mech Support authority.

## Visual direction

The first layered asset is the Specter. Its restrained composite silhouette is
the selected direction; the exploratory Foundry-breaker is too cartoonish and
does not establish an accepted production silhouette. Every suit must read as
a person encased in a powered shell at ordinary battle scale:
a helmet seated between broad shoulders, a continuous torso with a compact
backpack power unit, visible elbow and knee articulation, and two walking legs.
Keep enough human proportion that it cannot be mistaken for a Hound chassis.
The retained exploratory sheet is `art-source/marine-modular-topdown/power-suit-concept.png`;
the adjacent `.prompt.txt` records the built-in ImageGen prompt and source dimensions.

The XIV shock battlesuit retains utilitarian Domain-derived service hardware,
as established in `hegemony-lore-guide.md`. Its top-down silhouette should be
broader and heavier than Legionary line armor,
with a clear armored collar, square shoulder caps, slab forearm protection and
reinforced lower legs. Use the XIV's muted oxide red and charcoal, or military
gray/olive service variants, warm worn plate edges,
restrained service markings, replaceable panels, exposed service sockets, and
protected actuator runs. Avoid a tank hull, oversized cockpit, decorative wings,
or weapons that imply unimplemented hardpoints. The ordinary handheld primary
must remain legible against the torso. Shoulder equipment belongs to the issued
integral system and is present only where the composition contract calls for it.

The Specter uses narrower articulated limbs, smooth composite
shoulder shells, a compact dark power pack, sealed seams, and small sensor
clusters. It keeps the readable powered frame but gives up slab volume for
mobility. Blue or pale technical accents identify corporate manufacture without
using a glowing whole-body effect to imply permanent shielding. Casing colors
may vary independently of dark joints, hardware, and sensor accents. Its predictive
breach treatment remains a temporary capability with an ordinary expiry.

The top-down production contract keeps the torso, left and right shoulder
shells, helmet, feet, and weapon separate. Shoulder shells articulate around
their inner hinges, alternating with the walking stride and shifting into
firing or braced stances. Flexible joint backing maintains a continuous body
beneath them. The helmet looks independently inside the collar; the two feet move
independently beneath the body. Handheld weapons remain swappable infantry
equipment. Paint variants share identical silhouettes and registration so color
never changes articulation or physical geometry. Slate, olive, oxide, and sand
provide the first casing palette; campaign cosmetic selection remains a separate
design decision. Source plates, material masks, geometry, and export recipes
belong together under `art-source/marine-modular-topdown/specter-v2/`, retaining
the v1 source art and material masks.

Arbalest's first playable layered body uses the accepted shell in its olive
palette, with independent shoulders, helmet and feet and an authored left-pod
mount. Its appearance family is distinct from ordinary Legionary equipment.
The shared shell registration keeps weapons and joints aligned; the pod supplies
its support silhouette. This is a shared first-pass body, not final unique
Hegemony support-suit artwork. Recovered and military patterns share their
family's silhouette; their issued tier, protection and capability remain visible
in equipment inspection.

The Foundry-breaker needs a more grounded revision: mismatched welded ship plate over
a visible cargo exoframe, irregular shoulder mass, load-bearing struts, guarded
hoses, crude welding seams, and a salvaged power pack. Rust, soot, industrial
yellow remnants, and dull steel identify origin. Irregularity must not make the
weapon, facing, or two-leg walking motion unreadable. Its greater crude capacity
is visually volume, not superior material quality. Avoid incidental extra guns
or machinery that promise mechanics the pattern does not own.

Produce reference comparisons beside an ordinary Legionary marine and a Hound
at one shared camera scale, including front, rear, and top-down views. The three
suit concepts should remain distinguishable in silhouette before their colors
are inspected. Later shipped assets use the existing layered infantry
composition, with matching head, torso, arms, legs, weapon clearance, animation
anchors, shadows, picking, and any changed physical geometry. Cosmetic scale
must not quietly enlarge collision or alter targeting.

Existing appearance families are acceptable for the first code slice and do
not establish final visual acceptance. A concept sheet communicates direction;
it is not a game-ready sprite sheet, authored layered asset, or battle evidence.
Verify final readability in ordinary crowded combat at 1920x1080 and 100% UI
scale, rather than accepting only an enlarged isolated doll.

## Acceptance boundary

`power-suit-field-acceptance.md` owns the remaining verification. Required
questions are whether endurance actually reduces deployed formations, whether
regular specialist sheets remain useful, whether concentrated anti-armor fire
still defeats the shells, whether named refit and save/reload preserve identity,
and whether game-scale art makes the new class legible. Synthetic TTK or catalog
checks can establish their narrow mechanisms; they cannot answer those field
and live campaign questions.

`progression-nouns.md` owns quality and issue, `personnel-nouns.md` owns people,
`combat-durability-nouns.md` owns damage, `role-and-access.md` owns tactic-sheet
composition, and `equipment-lore-catalog.md` owns credible circulation. This
direction amends their former blanket preference for capability growth without
greater base protection; it retains the rule that integral systems never add
permanent durability.
