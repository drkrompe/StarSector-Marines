# Latchkey District on Agreus

Status: DRAFT — original local setting direction on an existing vanilla world for the post-Shepherd paramilitary arc.

Written: 2026-10-13

Updated: 2026-10-19 — the family's ships are an Enforcer and a Wolf.

## Scope and provenance

**Agreus in Arcadia** is existing Starsector 0.98a content. The installed vanilla
`Arcadia.java` identifies entity `agreus` as a barren moon of the ice giant
Syrinx, with **Syrinx Passage**, **Syrinx Relay** and **Citadel Arcadia** nearby.
`data/campaign/econ/arcadia.json` gives Agreus an Independent size-five market,
cold and no-atmosphere conditions, extensive ruins, a megaport, tech-mining and
refining. `descriptions.csv` entries `planet_agreus` and `station_arcadia`
establish Ko Combine planetary administration, major shipbreaking and reconstruction,
crater-arcologies and transport tubes, and a Hegemony logistics center capable
of projecting fleet power throughout the system. These installed files are
read-only primary references, not mod-authored claims.

House Dormer, Greyhook, every named local person below and the **Latchkey District**
with all its named surface sites are **MOD-AUTHORED** proposals. The concession,
lease arbitration and local security arrangements are invented beneath Ko Combine
administration; Dormer is neither Agreus's sovereign nor a substitute for that
administration. Independent decentralization, pirate predation and fragmented
procurement follow `faction-lore-nouns.md`, `independents-lore-guide.md` and
`pirates-lore-guide.md`. This document proposes no new system, planet, moon,
station, belt or relay.

This document owns the enduring place and cast. `a-small-war.md` owns the finite
chapter sequence, player commitments, mission acceptance and reward schedule.
`early-operation-nouns.md` remains the authored-operation authority;
`campaign-nouns.md`, `living-world-nouns.md` and `narrative-nouns.md` retain their
political and truthful-presentation boundaries.

The company arrives after Morrow actually joins the fleet. A recovery claim,
grounded construction capability or completed repair job cannot substitute for
that departure. Agreus is the first sustained commitment beyond the familiar
Corvus circuit: the company's new working ship makes supporting this client
credible, while neither ship nor sponsor makes it a military power.

## A place worth keeping

Agreus's industrial environment supplies the setting: stripped hulls on cold lava
plains, quarantined reactor pits, inhabited crater-arcologies and enclosed transit
routes to processing yards. The Latchkey District is one invented concession
within that larger economy. Its repair work matters to the people dependent on
it without defining the whole moon's industry or government.

**Silt Basin** is an enclosed industrial coolant and reclamation complex, not an
open-water estuary. Settling tanks, recovered-fluid treatment and heat exchange
serve adjacent hull-breaking and repair facilities. Imported food, medicine and
life-support stock remain essential. Workers know which pumps keep reclamation
usable and which reclaimed part can run one more season. A quiet shift is marked
by cranes, floodlights and condensate inside sealed service halls.

**Low Quay** is the district's pressure-habitat port quarter: bonded stores,
freight offices, a clinic, boarding rooms and a lease-arbitration office. House
Dormer maintains administrative rooms above a working customs shed. The baronet
has an office overlooking overdue cargo rather than a palace overlooking subjects.

Low Quay's **bonded stores**, at the quarter's industrial edge, are one walled
compound. A secure freight gate in its western wall is the only way between the
company's apron and the single loading lane that runs east to the load court.
North of the lane stand the gatehouse over the gate, the records archive and the
bonded warehouse with its rack aisles; south of it lie the hold where Drent
Haulage stages diverted cargo and the calibration shop with its cage. Occupied
rooms are reached through enclosed service galleries whose pressure vestibules
open on the lane at one place, **the crossing**: the supplier's technician walks
in across it, the calibration carrier drives out over it, and Greyhook's finite
rearguard comes to it from the north freight gate down the north gallery. In
Limited Warrant the rearguard's objective is that crossing rather than the gate
mouth; while the technician is walking in, a second company squad holds it, and
the technician waits whenever the company knows of enemies there. The protected
calibration work counts the whole shop with its cage. Vestibules and galleries
sell the pressure hull; nothing counts oxygen.

The **diverted clinic consignments** are identifiable cargo — insulated totes and
medical stock matching Nadi Orr's list, in the hold, on the dock apron and beside
Drent's truck in the transfer yard. They are evidence of the seizures, not an
objective: Limited Warrant neither moves, returns nor pays for them, and any
return to their named recipients needs its own implemented outcome.

**Latchkey Yard** stands along the elevated freight route beyond the habitat
quarter. It repairs small commercial hulls and manufactures replacement assemblies
from recovered stock. A workers' association operates its leased machinery and
shared service spaces. Dormer holds a secured district claim under the Combine's
administration; the crews have the knowledge that makes possession valuable.

### The yard on the ground

Latchkey is one walled working yard, not a scatter of workshops on open concrete.
The boat apron lies west of the wall. A **freight gate** opens from it onto a
freight road that runs past the **dispatch house** (gate office, dispatch floor,
records room) and round to the tooling hall's north doors. The marked **handover
bay** at the dispatch house's yard door is where a custody party stops and signs.
The yard's courts stay open: assembly ground inside the gate, lay-down stock and an
inbound truck at the edges, lanes wide enough for boats' passengers, constables and
an employer-lifted mech to move as formations.

The **tooling hall** is the yard's middle: machine bays either side of a working
nave, a tool crib, and a walled **calibration room** at its east end where the
marked calibration machine stands on a painted plinth. The hall has exactly **two
ways in**: its north **freight doors** off the yard, and the south door of the
**service gallery**, a covered sealed corridor from the **operator entrance**.
The operator entrance stands in the south wall on the service road: an outer and
an inner pressure door with the suit room and pressurisation plant beside the lock,
then the shift lobby. Pressure doors, the sealed gallery and the heat main sell the
airless moon; nobody counts oxygen.

East of the wall, the **Cinder Steps** terraces drop away in broken lines of
furnace scrap and cold furnace housings. Their **service lane** crosses them to
the yard's **Cinder Steps gate** and sends a spur north to the homes. The homes and
the **district heating service** stand on their own street north of the wall; the
heat main runs between them and the yard. They are lived-in frontage with no
capture duty and are never a battlefield objective.

The benches show whose work stops if the hall is lost. Lenna's machine labels name
the association lathe that turns North Weir's pump impellers, the heating-service
bench rebuilding the Steps' circulators, and the shared inspection bench where Low
Quay's clinic sterilizer parts are checked. They are attributable labels on ordinary
props with ordinary damage; the calibration machine is the only one the inspection
requires.

**The choice the ground makes.** The company takes dispatch and the gate in the
open, the hall through its freight doors or by the operator entrance and gallery,
and the entrance from outside or from the hall behind it. When dispatch first
falls, Voss commits her finite counterattack in two groups: one down the Cinder
Steps lane for the hall's freight doors, the other along the south road for the
operator entrance and the gallery behind it. Because the hall has only those two
doors, a company that has gathered at one end to escort the inspection finds the
other door open; holding both approaches and getting a living technician to the
calibration room are competing uses of the same squads.

**Returning in Terms of Protection.** The fourth chapter revisits this map rather
than a new one: the same ground at the same seed, with what the inspection left
standing. The operator entrance is now a constables' sandbagged post on the service
road; the calibration room holds the captured Greyhook rig tooling on its benches,
where commissioning is done; the nave keeps the inspection's stock. The handover bay
at dispatch is the signing point and the employer's records carrier comes in by the
west road to it. The Cinder Steps lane and gate are the approach the estate guards
hold under House Custody, and the way the second prize-crew party comes. On the
terraces beyond the wall a dug pit faces the yard, with its back to the lane: it is a
place a mortar can be set up and found from, not a marker that one is there. Nothing
in the third chapter depends on that visit.

**The Cinder Steps** are old furnace terraces between yard and reclamation plant.
Workers occupy sealed galleries and pressure habitats beside processing sheds,
active district heating and buried service routes. Households remain woven through
industry; an enemy compound can sit beside somebody's livelihood.

**North Weir** controls coolant diversion and power distribution to the basin.
It is a closed-circuit reclamation plant with two circuits: intake pump groups
on one side and settling tanks with heat exchangers on the other, joined by a
covered conduit that service crossings pass over. Each circuit answers to its own
sealed switching room, so reopening one room restores one visible part of the
works rather than an abstract half of a timer. Repair loads come in from the
western service apron and dock in a screened bay at the conduit's head, between
the isolation switch house and a crew shelter; both are entered through pressure
vestibules, which is how the airless setting reads without any survival
bookkeeping. The maintenance road enters past a checkpoint, with a service track
along the outfall line as a second approach for anyone holding the road.
Losing the weir interrupts work, heat and essential services. Its importance
comes from the people and machinery it serves, not an unexplained strategic label.

**Black Sluice** is the basin complex's outer fuel and dispatch apron. Protected
tanks, a customs causeway and transfer records link district cargo to outbound
tenders. Greyhook exploits this commercial infrastructure to move prizes and
sustain levies; losing it breaks a local operation without destroying its fleet.
The retained dockside names are local industrial usage, not evidence of a sea.

The causeway is the one transfer road across the outer basin. It runs between
walled settling beds of silt, so anyone on it is in the open; sealed service
galleries run through the bed field to the north and south, entered through
pressure vestibules, and are how people cross under cover; the north gallery
comes out behind the fuel-control house, out of the road gun's sight. The customs block
stands at the causeway's landward end beside an open staging bay. The sheltered fuel-control house stands at the seaward
end, with the marked tank farm behind it and a support-gun position on the road
in front of it. Dispatch occupies the pier beyond. Past the pier is the walled
berth yard: warehouses either side of a concourse, fuel mains along it, sealed
galleries along its north and south walls and pressure vestibules through its
landward wall, ending in the **berth gate** through its east wall. A separate
service road runs east to a vehicle pressure lock under the yard wall: the way
Greyhook's crews come and go, and the way out.

Dispatch's berths are not on the fight's ground. South of the band a broken ridge
runs the width of the basin, and the basin's own rim closes the south-west:
lobes and spurs of different heights cut back by gullies, broken outcrops on the
flats below, scree fanning from their toes. Neither has a way through. Below them is the **pad field** where Greyhook's ships sit landed
at their true size (`landed-ships-true-scale.md`): a concrete cradle for the
Venture with a Hound berth either side, kerbs, painted borders, tie-downs, floor
drains, deck hatches and engine-wash scorch, gantry arms out to the hulls, a pad
shed and a pad-control house. The only way down is out of the berth gate and
along the berth road down the east side. The ships' fight after Voss's order goes
where its fighting takes it, in sight of the people on the causeway.

West of the landing apron the outer basin runs on as **open approach ground**, the
ground Dormer's ships cross. North of the ridge it is silt flats under regolith drifts:
the transfer road runs on inland from the causeway's landward end, past the first
settling beds, silted full and abandoned with their walls fallen in stretches, to a
low broken rim off the north edge. South of it the ridge runs on to the map's edge
over rim country, a massif with its lobes, spurs and gullies reaching onto a second
floor under the basin's west rim. Nothing of the ground fight is out there. It is
there because the ships fight at their own reach: without it the family's ships crossing the
edge had Greyhook's pads in range at once.

Open yards are airless and cold. Exterior workers and combatants use appropriate
pressure protection; occupied interiors have life support. Tactical art, equipment
presentation and correspondence must respect those conditions. This setting does
not add oxygen consumption, suit sealing costs or a separate survival mechanic.

## Arcadia beyond the district

**Syrinx Passage** provides an existing approach associated with Agreus.
**Syrinx Relay** is an existing Hegemony communications installation. Public route
and relay access do not grant hidden tactical intelligence; a dispatch record or
transmission needs an actual attributed source before Castle can use it.

**Citadel Arcadia** is an existing Hegemony logistics and military center around
Syrinx. Its patrol power is real. Greyhook survives through concealed transfers,
false documentation, coerced local access and short opportunistic raids among
industrial traffic and salvage; it does not openly control the system or rely on
an unexplained absence of Hegemony fleets. The authored local conflict grants no
Hegemony endorsement, unlimited response force or free naval supremacy. The one
Hegemony hulls that appear, Chapter 5's pair of lent frigates, fight their own
finite engagement and are never the company's to command.

Vanilla `data/campaign/starmap.json` places Corvus at `(400, -9400)` and Arcadia
at `(-2500, -11900)`. At 2,000 hyperspace units per light-year, their anchor
separation is approximately **1.914 LY**. This is a nearby move outside Corvus,
not a remote frontier expedition; actual travel and local maneuvering still cost
fuel, supplies and time. The first referral names Agreus and its actual course,
with commercial preparation advice rather than a promise of safe passage.

Bind the authored contact and district to existing entity `agreus` and resolve
its actual market through the entity. Never guess a market ID, create a duplicate
market or overwrite vanilla world generation. New and existing saves initialize
the authored story identity once. Accepted work freezes its real site; a missing
or inaccessible Agreus pauses the work rather than moving these people elsewhere.

Orbital access does not resolve possession. Yard machines, service galleries,
local records and occupied habitats must remain useful after the firing. Neither
local side has capital-fleet supremacy; wider fleet authorities remain present.
Ground fighting secures entrances, utilities, traffic and working space under
ordinary lift, fleet risk and finite support constraints.

## House Dormer and its concession

**House Dormer** is a minor local commercial house holding a district concession
on Agreus beneath the Ko Combine administration. Its
material power is a cluster of secured loans, port leases, processing interests
and rights under an authored district concession and lease agreement. Those assets
confer bargaining strength and responsibilities. They do not make it owner of every resident or spokesman for
all Independents.

**Baronet Elian Dormer** is the last of a cadet branch of an Eventide house. The
Hegemony's aristocracy still recognises the baronetcy; nobody on Agreus owes it
anything. The branch lost favour two generations ago and put what money it kept
into freight and secured lending. Dormer bought into the Latchkey concession with
the remainder and trades on the name to keep the Combine's lease office answering
his letters. The title is real, its standing on Eventide is thin, and on an
Independent world it is a liability as often as an asset: Lenna and Voss both use
it against him. It confers no Hegemony commission, no authority over Agreus and no
ordinary claim on Hegemony forces, and the living world's political model does not
promote House Dormer because of it.

**Why no authority steps in.** The Ko Combine administers Agreus under its
corporate charter and treats Latchkey as a tenant's lease dispute beneath its
notice: it will let the arbitration award it already made be enforced, and nothing
more. The Hegemony has no jurisdiction on an Independent world. It watches anyway,
because its navy consigns hulks to Agreus for scrapping and Citadel Arcadia sits
beside Syrinx. That gap is the space the company is hired into.

**The family's check.** An Eventide house cannot let a cadet's public failure
stand on a neighbouring world, and the Hegemony welcomes any occasion to show an
Independent colony what it can put overhead at short notice. When Reed's raid in
Chapter 4 shows that Dormer cannot hold his own lease, he writes home. The answer
arrives for Chapter 5 as a pair of Hegemony Enforcer destroyers, lent from Citadel Arcadia
through family patronage rather than ordered out by the navy. Sending two over a
cadet's embarrassment is itself the message. It is a favour with a price: the
family now has an interest in how Latchkey ends, and it notices the company that
fought beneath its ships. `a-small-war.md` owns the engagement.

Dormer can fund a limited series of contracts, release a small depot of restricted
security equipment and mobilize quay constables and estate guards. His guards
know their posts and stores; they cannot police Agreus or the system or sustain an
expeditionary army. Paid shipping services are commercial arrangements, not an
unlimited reserve of employer transports or soldiers.

His immediate object is to restore dependable yard output and stop Greyhook from
turning coercive access into permanent control. He also wants to consolidate his
secured interest in Latchkey. Both aims are public enough to appear in agreements.

Latchkey's arrears are real. Dormer advanced money for replacement machinery after
a major furnace failure; interrupted sailings and pirate levies then prevented
scheduled repayment. A mod-authored lease-arbitration proceeding under Combine
administration has authorized repossession. The workers
dispute the proposed operating terms and accounting, rather than possessing a
secret document that makes every debt vanish.

Repossession would let Dormer appoint management, control service access and require
new leases for association shops. Crews could lose access to shared tools and
accept debt deductions to keep working. Those consequences can be lawful and still
severe. Securing a yard against pirates does not itself settle who should control
it afterward. The story must give the captain enough disclosed terms to understand
that distinction before accepting an enforcement commitment.

## Elian Dormer: manner, strengths and flaws

Dormer inherited a famous name and very little else young, and learned the business by handling
freight failures and insurance arguments. He is comfortable reading a repair
estimate and uncomfortable admitting that the people repairing it have political
claims of their own. His clothes are good, repeatedly mended and poorly suited
to inspection shifts in the chilled service galleries, where he insists on carrying
his own pressure gear.

His voice is measured and precise. He names an obligation, a date and a person
responsible; when frightened, he becomes more formal. He remembers a dead driver's
name but turns too quickly to the surviving delivery schedule. Courtesy remains
real even when the terms behind it are hard.

His observable flaw is treating a signed instrument as evidence that consent
continues. He offers accommodations inside his plan before asking whether his
plan should change. Lenna can catch him calling workers' independent shops
“our capacity,” and he can correct the phrase without correcting the assumption.
He signs with the title only when he needs a door opened, and he is ashamed of
how often that is.

He is capable of paying what he owes, accepting specific operational limits and
putting his own funds at risk. He is also capable of asking mercenaries to enforce
an arrangement from which he benefits. Let both survive scrutiny; kindness need
not acquit his policy, and ownership need not prove secret cruelty.

**Designer-only intention:** Dormer wants the restored yard to secure his house's
survival and improve its standing. He fears that bargaining away control now
will teach every creditor and neighboring interest that his concession is insecure.
These are writing guides, not facts Castle may announce without evidence.

## The referral and the equipment ceiling

Tamsin Vale knows Latchkey through cooperative parts purchases and reclaimed
machinery consignments. Dormer asked Basin Reclamation for a competent small company,
not a celebrity commander or a deniable army. Vale can attest to work she actually
observed in the basin and to Morrow's completed departure. She cannot guarantee
Dormer's judgment or disclose thoughts she has never heard.

Zoey's network supplies a commercial introduction; Vale supplies the practical
reference. Their correspondence makes the opportunity feel earned by the company
without asserting an intact roster, surviving Hound or universal prosperity.
Dormer values a company that can protect technicians and reopen a work site. He can
also afford that company more readily than a fully equipped military contractor.

His sponsorship introduces a limited, low-grade restricted suit capability with
known provenance: local security stock and workshop support maintained for hard
industrial guard work. It is a consequential improvement over ordinary issue,
with conspicuous limits in integration, protection and sustained performance.
A sponsor's permission and stock solve specific access problems; they confer no
military procurement status and do not turn the whole company into elite infantry.

Recovered Greyhook rigs retain their illicit or field-recovered provenance. Their
patterns are adapted from salvage and industrial hardware, clearly below military
patterns even when a particular squad uses them effectively. Recovered Specter
and Arbalest variants must remain below their military counterparts; familiar
roles do not erase degraded condition. Exact patterns, suggested squad
compositions and fitting terms belong to `a-small-war.md` and
the equipment authorities, not this setting's biographies.

## Greyhook flotilla

**Greyhook** is a coalition of free captains, boarders and ground outfits organized
around Captain Sera Voss's ability to assign prizes, settle shares and obtain
repairs. It takes its name from the salvage rig Voss commanded before she
turned to coercion; the hull is long sold, and the name stayed with the people. It is a local flotilla with fragile agreements, not all pirates speaking
with one voice. Its wealth rests on stolen cargo, ransom, forced levies and access
to workshops whose owners cannot always refuse it.

The coalition genuinely preys on neighboring traffic. It seizes medical freight,
coerces crews and punishes resistance through selective destruction. Its people
also buy meals, support dependents and honor some internal bargains. Those facts
explain how it persists; they do not turn its victims into mistaken oppressors.

**The prize crews**, Voss's veteran boarders, secure prizes and decisive rooms.
They work in paired entries behind short suppressive bursts, use cutters and
close-range weapons, and preserve an exit. Dull charcoal pressure gear, pale hook
marks and repaired rigid plates distinguish them from a military uniform.

**Berth Four**, led by yard boss **Daro Reed**, collect levies and intimidate
loading crews. They take their name from the quay berth where most of them once
worked as stevedores, which is why they know every loading crew by face. They mass rifle fire from prepared corners and rush a shaken line.
Orange salvage webbing and mismatched work coats identify their dockside origin.
Their territorial grudges can make them hold ground Voss would prefer to abandon.

**Drent Haulage**, a registered tender company under **Mika Drent**, moves fuel,
prizes and replacement parts. The registration is real and the manifests are not. They favor service roads, false cargo documentation
and delaying ambushes while stores move. Blue cargo stencils survive under scraped
registration paint. They retreat from an unprofitable stand rather than serving
as an endless supply of sacrificial reinforcements.

Heavy rigs are scarce coalition assets, concentrated where a valuable seizure
justifies their repair bill. Patch plates, exposed hoses and hand-marked controls
show actual workshop provenance. Named crews have coherent habits and finite
means; random equipment and endless waves cannot stand in for their identities.

## Sera Voss: command through shares

Voss once commanded a commercial salvage tender. She became a pirate by making
coercion part of her business and kept the habits that made a salvage crew trust
her: remember the shares, inspect the rig and retrieve useful people when possible.
She understands yards better than courts and regards lawful repossession as another
kind of prize-taking. Her analogy is persuasive to some listeners and self-serving.

Her voice is direct, dry and unusually calm. She asks what something is worth and
who is going to carry it. Threats name a practical loss: a load, a berth, a crew's
return passage. She does not explain her philosophy during a firefight.

Her observable flaw is treating refusal as a negotiating posture that pressure
will eventually cure. A crew that cannot pay a levy becomes a lesson to others.
Her loyalty has boundaries: she can rescue a veteran and abandon a coerced dock
worker in the same decision. An opposing officer can respect her competence
without endorsing what she does with it.

**Designer-only intention:** Voss wants stable repair access more than a ruined
Latchkey District. She believes a frightened yard will supply it more reliably than an
independent one. As the company threatens that access, coalition discipline frays;
her tightening demands cause conflict rather than uncovering a harmless true cause.

## Lenna Saye and the yard's independent people

**Lenna Saye** is a fitter elected to speak for the Latchkey association. She keeps
working shifts because credibility comes from having the same hands and access
at risk as the people she represents. She helped negotiate the machinery loan and
cannot dismiss its failure as somebody else's mistake.

Her voice is plain, tired and specific. She corrects “the yard” to the shop or
service being discussed and brings arguments back to who can use it tomorrow.
She can recognize that Dormer's protection saves lives while refusing his proposed
leases. She opposes Greyhook's levies and recruitment; opposing repossession is
not evidence that she wants the pirates to win.

Her observable flaw is asking consensus to carry decisions that now need speed.
She sometimes treats a negotiator's compromise as betrayal before testing its
terms. She cannot promise that every worker agrees, stayed clear of smuggling or
will obey her instructions when relatives are threatened.

**Designer-only intention:** Lenna wants continued independent access and an
accounting process her members can inspect. She fears losing their trust as much
as losing a machine. She is willing to bargain over repayment and security,
provided bargaining leaves them a continuing voice and a livelihood.

After the arc settles, the signed agreement decides who posts the district's
recurring escort work. Under House Custody it comes from Dormer's office, written
on the concession's schedule and carried by his custodial clerks; under the compact
Lenna's association posts common-service consignments (weir pump parts, recovered
stock) with its own delegates. Either way the freight leaves Low Quay's loading lane
for the western apron through the same raider-worked lane, with the same constables
and the same fee: the office changes the voice and the people, not the protection.

**Nadi Orr**, clinic quartermaster at Low Quay, keeps signed shortage lists and
refuses to let either side describe medical freight as expendable. **Foreman Tor
Edek** runs North Weir's maintenance watch and can refuse an unsafe restart.
They are useful witnesses with responsibilities, not neutral dispensers of truth.
Workers can organize escorts, report observed theft, conceal family members,
withhold technical labor or argue for repayment. Their actions must not require
the player to grant them agency first.

## What the captain can know

Castle delivers factual operational briefs: the correspondent, location, agreed
purpose, available support, known opposition and disclosed commercial terms.
Dormer, Lenna, Vale and Voss appear through attributable correspondence, meetings
and radio. Letters can make claims; Castle distinguishes a claim from confirmed
knowledge. No cinematic scene grants the captain access to private deliberation.

Debt documents, manifests, witnessed seizures and actual facility conditions
provide evidence. Radio responds to confirmed landings, sightings, captures,
losses and other public events. A hidden reserve, pirate withdrawal order or
private intention remains silent until an appropriate observation reveals it.
Completed records preserve the actual agreement and outcome, including losses.

The core tension stays legible after discovery: Greyhook threatens real neighbors,
Dormer offers real protection with costly ownership terms, and workers need both
security and control over the means of earning a living. Winning a battle cannot
answer every question those facts raise.
