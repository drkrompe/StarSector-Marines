# Campaign themes

Status: DRAFT

Written: 2026-08-23

## Mercenary company in a fractured sector

The player is a recognised mercenary company: useful to patrons, constrained
by clients and credibility, and unable to be universally trusted. The campaign
is about what such a company owes, protects, enables, and becomes—not about
turning the player into an unconstrained replacement faction.

Local houses, syndicates, cells, and boards make the political layer legible
inside existing vanilla factions. The same enduring conflict can wear corporate,
feudal, underworld, or sectarian fiction without requiring separate simulation
rules for each flavor.

## Consequence ladder

Campaign actions should grow from frequent local consequences to rare,
explicitly authorised sector consequences. Local work can change a house's
standing or stake. Broader economic and political pressure requires a longer,
visible commitment. Faction ownership and diplomacy are exceptional outcomes
with real external consequences, not a routine reward for winning a mission.

This is direction, not a promise that every intermediate escalation is already
implemented. Current political behavior belongs to `living-world-nouns.md` and
the vanilla-transition boundary belongs to `t3-endgame-nouns.md`.

## Design discipline

- The player should be decisive without making the sector passive.
- A client relationship should create opportunity and constraint, not merely a
  reward multiplier.
- Flavor changes names, motives, and authored content; it does not justify
  hidden exceptions to shared campaign law.
- Company growth should create harder choices about reach, obligation, and
  trust rather than only more available work.
