# Campaign economy

Status: DRAFT

Written: 2026-08-23

## Direction

The campaign economy should make a mercenary company choose between immediate
work, dependable commitments, and investment in its ability to operate. Growth
must not become automatic safety: wider reach, more standing commitments, and
more ambitious work should expose meaningful financial and operational risk.

The intended long arc is pressured early survival, a stabilising middle game,
and late-game choices about the cost of scale. Credibility and client access
should be scarce alongside cash, so the company cannot optimise every patron
relationship at once.

## Current boundary

Current code settles contract payout and stationing retainer terms, recovery
choices, and explicit event commitments. The company view can observe vanilla
cashflow and runway alongside active commitments and company credibility.

Licensing postures, insurance, a bankruptcy lifecycle, company-scale
inefficiency, deployment-cost policy, and infrastructure upkeep are not yet
implemented campaign economy. This direction must not be read as a claim that
those systems already charge, gate, or rescue the player.

## Future laws

- A new cost must identify its payer, settlement timing, failure outcome, and
  the campaign authority that owns it.
- A recurring benefit may not outlive its successfully settled upkeep.
- Financial pressure should create choices among commitments and locations; it
  must not silently tax unrelated battle systems.
- Reputation scarcity must come from attributable political and contract
  outcomes, not opaque global penalties.
- Infrastructure remains location-bound direction in
  `infrastructure-nouns.md`; recovery economics remains in `loot-nouns.md`.
