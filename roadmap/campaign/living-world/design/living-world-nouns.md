# Living world nouns

Status: SHIPPED

Written: 2026-08-23

## Purpose

The living world is the campaign's background political field: houses hold
stakes, pursue bounded ambitions, and change the field slowly enough that the
player's operations remain consequential. It produces learned news rather
than an omniscient activity feed, and supplies political causes for selected
campaign events.

## Political field

A **house** is a stable political identity. It is either active or a dormant
tombstone; a dormant house is not recycled into a different actor. A **stake**
is a house's share of one market industry. Stakes are the material substrate of
political change, while rank and promotion describe a house's political reach.

An active house carries one current **ambition**: consolidate a home stake,
seek promotion, or claim a throne. Ambition is intent, not entitlement. It
must remain valid against the present field, and a house may run at most one
autonomous political chain at a time.

The field moves at two tempos:

- **Drift** makes small, deterministic, periodic stake changes among valid
  rivals.
- **Chains** are longer, location-bound political moves with an actor, target,
  and terminal outcome. They make larger changes or prepare a handoff.

Both tempos are background pressure, not a replacement for player agency.
Player contract success can mutate the same stakes and promotion path. A
house that loses every positive stake closes its political work and ordinary
patron work before becoming dormant, preserving identity and historical
outcomes without reviving a spent actor.

## News and discovery

The **Chronicle** records what the player has learned, not every simulation
mutation. Terminal political outcomes become news only when they are intimate
to a house the player knows or epic at high political rank; the middle remains
silent. Active autonomous chains can first appear as uncertain, risk-gated
rumors. A later confirmed outcome does not rewrite the earlier learned record.

Chronicle presentation and voice belong to `narrative-nouns.md`; this feature
owns which political facts are eligible to become learned events.

## Civil-war boundary

A throne claim is a political chain whose resolution prepares a handoff; the
living world never performs faction ownership mutation itself. The
`t3-endgame-nouns.md` boundary owns applying or rejecting that handoff and the
resulting faction state.

The player may participate on either side through contract lineage. Successful
work, rather than mere offer acceptance, establishes a durable side and
contribution record. That record is the authority for later political
consequences; autonomous results remain player-neutral. Contracts own their
ordinary lifecycle and payment terms in `contracts-nouns.md`.

## Campaign-event boundary

The world can surface exceptional choices, but their shared lifecycle belongs
to `campaign-event-nouns.md`. Political chains may source an event; an event
never silently changes a chain's terminal ownership or invents a political
result. Battle setup and tactical rescue behavior remain battle-owned; this
feature owns the campaign facts that a completed event reports.

## Extension laws

- Add political behavior by giving a house a legible intent and a persisted,
  attributable consequence; do not create hidden background winners.
- Preserve deterministic selection and frozen identity whenever a future
  producer chooses among houses, stakes, chains, or sites.
- Keep the Chronicle learned and immutable. New information may append a new
  fact but may not revise history into omniscience.
- Keep external ownership boundaries narrow: contracts resolve service,
  `t3-endgame-nouns.md` applies faction change, and narrative renders prose.
- `house-disposition.md` is future direction only. No trait, loyalty, or
  dossier knowledge mechanic is currently a political decision authority.
