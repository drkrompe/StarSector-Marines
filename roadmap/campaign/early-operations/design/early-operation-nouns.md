# Early operation nouns

Status: ACTIVE

Written: 2026-08-23

Updated: 2026-10-20 — Relief's story chapter starts the company ashore beside Wayline's truck under the ore-dust cover story; Militia Relief keeps company lift.

Early operations are the opening proof that a new marine company can take
meaningful work without being asked to behave like an invasion force. They are
authored First Contract missions, not weakened generic battles. Their force
model remains aimed at a small company as it grows; the fixed, modest payout is
the reason for a veteran company to move on, rather than an eligibility rule
withdrawing the work.

`campaign-overview.md` maps the full authored route. The five arc folders hold
their reading guides, local setting material and remaining work; this document
continues to own the shared authored-operation model and standing chapter laws.
Use the arc's own open-work board before changing its plan.

## Story arcs, chapters, and recurring work

**A Working Ship** is the post-**A Cold Account** recovery arc on Asharu.
Mara refers the company to yardmaster **Tamsin Vale** of the **Basin Reclamation
Cooperative**, an authored local Independent employer. Basin is a Wayline account:
recovering Wayline wins back trade by hauling supplies, fuel and heavy machinery
slightly under rival logistics outfits' rates, and Vale buys through Mara because
it is cheapest. The referral is a supplier on thin margins recovering a customer,
not a favour between friends. Vale suspects her
own outsized fuel order leaked through Wayline's dispatch while its schedules were
being sold; that remains her unproven suspicion. Castle's bargain exchanges
reopening the recovery corridor for the repaired Shepherd **Morrow**. The
basin grew into the cooperative's shipbreaking yard around its claim. Three
cycles ago, the Colossus and accompanying ships came down during unnamed low-orbit
fighting over Asharu. Basin was first to work this basin and built its camp around
the wrecks over time. Recently opening a sealed hold brought rumors and armed
rival scavengers. Morrow is a Shepherd from the same wreckfall, which Basin hauled
onto the yard's service cradle to break for parts; her original crew are long gone.
The occupiers had begun trying to wake her drone bay. No Basin people leave with
her; the company crews her from its own fleet.
Armed scrappers occupy the yard. Vale says they call it abandoned; she disputes
that claim. Both crews work the same salvage trade. That outfit's armed occupation
makes it the opposition, not scavengers or Independents as a whole. The wreckfall
and local claims are mod-authored history, not a general salvage-law rule.
Vale's cousin's crew remained hidden inside the Colossus. Their offscreen report
informs the first briefing; it is bounded correspondence, not a simulated actor,
rescue objective, casualty counter or live source of hidden enemy positions.
The cooperative works in Hegemony-dominated Corvus without acting for the
Hegemony or seeking a fight with it. Vale's local recovery permit is not planetary
government authority. She wants the salvage moving before official scrutiny,
fees or contested claims consume her margin, and before pirates or rival outfits
see a larger opportunity. That is her commercial assessment, not a new canonical
tax law or proof that every official is corrupt. The armed occupiers are the
immediate opponent; Hegemony forces do not become enemies through this contract.
This urgency supplies a motive to hire outside protection, not a hidden expiry,
inspection timer, faction-relation change or permission for fleet bombardment.
The broken Colossus against the northern rear canyon wall holds a shipment the
cooperative found before evacuating, surrounded by ordinary yard stores and hull
scrap. Vale prioritizes access to that shipment and refuses to share its manifest
with hired security. Its contents, buyer, legality and value remain undecided: neither
contraband nor rare schematics are confirmed, and secrecy is not proof of either.
The cooperative keeps the Colossus, shipment and yard and owns Morrow throughout
inspection and repair. The original 18,000-credit fee secures Basin's yard and
protects its inspection; the 22,000-credit fee protects Basin's repair crew.
Basin supplies parts and engineers to repair its own salvage, while its militia
originally cover the outer road. The protected recovery claim is Basin's yard
claim and inspection milestone; it confers no player title or early claim on the
hull.

Cold Start reveals the repaired drone bay's usefulness. Its completed-repair
debrief establishes the need for additional combat and construction to reopen
the haul road. At the first Working Passage briefing, Vale offers payment after
shipment dispatch or a loan. Castle asks for Morrow as the fee for this new work,
having seen the working bay.
The agreement covers construction, repair and defensive positions without promising
a fixed gun count. Basin keeps the built defenses and the road its militia secure.
Title passes only after actual physical departure; no crew transfers with her.
This is a new use for ordinary machinery, not a secretly exceptional hull or a
change to the Shepherd's market price. The Colossus grants no fleet member, extra
objective or cargo inventory. Readouts distinguish the original cash jobs from
the subsequent conditional transfer agreement.
Both parties have a business to sustain. The company sells security, escort and
repair cover for chapter fees and the ship; Vale supplies technicians, equipment
and militia to recover the cooperative's larger source of income. Keeping skilled
people alive preserves those businesses as well as mattering to the characters.
Castle accounts for company pay, supplies and losses; Vale is responsible for her
own crews. Neither employer goodwill nor the company's help makes their interests
identical. Dialogue expresses those interests through the work ahead, practical
questions and personal responses; characters need not recite the contract or
declare their political position. Exact fees, ownership and retry conditions stay
clear in the accompanying readouts. This adds no casualty penalties, cargo
rewards or free replacement ship. `shepherd-recovery-dialogue.md` owns the
chronological dialogue and character register, including the distinction between
what a character knows and what has been revealed to the player.
Completion of the Corvus arc and acknowledgement of its aftermath precede the offer;
the supply-route outcome does not change eligibility. Hound survival is optional.
The three chapter maps revisit one basin: **Salvage Rights**, **Cold Start**, and
**Working Passage**. Salvage Rights uses the company's selected marines and fitted
boats, with two squads recommended and two four-person squads as its minimum.
Cooperative militia hold gains; a physical inspection party follows only after
the company secures the cradle. The protected claim requires safe working time,
living company cover and a surviving technician. A finite response comes along
the haul road; victory does not require hunting every surviving scrapper.
The receiving store and repair workshop are secured by four continuous seconds
of company combatants occupying their actual interiors without a hostile
combatant inside. Empty ground or enemy entry resets that occupation clock.
Exterior recesses in a concave building's enclosing rectangle do not count as
its interior. Company squads approach the centers of its bounded rooms and
clear those rooms after entering; arriving near an entrance or corner does not
mean the building has been cleared. The map outlines that same interior, with
its beacon and Locate destination on a central walkable floor cell.
Those duties use public room geometry and
the company's own presence, preserving player orders and keeping hidden hostile
positions outside mission command.
The fixed 18,000-credit payment and Basin yard-inspection milestone settle once
per accepted victory.
Failed attempts preserve company and technician losses; a retry is a new contract
attempt with a replacement cooperative inspection party, not resurrected crew.
Morrow's original crew are unauthored and are not inferred from that party.

Salvage Rights and Cold Start take place at night. Lamps belong to occupied camp
interiors, entrances and the wreck's service points; open ground remains dark.
Lighting changes presentation, not perception or navigation. Cold Start's completed
shore-power connection brings the yard circuit online immediately; a later attempt
retains that restored circuit. Working Passage departs by day.

pauses rather than restarts, and the end of an attack ratifies nothing.

**District Escort** is the arc's recurring job. It is offered only once *A Small
War* has settled (Chapter 5) under a ratified agreement, and only by that route's
escort contact: Rusk's office under House Custody, or Lenna Saye's association
under the compact, who then replaces Rusk as the Agreus contact. The two offices
differ in letterhead, voice and the handover party's title; support, terms and fee
are identical. Each posting is a finite two-squad escort on the Low Quay freight
lane: the company links up with a fresh consignment and its two-person handover
party at the loading lane, then covers both to the western apron, where four
constables hold. Two raider pickets sit on the lane, and one finite raider group
commits when the company comes down, through the north freight gate to the lane's
crossing. The company screens either side of the lane rather than on it, and the
party walks behind its load. Delivery needs the carrier, a living party
member and company infantry together at the apron under cover; enemy casualties
never stand in for it, and an absent company cannot win through the constables.
Losing the load or both of the party, or withdrawing, ends the attempt unpaid.
Every posting carries a new job and attempt identity, a new consignment and new
people; the route is part of the identity, so an attempt under one agreement can
never settle the other. The active attempt's own receipt settles once for 24,000
credits. The job writes only its own ledger: it awards no named equipment, replays
no ratification, pays no premium, and grants no house promotion, MRB tier,
stationing contract or faction commission. The map reuses Low Quay through its
public generator and solves the carrier's clearance route at setup, so the
district's own layout may change without changing the job.

A **story arc** is a finite sequence of authored operations for a named contact,
with a problem, a resolution, and a visible completed record. A **chapter** is
one operation within it. Victory closes that chapter; defeat or Retreat leaves
it available after the company recovers. A **recurring job** is a separate
posting, with its own identity and terms, which may reuse a chapter's tactical
setup without replaying its historical event. Company growth never resets a
chapter or removes ordinary small jobs.

The opening arc is **Wayline: An Open Road**. **Mara Venn**, an Independent
broker based canonically on **Jangala in Corvus**, represents the **Wayline
freight cooperative**, an authored local commercial interest. Neither her
affiliation nor its militia implies that Jangala's government is Independent.
Wayline used to run freighters between worlds. It lost cargoes to interception,
sold its hulls to cover them, and is down to drivers hauling on one planet's
roads, pooling what is left for local protection. Raiders occupied their freight
depot and now threaten its militia post. Mara is Wayline's broker and, since its
director left for a post off-world, its acting head; she still does the
brokering because nobody else can.

Wayline is a soft target, and that is the public answer to why the raiders are on
its road: no licensed security, no ships left, a watch made of drivers. The
deeper answer is that someone is selling Wayline's schedules. The interceptions
that cost the hulls and the depot raid both followed them. The Corvus Account
reveals the seller as the departed director, Teodor Prell; why he did it, and
who Sable Wake answers to, stays open beyond Corvus. Until then the opening and
supply-route chapters only hint at it, one unexplained line at a time. This is a local company story,
not a simulated change to vanilla market ownership, trade, or faction relations.

The opening introduction is a first commercial relationship. Castle puts the
company forward; Mara introduces herself through the drivers whose pooled money
pays both the checkpoint watch and the company's fee. The preparation handoff
marks a founding company's first paid job, while the office and mission text
refer to the first **Wayline** contract so established companies and retries do
not acquire a fabricated career history. Mara offers further work on the strength
of this job, not a faction commission, permanent payroll or new patron-system status.
Her personal knowledge of the loading crews makes the cooperative's need concrete.

The company's visitor papers on Jangala forbid paid work, so the first job is
written as a consignment. Mara buys the company's own ore dust, the cargo its
arrival hail declared, delivered to the checkpoint gate; Sabine's twelve are the
shipper's escort and ride up the road on a Wayline truck. That is why the story
chapter starts its squad on the ground and pays on delivery. The cover story
belongs to the chapter: Militia Relief is another crew's posting and keeps
company lift. No line names the fee; the amount lives in the offer's terms.

Mission prose stays in-universe. Contacts address the captain, company, crews
and dispatch office; transport requirements name company lift. Persistent
casualties and deliveries are explained through manifests and remaining work,
not player labels, retries, scripted events or campaign-finale terminology.

Only the current uncompleted chapter is offered at Venn's accessible office.
Other Independent brokers refer the company there until the final victory.
Relief is visible before the first squad is founded; deployment owns the
personnel check. The story chapter needs no company lift, so Counterattack is
the first chapter whose deployment checks it. Chapters have no seed lottery or maximum company size.

After the arc, **Militia Relief** and **Depot Clearance** become distinct
repeatable postings at eligible Independent brokers, including Venn. Their
names, identities, source, and local-employer briefings distinguish them from
Wayline's closed chapters. They reuse the finite opening battle setups and
payments. Their victories never write story-completion facts or replay Mara's
story debrief. They remain available after success or failure. This authored
exception does not fabricate ordinary patron contract rows.

Progress derives from existing opening victory facts. Older Relief victories
advance directly to the depot; older Counterattack victories close the arc.
No meeting flag, re-acceptance, or migration resets that history. The final
victory is authoritative even for a legacy record missing its earlier fact.

Relief's pre-mission briefing is Mara's recorded introduction: who she is, what
Wayline lost and what she is asking for, followed by the founding field
commander's word on getting off the truck and going to the line together. The
briefing screen carries an unvoiced tactical summary beside it, so nothing the
player needs to choose squads is said only once over the radio. The tactical
detail is radio traffic in the mission: one ordered exchange begins at launch
and plays through while the company moves up from the apron. Arrivals in Relief
are facts rather than cues, so
the exchange cannot fall out of order; urgent militia losses interrupt it, and
an interrupted instruction resumes only within its relevance window.

Story results contain a message from Mara alongside normal settlement and
personnel facts. Allied and company losses are acknowledged only from the frozen
outcome. A total militia loss is never described as a line saved intact. Story
text does not introduce a survival bonus or override battle victory.
Relief victory also carries a short written Castle/Sabine/Mara exchange: the
first Wayline payment has arrived, Sabine reports her muster, the company has
earned a name with the drivers, and Mara wants to work together again. Sabine and
Castle acknowledge company deaths when present; otherwise she turns to crew pay and supplies without promising that
every survivor is uninjured. The exchange grants nothing and is absent on defeat
and recurring work. Its unrecorded transcript offers no audio playback controls;
the existing tactical radio recordings remain the operational briefing.

The office uses reachable campaign geography, through the one place order the
opening shares with Second Wind's yard (`OpeningPort`). A qualifying market is a
visible, populated, non-player planetary market whose owner is not hostile to the
player. When the company's own port (Second Wind's start yard, or its depot)
qualifies, the office is there. Otherwise prefer Jangala, then the rest of Corvus,
then an Independent world, then the qualifying world nearest Corvus, with market id
