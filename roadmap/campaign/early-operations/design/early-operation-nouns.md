# Early operation nouns

Status: ACTIVE

Written: 2026-08-23

Updated: 2026-10-10 — Relay Nine finite capture, militia holds and road counterattack.

Early operations are the opening proof that a new marine company can take
meaningful work without being asked to behave like an invasion force. They are
authored First Contract missions, not weakened generic battles. Their force
model remains aimed at a small company as it grows; the fixed, modest payout is
the reason for a veteran company to move on, rather than an eligibility rule
withdrawing the work.

## Story arcs, chapters, and recurring work

A **story arc** is a finite sequence of authored operations for a named contact,
with a problem, a resolution, and a visible completed record. A **chapter** is
one operation within it. Victory closes that chapter; defeat or Retreat leaves
it available after the company recovers. A **recurring job** is a separate
posting, with its own identity and terms, which may reuse a chapter's tactical
setup without replaying its historical event. Company growth never resets a
chapter or removes ordinary small jobs.

The opening arc is **Wayline: An Open Road**. **Mara Venn**, an Independent
broker based canonically on **Jangala in Corvus**, represents the **Wayline
freight cooperative**, an authored local commercial interest. Neither her
affiliation nor its militia implies that Jangala's government is Independent.
Wayline's drivers pooled money for local protection; raiders occupied their
freight depot and now threaten its militia post. This is a local company story,
not a simulated change to vanilla market ownership, trade, or faction relations.

1. **Hold Until Relieved**: land beside the militia and break the bandit assault.
   Successful settlement pays 6,000 credits, records Relief complete, and unlocks
   the depot chapter. Recovered traffic logs connect the two operations; the
   story does not require or invent surviving militia witnesses.
2. **Take Back the Depot**: join the local advance and eliminate the depot's
   bandit force. Successful settlement pays 9,000 credits and closes the arc.
   Mara reports that dispatch can resume and refers the company to other
   Independent brokers. Her completed dossier preserves both chapter labels.

Only the current uncompleted chapter is offered at Venn's accessible office.
Other Independent brokers refer the company there until the final victory.
Relief is visible before the first squad is founded; deployment owns personnel
and lift checks. Chapters have no seed lottery or maximum company size.

After the arc, **Militia Relief** and **Depot Clearance** become distinct
repeatable postings at eligible Independent brokers, including Venn. Their
names, identities, source, and local-employer briefings distinguish them from
Wayline's closed chapters. They reuse the finite opening battle setups and
payments. Their victories never write story-completion facts or replay Mara's
story debrief. They remain available after success or failure. This authored
exception does not fabricate ordinary patron contract rows.

Progress derives from existing opening victory facts. Older Relief victories
advance directly to the depot; older Counterattack victories close the arc.
No meeting flag, re-acceptance, or migration resets that history. The final
victory is authoritative even for a legacy record missing its earlier fact.

Story results contain a message from Mara alongside normal settlement and
personnel facts. Allied and company losses are acknowledged only from the frozen
outcome. A total militia loss is never described as a line saved intact. Story
text does not introduce a survival bonus or override battle victory.

The office uses reachable campaign geography. Prefer Jangala when it is a visible,
populated, non-player planetary market whose owner is not hostile to the player.
Otherwise use a qualifying Independent planet, then another qualifying planet,
with stable market-id ordering within each preference. Stations, hidden markets,
and player colonies cannot become an office. This is a read-only location policy,
not a simulated travelling contact: current campaign facts determine its location,
and HQ and local offers consult the same answer. If no office is accessible, HQ
says so and disables its course control. It never points to an absent planet.

HQ provides opening guidance with Venn's portrait, the Independent crest, her
current planet/system, the exact mission and payment, and preparation advice.
The local route is **Marine operations → Mara Venn**. **Plot Course** lays in a
vanilla navigation course and dismisses HQ; it neither launches an operation nor
accepts work. Relief completion advances the notice to the depot job. Completing
the arc retires the notice; its completed dossier and separate recurring work
remain. The broker is an authored Marine Operations
client, not an additional vanilla comm-directory person or relationship track.
Faction-client access and deployment checks still apply; this exception does not
refill ordinary patron contracts or add invented news events.

## Small-company promise

An **early operation** is tuned around scarce player lift, mixed persistent
equipment, and a company still learning to fight together. Employer militia
makes the battle larger but never replaces the player contribution: local
troops take their authored seats and campaign personnel retains ownership of
player seats.

The enemy is a finite ragtag force. An early operation deliberately excludes
regular infantry, mechs, static defense posts, fighter cover, and open-ended
reinforcements. Tuning must first change local force ratios, approach timing,
or battlefield pressure—not add late-game support merely to make a battle more
eventful.

## Authored scenario identity

**Opening operation kind** gives each rung its tactical premise. Relief owns a
local defensive anchor and asks militia to hold until the player arrives;
counterattack omits that predeployed line and makes the joint advance the
problem. Both use militia-only non-player forces. Their shared small-company tier does
not erase different scenario roles or require identical map footprints.

The finite-force command duel uses public scenario places rather than hidden
enemy positions. In Relief, the local line keeps authored garrison ownership,
mobile friendly squads preserve its anchor, and raiders advance on that anchor.
Relief's local line holds a freight checkpoint gate, with a relay office set
back inside its compound. The compound is planned before surrounding buildings:
its inspection frontage, warehouse, rear unloading apron, gates and connected
freight/service roads form one authored place. The story keeps that geography
stable while interior details and the target world's wild surface may vary.
Employer troops arriving by shuttle reinforce the gate under allied garrison
authority. They do not become player squads or remain stationed at their pad.

The bandit assault is a finite force committed at setup. Its opening element
is already on the approach; two later groups enter at road mouths and march
along their authored approaches. First player disembarkation and arrival at the
checkpoint may accelerate separate groups inside bounded release windows.
These are explicit scenario triggers, not hostile intelligence granted to a
commander. Latest-release times keep an absent or waiting player from cancelling
the assault. A lull or the death of the opening element never discards reserves.
Pending ground arrivals count as forces still in play for elimination; victory
requires the entire committed assault to be defeated.

Road arrivals create ordinary, equipped militia squads. Scripted ownership
covers their approach only; reaching it or observing direct contact hands them
to the ordinary assault commander. Squad AI owns contact, cohesion and survival.
The policy neither earns reinforcement tickets nor replenishes its finite force.
Transport approach starts after a short interval for the local engagement to
develop; touchdown still depends on the fitted craft and landing geometry.
The militia should have a line left to relieve, while delaying the company risks
losing it. Local cover is a temporary advantage, not enough extra manpower to
make the player irrelevant. Force and timing changes are checked against both
ordinary arrival and delayed/absent-player controls. A timeout in those controls
is unresolved observation, not a new defeat rule.

Counterattack retakes Wayline's freight depot after the checkpoint victory.
Its larger, mission-first compound provides a sheltered western boat apron,
an approach between sheds, a freight gate, a loading yard and a dispatch
warehouse. Freight circulation bends around the weigh office, interrupting the
sightline from the approach to dispatch. The map publishes three command places:
gate, yard and the finished warehouse interior. Building detail and surface may
vary without moving this sequence. A larger footprint buys distinct encounters;
it does not increase the operation tier or replace the small-company force model.

Employer troops advance under separate allied mission authority alongside the
company. Each attacking squad progresses through the three places from its own
reported position, using attack-move duties that retain the destination through
contact. Player-owned orders remain outside the commander's pool. Neither side
receives an enemy position or a hidden capture state from the scenario.

The twelve raiders start as four gate guards, four yard guards, two permanent
warehouse guards and a two-person reserve. Bounded defensive duties prevent a
warehouse post from chasing the first contact to the landing apron. Forward
contact or losses commit the reserve to the yard; loss of the gate squad recalls
it to dispatch. Gate and yard squads fall back one position after losing half
their starting strength; an engaged yard also falls back if the gate squad is
gone. Those decisions use frozen own-force reports, while squad doctrine owns
contact and survival. The permanent rear guard does not join the reserve.

Reaching a stage is travel progress, not capture. The depot intent leaves the
existing elimination objective and victory authority intact: defeat the finite
bandit force. These scenario duties are not rules later Assault missions inherit.

## Small territorial chapters

A **small territorial profile** reuses compound capture, reversal, resolved
capture rooms and territorial victory without inheriting the generic Conquest
siege. Its map, finite force, arrival manifest and support duties are authored
for that chapter. It has no required airfield, fighter or mech complement,
resource production, open-ended reinforcement layer or free garrison sorties.
This is a mission-scoped exception, not a lower risk setting for a large siege.

**Open the Junction** is the common battle planned for Broken Supply Route.
Its western turnout, Weigh Station, Freight Yard and Junction Dispatch are the
four required territories. Dispatch is the canonical command post. Decorative
warehouses and the roads toward Rill and Relay Nine add no capture requirements.
Those roads do not choose the later campaign branch.

The company takes the first outstanding site in approach order, returning to
lost ground when needed. Employer militia initially cover the landing, then
move to hold the first confirmed gain and retain that duty through reversals.
They remain a separate allied force. Site guards retain garrison authority;
one finite reserve waits in the maintenance building until a hostile site is
confirmed captured, then approaches the first lost site along a screened
service route. No repeated capture creates additional raiders. A reserve killed
before commitment cannot produce an announcement that it counterattacked.

Command reads frozen territory state and own-force reports, never live opposing
positions. Local contact, survival and cohesion remain squad decisions. External
player duties are preserved. Company boats fly their committed fitted airframes;
employer lift is separately identified. Victory requires all four territories
held together and a company participant in play, including committed inbound
personnel. Killing every raider alone does not close the mission.

**Relay Nine** is the freight-recovery finale. Its western apron, Power Yard,
Freight Gate and Relay Hall are four required territories, with the hall as the
canonical command post. The hall guarantees opposed direct-road and loading-road
entrances. Decorative service buildings add no objectives. Company boats retain
their committed fittings; two employer boats deliver four militia each.

The company normally clears Power Yard, Freight Gate, then Relay Hall. Explicit
player orders can change that approach. Confirmed loss of a previous gain takes
priority over a fresh assault; a contested gain can remain the militia's fight
while the company advances. Two stable allied detachments take separate hold
duties in capture order, supporting the first gain together until another is
available. Loss or contest never silently turns those holders into assault squads.
Their physical presence in the capture room, rather than their issued order,
establishes a hold for story presentation.

Twenty raiders begin at local posts. One precommitted eight-person road reserve
enters after the first confirmed Power Yard or Freight Gate capture, using the
service entrance associated with that first loss. Scripted authority covers its
road approach and hands off on arrival or direct contact. The reserve then
tries to retake that gain, or another publicly held gain if its first has been
recovered. Recapture never replenishes it. Guards retain garrison authority;
combat decisions use own reports and local contact. Victory requires all four
territories held together with a company participant; surviving raiders elsewhere
do not prevent success. Neither an extra hold timer nor destruction of relay
equipment is part of this objective.

## Authored evacuation chapters

**Last Lift** returns for Rill's twelve workers after the Junction choice. The
motor pool, loading-street crossing, covered maintenance lane and southern apron
form one authored corridor. Six employer militia hold the shelter approach;
twelve raiders occupy blocking posts and two finite six-person reserves enter
along side roads. Leaving the shelter and crossing the street can accelerate
those arrivals inside bounded windows. Waiting cannot cancel the reserve.
Conventional responders receive the public source and an identity-free alarm;
local beliefs determine interdiction, never the hidden cohort location.

The selected company escort must secure the release point before workers leave.
Each civilian remains a physical person under a close escort leash. Separated
workers become regrouping anchors; a surviving screen can take over a lost
escort's duty. No available escort or no reachable route means holding and a
published explanation. These rules do not erase player command ownership.
Other squads maintain corridor screens and employer militia retain their post.

The employer's pickup has twelve dedicated seats, independent of committed
player insertion boats. Boarding requires a landed craft, available capacity
and physical arrival, one passenger at a time. Pickup loss fails the attempt;
people left alive remain stranded. Victory requires at least one boarding and
resolution of every active person, so it cannot end at the first passenger.
Neither corpses nor people already aboard appear in the next attempt.

A **story person** has a stable identity across attempts, separate from the
entity identity of their current battle. The saved cohort distinguishes rescued,
confirmed dead and still stranded. Terminal abandonment is unsaved, not proof
of death. The immutable attempt outcome carries only confirmed boarding and
death identities; everyone else remains recoverable. Ordinary representative
rescue events keep their existing aggregate population semantics.

Settlement accepts the active attempt identity once, including after reload.
A retry contains exactly the remaining stranded identities. No remaining people
closes the chapter with its actual result; previous rescue on a defeated sortie
does not manufacture a victory. A later qualifying victory may earn the single
chapter entitlement of 4,000 credits plus 500 per cumulatively rescued worker.
Defeat, withdrawal, duplicate results and foreign or stale attempts earn none.
The record does not itself add cargo, credits or personnel: the campaign launch
and settlement adapters retain those external-write responsibilities.

## Story beats and presentation hooks

A **story beat** is a confirmed event in one battle attempt, identified by a
stable scenario key and simulation tick. It is a presentation hook, not a new
objective, a wave-release rule, or a campaign completion flag. Opening scenarios
publish the start, actual company/employer arrival, proximity to authored
places, and terminal victory, defeat or retreat. Relief additionally reports
the original militia line weakening or being lost and actual road arrivals;
Counterattack reports reserve commitment and defensive fallback. Junction
reports completed captures and the first confirmed recapture of each site. Its
militia hold beat requires an accepted hold order and a living ally actually
inside the secured capture room; ordering the move is insufficient.

Relay Nine publishes each site's confirmed capture, first enemy recapture and
first restoration, plus each established militia hold. Its actual road-reserve
arrival remains observer-only. A capture announcement never implies a repaired
machine or settled campaign reward.

Each key fires once per attempt. Events remain in an immutable, ordered history
so a slow presentation frame cannot miss a beat and multiple consumers cannot
drain each other's events. A new battle or retry starts a new history and new
consumer cursors. Completion closes the attempt; later observations cannot add
another outcome. Reaching a place means proximity, never proof of capture or
warehouse entry. Lost militia cannot produce a surviving-line announcement.

Player presentation reads only public events. Hidden road arrivals and defender
orders are observer-only facts for authoring and evidence, not intelligence for
radio or AI. A later sighting-based announcement needs its own confirmed public
trigger. Scenario keys work for both chapters and recurring work; the enclosing
mission identity chooses story-specific content, so a recurring job cannot
replay Mara's historic chapter dialogue accidentally.

The battle publishes facts without running presentation callbacks or consuming
randomness. A future dialogue/voice adapter maps keys to content after simulation
advance and handles queueing, expiry, subtitles and audio under the shared radio
budget in `battle-radio-nouns.md`. Generated voice assets and their provider are
content-production concerns; neither network access nor playback belongs in the
simulation. Hooks alone do not enable spoken lines.

## Boundaries and acceptance

Operation tier supplies the shared scale vocabulary; it does not replace the
small-company force promise or scenario identity. Company-view owns the player's
squad and lift semantics. Battle setup owns finite force composition and
mission command. The arc owns chapter progression and correspondence; recurring
work has no authority to advance it.
Explicitly persisting that authored tier through the mission boundary belongs
to `retire-risk-tier-bridges.md`.

An eventual local-survival incentive must consume allied casualty facts frozen
into the mission outcome. The outcome already carries these facts for debriefs;
a payout or objective incentive remains a separate gameplay change.

The remaining live play is tracked by `opening-ladder-live-acceptance.md`: it
must validate arrival timing, actual lift requirements, force ratios, and
briefing claims. Both chapters have production-factory headless replay evidence;
actual campaign loadouts and the linked live sequence remain to be accepted.
`broken-supply-route.md` plans a follow-on alpha arc with a committed choice;
Junction, Last Lift and Relay Nine have battle prototypes, including a save-owned
rescue ledger and frozen outcomes. Campaign launch, choice, Results and settlement
integration remain open.
Live first-offer discovery and save/reload acceptance remains a separate alpha
gate tracked in `first-contract-discovery.md`.
Shipped implementation evidence is in `shipped.md`.
