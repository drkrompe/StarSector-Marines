# Early operation nouns

Status: ACTIVE

Written: 2026-08-23

Updated: 2026-10-13 - Working Passage departure, recovery and permanent fleet handover; live campaign acceptance remains open.

Early operations are the opening proof that a new marine company can take
meaningful work without being asked to behave like an invasion force. They are
authored First Contract missions, not weakened generic battles. Their force
model remains aimed at a small company as it grows; the fixed, modest payout is
the reason for a veteran company to move on, rather than an eligibility rule
withdrawing the work.

## Story arcs, chapters, and recurring work

**A Working Ship** is the post-**A Cold Account** recovery arc on Asharu.
Mara refers the company to yardmaster **Tamsin Vale** of the **Basin Reclamation
Cooperative**, an authored local Independent employer. Castle's bargain exchanges
reopening the recovery corridor for the repaired Shepherd **Morrow**. The
basin is the cooperative's shipbreaking yard, disrupted by wreckage from recent
fighting overhead. Morrow's crew made an emergency landing in their own cradle
and evacuated; surviving hands return with the repair party. Armed scrappers
occupy the abandoned yard. That outfit is the opposition, not scavengers as a
whole; the overhead battle and local claims are mod-authored history.
The cooperative's local permit is not planetary government authority. Completion of
the Corvus arc and acknowledgement of its aftermath precede the offer;
neither Broken Supply Route branch changes eligibility. Hound survival is optional.
The three chapter maps revisit one basin: **Salvage Rights**, **Cold Start**, and
**Working Passage**. Salvage Rights uses the company's selected marines and fitted
boats, with two squads recommended and two four-person squads as its minimum.
Cooperative militia hold gains; a physical inspection party follows only after
the company secures the cradle. The protected claim requires safe working time,
living company cover and a surviving technician. A finite response comes along
the haul road; victory does not require hunting every surviving scrapper.
The receiving store and repair workshop are secured by four continuous seconds
of company combatants occupying their actual interiors without a hostile
combatant inside. Empty ground or enemy entry resets that occupation clock.
Exterior recesses in a concave building's enclosing rectangle do not count as
its interior. Company squads approach the centers of its bounded rooms and
clear those rooms after entering; arriving near an entrance or corner does not
mean the building has been cleared. The map outlines that same interior, with
its beacon and Locate destination on a central walkable floor cell.
Those duties use public room geometry and
the company's own presence, preserving player orders and keeping hidden hostile
positions outside mission command.
The fixed 18,000-credit payment and recovery claim settle once per accepted victory.
Failed attempts preserve company and technician losses; a retry is a new contract
attempt with a replacement cooperative inspection party, not resurrected crew.
The original Morrow crew's complement and casualties are not inferred from that party.

Salvage Rights and Cold Start take place at night. Lamps belong to occupied camp
interiors, entrances and the wreck's service points; open ground remains dark.
Lighting changes presentation, not perception or navigation. Cold Start's completed
shore-power connection brings the yard circuit online immediately; a later attempt
retains that restored circuit. Working Passage departs by day.

Cold Start follows the protected claim. The company escorts cooperative repair
cargo to the workshop and covers living technicians at the pump and grounded hull.
Delivered cargo and restored shore power are persistent field milestones; unfinished
work needs another mobilization. Confirmed technician losses remain on the account,
and replacement technicians are new cooperative personnel. A qualifying repair
victory pays the agreed 22,000 credits once. Withdrawal or defeat can preserve
completed field work without claiming repair victory or paying the chapter fee.

Restored power enables a mission-provided construction command card backed by
Morrow's grounded drone bay. The captain chooses a clear exterior plot; ordinary
finite, vulnerable builder drones fly out, work and return. Known hostile contacts
and actual damage interrupt construction under the standing tender rules. Grounded
construction retains the large static hull and its collision, has a fixed working
reach and grants neither flight nor a fleet member. Completing a real road gun
allows the repair task to proceed; the gun fires and takes damage ordinarily.

Working Passage follows completed repairs. The same hull travels in a low hover
with its full 24 by 20-cell body, clearing terrain along its actual movement.
Living company presence and clear ground release its successive assembly positions.
The pump bend and narrows each require a surviving constructed turret before the
company advances; two distinct cooperative militia teams inherit the earlier holds.
Finite scrapper teams approach along the named haul and quarry roads. Player orders
retain authority over the company and Morrow's helm; completed or cancelled build
work returns her to mission travel unless another remote order owns it.

The eastern yard requires 55 seconds of safe drive work, paused by absent company
cover, hostile pressure or an out-of-position hull. Finished work releases physical
climb-out; a timer or departure timeout cannot authorize transfer. Critical disable
at or below one fifth hull strength aborts the attempt. Withdrawal and defeat retain
the recovery claim and completed repairs, record confirmed militia losses, and leave
another mobilization available. Vale recovers and refits the same hull and assigns
replacement holders; unfinished departure work and field guns do not carry over.

Accepted physical departure transfers one actual fleet Shepherd named **Morrow**,
plus willing working crew sufficient for that member's actual minimum complement.
They enter fleet crew, not marine squads. The accepted attempt settles once and
retains the awarded member's permanent identity, including after sale or loss.
Later construction support requires a currently owned eligible hull; a closed reward
receipt never summons a replacement. There is no departure cash payment. The claim
alone grants no fleet member or general construction capability. `shepherd-recovery.md`
retains live campaign, save/reload and subsequent support acceptance.

**A Small War** is the planned next arc after that actual fleet handover. Vale's
reference leads through Mara to **Baronet Elian Rusk** on **Agreus**, in the
nearby **Arcadia** system. His local concession under Ko Combine administration
supports a bounded procurement warrant and recovered suit rewards through work against the **Greyhook flotilla**. Equipment
is issued to individual marines and can be mixed within existing squads. The
company also chooses who controls access to the liberated Latchkey Yard; earned
patterns remain learned on either route. `agreus-latchkey.md` owns the local
setting and cast; `a-small-war.md` owns the unimplemented chapters, agreements,
rewards and acceptance. No earlier Shepherd milestone substitutes for departure.

A **story arc** is a finite sequence of authored operations for a named contact,
with a problem, a resolution, and a visible completed record. A **chapter** is
one operation within it. Victory closes that chapter; defeat or Retreat leaves
it available after the company recovers. A **recurring job** is a separate
posting, with its own identity and terms, which may reuse a chapter's tactical
setup without replaying its historical event. Company growth never resets a
chapter or removes ordinary small jobs.

The opening arc is **Wayline: An Open Road**. **Mara Venn**, an Independent
broker based canonically on **Jangala in Corvus**, represents the **Wayline
freight cooperative**, an authored local commercial interest. Neither her
affiliation nor its militia implies that Jangala's government is Independent.
Wayline's drivers pooled money for local protection; raiders occupied their
freight depot and now threaten its militia post. This is a local company story,
not a simulated change to vanilla market ownership, trade, or faction relations.

The opening introduction is a first commercial relationship. Castle puts the
company forward; Mara introduces herself through the drivers whose pooled money
pays both the checkpoint watch and the company's fee. The preparation handoff
marks a founding company's first paid job, while the office and mission text
refer to the first **Wayline** contract so established companies and retries do
not acquire a fabricated career history. Mara offers further work on the strength
of this job, not a faction commission, permanent payroll or new patron-system status.
Her personal knowledge of the loading crews makes the cooperative's need concrete.

1. **Hold Until Relieved**: land beside the militia and break the bandit assault.
   Successful settlement pays 6,000 credits, records Relief complete, and unlocks
   the depot chapter. Recovered traffic logs connect the two operations; the
   story does not require or invent surviving militia witnesses.
2. **Take Back the Depot**: join the local advance, capture dispatch, then
   defend it against two finite road attacks. Secure Wayline's offline Hound
   for the push and warehouse defense.
   Successful settlement pays 9,000 credits, transfers that chassis and closes the arc.
   Mara reports that dispatch can resume and refers the company to other
   Independent brokers. Her completed dossier preserves both chapter labels.

**Secondhand Wings** bridges Counterattack and the later supply-route work without
adding another mandatory chapter. Castle explains why Second Wind's repaired Hermes
couriers are limiting the company; Mara locates a pair of surplus Aeroshuttles. The
optional 5,000-credit purchase follows separate restoration of the second bay and
exchanges the old complement for two refurbished 6-seat boats. HQ retains a Boat Deck
pointer until purchase. Story completion, not repeatable Depot Clearance, unlocks it.
Boat ownership, payment, review, loss and the hull-specific adapters are defined in
`company-view-nouns.md`. This interlude uses text and existing portraits, with no
new narration recordings.

Mission prose stays in-universe. Contacts address the captain, company, crews
and dispatch office; transport requirements name company lift. Persistent
casualties and deliveries are explained through manifests and remaining work,
not player labels, retries, scripted events or campaign-finale terminology.

Only the current uncompleted chapter is offered at Venn's accessible office.
Other Independent brokers refer the company there until the final victory.
Relief is visible before the first squad is founded; deployment owns personnel
and lift checks. Chapters have no seed lottery or maximum company size.

After the arc, **Militia Relief** and **Depot Clearance** become distinct
repeatable postings at eligible Independent brokers, including Venn. Their
names, identities, source, and local-employer briefings distinguish them from
Wayline's closed chapters. They reuse the finite opening battle setups and
payments. Their victories never write story-completion facts or replay Mara's
story debrief. They remain available after success or failure. This authored
exception does not fabricate ordinary patron contract rows.

Progress derives from existing opening victory facts. Older Relief victories
advance directly to the depot; older Counterattack victories close the arc.
No meeting flag, re-acceptance, or migration resets that history. The final
victory is authoritative even for a legacy record missing its earlier fact.

Story results contain a message from Mara alongside normal settlement and
personnel facts. Allied and company losses are acknowledged only from the frozen
outcome. A total militia loss is never described as a line saved intact. Story
text does not introduce a survival bonus or override battle victory.
Relief victory also carries a short written Castle/Mara exchange: the first
Wayline payment has arrived, the company has earned a name with the drivers,
and Mara wants to work together again. Castle acknowledges company deaths when
present; otherwise she turns to crew pay and supplies without promising that
every survivor is uninjured. The exchange grants nothing and is absent on defeat
and recurring work. Its unrecorded transcript offers no audio playback controls;
the existing tactical radio recordings remain the operational briefing.

The office uses reachable campaign geography. Prefer Jangala when it is a visible,
populated, non-player planetary market whose owner is not hostile to the player.
Otherwise use a qualifying Independent planet, then another qualifying planet,
with stable market-id ordering within each preference. Stations, hidden markets,
and player colonies cannot become an office. This is a read-only location policy,
not a simulated travelling contact: current campaign facts determine its location,
and HQ and local offers consult the same answer. If no office is accessible, HQ
says so and disables its course control. It never points to an absent planet.

HQ provides opening guidance with Venn's portrait, the Independent crest, her
current planet/system, the exact mission and payment, and preparation advice.
The local route is **Marine operations → Mara Venn**. **Plot Course** lays in a
vanilla navigation course and dismisses HQ; it neither launches an operation nor
accepts work. Relief completion advances the notice to the depot job. Completing
the arc retires the notice; its completed dossier and separate recurring work
remain. The broker is an authored Marine Operations
client, not an additional vanilla comm-directory person or relationship track.
Faction-client access and deployment checks still apply; this exception does not
refill ordinary patron contracts or add invented news events.

## Recovery, confidence and scale

The pause after the first Relief victory includes **A Proper Issue**: Mara makes
weapon and scout-suit patterns available, and Castle teaches one actual squad
refit in the Armory. Exactly two equipment paths give the player a manageable
choice and equipment to use on the depot job. The reward supports the company's
recovery without claiming casualties or resource bills have vanished. The
lesson is optional and resumes later; it does not gate Counterattack. Collection
and issue semantics remain in `progression-nouns.md` and `company-view-nouns.md`.


Wayline begins close to dissolution: losing the depot threatens the drivers'
work and the cooperative's ability to stay together. Relief buys breathing
room; recovering the depot restores somewhere to organize work. Mara's referrals,
the recovered Hound and the optional Secondhand Wings purchase make the company's
growing capability tangible. Purchased craft are celebrated only after delivery.
No chapter declares the player's finances secure or their roster intact.

The supply-route finale gives a pause before the Corvus pursuit. Freight receipts
support renewed commercial work; rescued people support Iona's continuing recovery
arrangement. Each account retains its losses and the forgone branch. Exhausted
failures remain failures. The subsequent introduction reads the persistent story
ledger, including recoveries or deliveries on earlier attempts.

Asharu turns reaction into investigation. Blind Watch is the confidence crest:
intelligence and preparation let the company choose the target and disable the
listening post. Its successful debrief gives Mara and Castle time to recognize
that initiative before asking for the next deployment. Casualties qualify that
achievement rather than disappearing behind a triumphant speech.

Cold Account puts that earned competence beside state military power. The company
finds the proof and secures its own extraction; Resolute supplies a scale of force
it cannot match. Mara's gratitude and continuing work remain real. Naval cooperation
in this operation grants no promise of future protection. The ending preserves
what the player built and gives them reason to choose future commitments carefully.

## Broken Supply Route campaign

After Wayline, Mara introduces dispatch coordinator **Iona Serr** and guarantees
**Open the Junction** at the accessible office. Opening its dossier explicitly
introduces the arc and freezes its planet and local sites. Merely projecting
an offer or reading HQ does not mutate a save. Later office movement never moves
accepted work: an inaccessible frozen site pauses deployment, and HQ retains
its name with an explanation. Older completed Wayline saves start uncommitted.

Junction victory pays 12,000 credits once and opens two inspectable requests.
Only **Commit support to Rill** or **Commit support to Freight** issues the
permanent support order. Inspection, Back and terrain preparation do not choose.
The cooperative funds one recovery mobilization; the other crew receives a
withdrawal order. Spare company boats, defeat, delay and reload do not reverse
that commercial decision. There is no menu timer or hidden faction penalty.
Commitment alone earns neither money nor recurring access. The commitment
panel remains visible beside its button, outside scrollable briefing prose.
It explicitly names Last Lift as the rescue branch's last story chapter, its
successful Crew Recovery unlock, and the forgone Thunder Run path.
The freight choice names Thunder Run, the successful Route Security
unlock, and Rill's lost escort without inventing deaths. Each branch has one distinct finale: civilian escort and boarding at Rill, or
physical cargo delivery along the freight corridor.

- **Last Lift** promises 4,000 credits plus 500 per cumulatively rescued person,
  at most 10,000, once on qualifying victory. Rescued and confirmed dead workers
  persist across defeated attempts; only stranded people enter another battle.
  Exhausting the cohort without victory closes the actual result without payment
  or an unlock. Successful rescue leaves this commercial route suspended and
  establishes Iona's **Crew Recovery** arrangement. Thunder Run is not undertaken.
- **Thunder Run** begins immediately on freight commitment and closes that branch.
  Junction is the common territorial setup; no second capture-and-hold chapter or
  separate relay fee precedes the convoy. Three identified employer cargo
  carriers have persistent delivered, lost and awaiting-transport states.
  Retry fields only unresolved carriers. A qualifying victory accounts for all
  active cargo with a surviving company presence and at least one delivery
  across the job. It pays 6,000 plus 4,000 per cumulative delivery, at most
  18,000, once, and establishes Mara's **Route Security** arrangement. Exhausted
  cargo on a defeated sortie closes the actual result without payment or an
  unlock. Older saves already closed after Relay Nine retain Route Security;
  unfinished, unlaunched Relay Nine commitments migrate directly to the convoy
  finale without inventing a relay victory or paying for a skipped battle. A saved
  active Relay Nine attempt keeps its identity and agreed award through settlement,

  frozen sites and recurring entitlements survive migration.

Recurring offers carry new job and attempt identities. Crew Recovery receives a
new twelve-person ledger for each job and the same rescue formula; a retry keeps
that job's stranded identities. Route Security uses the Relay Nine capture-and-hold battlefield and pays 12,000
per successful new job. Recurring results retain aggregate rescue/loss and payment records without
resetting the story crew or replaying a chapter bonus. The completed dossier
separates historical results from the current posting. Existing Wayline
repeatables and unrelated contracts remain available.

All four operations recommend twenty-four marines. They accept at least two
selected squads with four ready marines each, using acting NCOs where necessary.
One to four owned fitted boats cycle until the selected named force is delivered;
partial final trips contain only real selected personnel. Issued equipment,
squad identity and command groups cross the ordinary deployment boundary.
All company berths on each chapter's insertion apron share one logical landing
area. Six-seat loads and later ferry trips reunite into their original campaign
squad, preserving its NCO, fire-team billets and command group; a berth is not
a separate tactical destination. Arrival assembly counts every member once and
releases the squad's duty when its committed strength has landed.
Employer militia and the twelve-seat civilian pickup remain separate forces.
Wayline supplies heavy lift for the company's active mech lance on these chapters
and their recurring jobs. Mech Support carries only owned chassis with their
frozen installed equipment; an empty bay grants no substitute walker. The company
selects the support power in its briefing and calls it onto clear ground during
battle. Arriving mechs join that operation's mission commander and remain eligible
for optional direct control. Fighter cover and other strategic powers remain absent.

The arc owns a versioned save record, including its frozen site, commitment,
current job and attempt, story outcome and awards. Unknown or inconsistent
records fail closed. Terrain surveys read prospective identities; acceptance
starts the attempt only after construction and the roster overlay succeed.
Settlement consumes the frozen mission and rescue/freight identities once before the
ordinary credits, personnel and lost-boat writeback. Duplicate or foreign results
cannot repeat any of those writes. Outcome presentation is not reward authority.
This is local commercial fiction: no vanilla market ownership, population,
stability, industry or trade simulation is changed.

**The Corvus Account** follows either closed Broken Supply Route branch,
including a recovery or freight run whose finite ledger ended in failure.
Mara's correspondence preserves that account; it never claims the unchosen
operation succeeded or invents casualties. Existing recurring arrangements
remain at their original site.

The arc concerns **Sable Wake**, a mod-authored pirate crew selling Wayline's
sailing schedules. It has three ordered, single-payment chapters:

Blind

The
Hegemony Onslaught arrives nose-first from the east and stations its entire hull

fire; newly landed troops are subject to the same actual-position clearance rule.
The boundary adds six cells beyond the installation's

leave through ordinary flight. Campaign identity and combat records travel with
the passengers; a destroyed loaded boat loses them. Safe extraction does not
satisfy the defender's elimination objective.

insufficient, and a lost loaded boat cannot count as recovered. Empty returning

## Small-company promise

An **early operation** is tuned around scarce player lift, mixed persistent
equipment, and a company still learning to fight together. Employer militia
makes the battle larger but never replaces the player contribution: local
troops take their authored seats and campaign personnel retains ownership of
player seats.

The enemy is a finite ragtag force. Relief and Counterattack deliberately exclude
regular infantry, mechs, static defense posts, fighter cover, and open-ended
reinforcements. The later Thunder Run finale explicitly adds a small prepared
machine-gun checkpoint opposed by employer armor; this does not raise the
opening chapters to that equipment tier. Tuning must first change local force ratios, approach timing,
or battlefield pressure—not add late-game support merely to make a battle more
eventful.

## Authored scenario identity

**Opening operation kind** gives each rung its tactical premise. Relief owns a
local defensive anchor and asks militia to hold until the player arrives;
counterattack omits that predeployed line and makes the joint advance the
problem. Both use militia-only non-player forces. Their shared small-company tier does
not erase different scenario roles or require identical map footprints.

The finite-force command duel uses public scenario places rather than hidden
enemy positions. In Relief, the local line keeps authored garrison ownership,
mobile friendly squads preserve its anchor, and raiders advance on that anchor.
The company receives a bounded five-cell defense area around its gate rally;
acquiring a distant target must not discard the approach before it reinforces
that area. Broken morale can still interrupt the duty.
Relief's local line holds a freight checkpoint gate, with a relay office set
back inside its compound. The compound is planned before surrounding buildings:
its inspection frontage, warehouse, rear unloading apron, gates and connected
freight/service roads form one authored place. The story keeps that geography
stable while interior details and the target world's wild surface may vary.
Low cliffs frame the checkpoint and service-road approaches, with open ground
between its varied workshops. The rear compound has marked freight berths on
concrete rather than isolated pad tiles. The employer unloads south of the
through-road; the company's two berths share the northern apron so a split
transport load can assemble and advance together. Inspection parking and small
service yards stay outside the gate fight, relay entrances and attacker routes.
Employer troops arriving by shuttle reinforce the gate under allied garrison
authority. They do not become player squads or remain stationed at their pad.

The bandit assault is a finite force committed at setup. Its opening element
is already on the approach; two later groups enter at road mouths and march
along their authored approaches. First player disembarkation and arrival at the
checkpoint may accelerate separate groups inside bounded release windows.
These are explicit scenario triggers, not hostile intelligence granted to a
commander. Latest-release times keep an absent or waiting player from cancelling
the assault. A lull or the death of the opening element never discards reserves.
Pending ground arrivals count as forces still in play for elimination; victory
requires the entire committed assault to be defeated.

Relief fields twelve initial raiders and two four-person road groups. All twenty
carry Rooks at their ordinary low militia grades, matching the new company's
