# The Broken Supply Route

Status: PROPOSED — detailed alpha mission design; gameplay and campaign implementation remain open.
Written: 2026-10-10
Updated: 2026-10-10 — persistent civilian outcomes and retries for remaining people accepted; mission implementation and prototype tuning remain open.

## Scope and dramatic question

Wayline has its depot back. The next problem is restoring the outlying route
that feeds it. Mara introduces **Iona Serr**, the cooperative's dispatch
coordinator. At **Rill settlement**, drivers and maintenance workers are stranded;
at **Relay Nine**, the cooperative can still recover its dispatch installation
and bonded freight. The company decides which operation receives local support.

The choice changes the work the company does and the local program that survives.
Iona argues for people whose names are on her dispatch board. Mara argues for the
route that pays those people and the other crews along it. Both acknowledge the
cost. Neither becomes a villain or imposes a hidden faction-standing penalty.

This is a fictional local incident on Mara's accessible planet, offered without
a random roll after Wayline completes. Freeze the planet and site identities
when introduced. Later office movement must not relocate accepted work. Validate
or fall back before introduction; explain and pause inaccessible accepted work.
Do not claim ownership of a vanilla industry or mutate vanilla trade, market
ownership, stability or population.

Canonical boundaries: `early-operation-nouns.md`, `contracts-nouns.md`,
`narrative-nouns.md`, `conquest-nouns.md`, `extraction-command.md`, and
`battle-radio-nouns.md`. **Everything below is proposed content.** Map dimensions,
force counts and payments are prototype inputs to measure, not shipped balance.

## The choice at a glance

| | Bring them home | Keep the road open |
|---|---|---|
| Mission | **Last Lift** — escort and evacuation | **Relay Nine** — capture and retain territory |
| Main problem | A moving civilian cohort exposes gaps in the escort | Captured ground needs defenders while the assault continues |
| Company jobs | Lead screen, close escort, rear guard, landing-zone cover | Assault one position, hold another, reserve to answer recapture |
| What ends it | Resolve the rescue cohort with survivors physically boarded | Hold every required compound, including dispatch, with a company participant in play |
| Enemy behavior | Interdict crossings and landing approaches from local contact | Defend posts, fall back and use a finite force to retake lost ground |
| Immediate reward | Up to Cr. 10,000, based on people actually recovered | Cr. 16,000 for territorial success |
| What is given up | Relay crew withdraws; this commercial route stays suspended | Rill's escort request is withdrawn; the company cannot claim its rescue |
| Lasting access | Iona's **Crew Recovery** work | Mara's **Route Security** work |
| Lasting record | Rescued, lost and still-unrecovered people; suspended route | Route reopened; settlement withdrawal reported without invented deaths |

Each successful playthrough contains **two battles**: a common Junction mission
and one branch finale, with a decision between them and a closing dispatch.
The unchosen finale is recorded as **not undertaken**, not a failed battle.
Existing Wayline repeatables and unrelated sandbox contracts remain available.

## Why the company cannot simply select both

The cooperative authorizes one recovery mobilization: its local guides, escort
team and support transport go to one site while the other crew receives a
withdrawal order. This is an explicit commercial and organizational commitment,
not a claim that a wealthy fleet could never mount two operations. Alpha offers
these two commissioned resolutions, not a hidden third rescue-everything test.

The support order is issued only by **Commit support**. Before that action, both
briefings remain inspectable. After it, the unchosen operation is withdrawn even
if the company has spare boats. Additional player force helps execute the chosen
job; it does not reverse the cooperative's recall or buy a second story reward.
No menu timer, campaign travel deadline or unseen countdown makes this decision.

Commitment immediately fixes the opportunity cost, but grants no reward or
recurring access. The chosen site's successful resolution is still required.
While the mission remains unresolved, HQ says **evacuation committed / recovery
pending** or **freight committed / relay contested**, never a successful ending.

## Common chapter — Open the Junction

**Purpose:** teach taking and retaining territory after Wayline's elimination
battles, and let the player see the two roads before choosing between them.

Prototype: **192×112**, with a roughly 120×70 combat area; recommended two full
campaign squads (24 marines), six employer militia, twenty finite raiders.
Allow a documented understrength launch with two campaign squads containing at
least four ready marines each (eight total), then prove whether this is viable. Four-squad companies keep
the same opposition. Named officers improve coordination; acting NCOs remain
valid. No mechs, fighter wings or infinite replacement economy in this opening.

Authored geography:

- A protected western turnout provides separate player and employer berths.
- **Weigh Station** controls the road entrance and is the first capture room.
- **Freight Yard** controls the northern service approach.
- **Junction Dispatch** is the final command post, at the split between signed
  roads to Rill and Relay Nine. Those exits are landmarks, not extra objectives.

The first gate falls before the whole junction does. Four starting reserves
respond through the yard after the first confirmed territorial loss, using
ordinary defender knowledge for combat. They can retake an unattended station.
The employer element receives an explicit hold duty after a secured position is
available, so the company sees why a holding force matters. This handoff is a
planned mission behavior, not something the current opening commander already does.

Prototype force: four at Weigh Station, four at Freight Yard, eight at Dispatch,
four in reserve. All are committed at setup. Capture can reverse; victory requires
all three hostile compounds and the friendly beachhead held at once, plus a
company participant still in play. Killing all raiders alone is not victory.
Do not register decorative buildings as surprise territorial requirements.

Successful settlement pays **Cr. 12,000** once and opens the decision. It does
not silently choose a branch based on which road the player approached or which
commander survived. Company losses use ordinary settlement; neither later branch
requires the same named officer or a particular surviving militia witness.

## Evacuation finale — Last Lift

**Player experience:** advance to people, escort them back through contested
ground, then protect boarding. Progress changes where the useful ground is.

Prototype: **192×128**, with a sheltered southern apron, a central loading-street
crossing and the Rill motor pool in the north. A longer maintenance lane offers
cover around the short exposed street. Both paths are genuinely navigable; the
escort commander must follow a route guide, not pull civilians through buildings.

1. **Make contact.** The twelve-person work crew shelters in the motor pool;
   six employer militia hold nearby cover. Company boats land at the apron.
   A player escort must reach and secure the release area. Nobody starts a long
   unescorted sprint because the battle clock began.
2. **Bring the crew out.** The cohort follows its assigned escort toward the
   apron. Other squads screen the next crossing and protect the rear. A lost
   escort or blocked route pauses movement and produces a readable request for
   help; it does not teleport people or make them charge through contact.
3. **Cross under pressure.** Twelve raiders begin at blocking positions. Two
   additional six-person groups are the entire off-map reserve: the first is
   released after the cohort leaves shelter, the second after it reaches the
   crossing. Give the releases bounded latest times so waiting cannot cancel
   them. Entry occurs on connected side roads outside the protected shelter and
   apron, never underneath the cohort. Exact times require replay tuning.
4. **Protect the lift.** Employer evacuation capacity is reserved for all twelve
   people, visibly separate from company insertion seats. Use a physical pickup
   craft and actual seat/cycle accounting. Civilians count as recovered only
   after successful boarding through the existing evacuation boundary; standing
   near a marker, requesting pickup or destroying raiders is insufficient.

The company flies its committed fitted boats. Any employer pickup craft is named
as employer support in the manifest. It cannot replace the player's insertion
boat or turn civilians into marine cargo, recruits or saleable inventory. The
single twelve-person cohort represents twelve actual people in this story;
there is no implied population multiplier or second cohort to micromanage.

**Proposed objective and payment:** aim to recover all twelve. A victorious
rescue requires at least one person boarded and resolution of all active cohort
members; it must not terminate automatically at the first boarding. Partial
rescue is reported plainly. On successful chapter settlement pay **Cr. 4,000 +
Cr. 500 per recovered person**, up to Cr. 10,000. The terms show the formula and
maximum before commitment. This rewards another safe passenger without imposing
an arbitrary six-person cliff. Defeat and Retreat retain ordinary defeat payment
rules; they cannot be used to collect the base fee repeatedly.

Defenders are conventional raiders: source guards and bounded responders, not
Civilian Rescue's swarm director. Their alarms may authorize mobilization but
must not disclose the live cohort position or the company egress. Local sightings
and contact determine interdiction. Civilian tracking and the conventional
commander need a deliberate adapter; sharing the Extraction label does not
provide this combination automatically.

**Success consequence:** the cooperative records this route as suspended because
its relay team withdrew. Iona establishes a crew-recovery arrangement with the
company. Mara accepts the decision but mentions the lost run and its work; she
still offers ordinary contracts. The ending gives actual rescued and lost counts,
with no blanket "everyone is safe" line after a partial rescue.

## Freight finale — Relay Nine

**Player experience:** take a compound, leave useful strength behind, and press
on while raiders try to cut the connection. This is where hold and assault groups
have an obvious purpose beyond a compass label.

Prototype: **208×128**, with three hostile compounds and a friendly landing
beachhead. Recommended company strength remains 24 marines; eight employer
militia support the advance and hold duties. Initial raiders total twenty, with
one eight-person road reserve. All forces and arrivals are finite.

- **Power Yard**, nearest the western approach, controls the installation's
  service access. It has a bounded capture room, not an enormous outdoor zone.
- **Relay Hall** holds the dispatch equipment and is the canonical command post.
  A direct approach is exposed; a loading corridor gives another entry.
- **Freight Gate** controls the road beyond the hall. Its lateral service road
  lets the reserve threaten the company's earlier position without teleporting.

The company can approach Power Yard or Freight Gate first. Capturing either
releases the precommitted road reserve once; repeated recaptures never manufacture
another wave. Defender guards remain at useful posts while the reserve follows
own-force reports and local contact. The Marine command picture distinguishes
**hold Power Yard**, **assault Relay Hall**, and **support Freight Gate** using
authored places. Employer holders are separately owned, finite and replaceable
only from the support already committed to this mission.

Victory follows Conquest's territorial law: all required compounds, including
the beachhead, are held at once and the company retains a participant. Dispatch
then confirms reconnection in the outcome. This prototype does not promise a
technician repair minigame, a hidden "hold for two minutes" test or a destructible
relay-integrity bonus. If an intact-equipment mandate is added, it needs explicit
asset-result authority and briefing disclosure before it becomes a rule.

**Success consequence:** pay **Cr. 16,000** once, mark the authored route reopened,
and unlock Mara's route-security arrangement. Rill's crew received a withdrawal
order without company cover; dispatch can report that order, not a fabricated
successful evacuation or death toll. Iona remains a correspondent rather than
an enemy faction. Her recovery program was not established with this company,
so its exclusive work is unavailable. Ordinary rescue missions elsewhere remain.

## Recurring work that makes the choice matter

| Unlocked arrangement | Repeatable job | Tactical promise | Prototype terms |
|---|---|---|---|
| Iona: recovery roster | **Crew Recovery** | Reach a new stranded crew, escort it through conventional interdiction and protect physical boarding. New cohort and job identity each time. | Cr. 4,000 + 500 per recovered person for a twelve-person prototype; tune profitability against duration and losses. |
| Mara: route-security roster | **Route Security** | Retake a small transfer site and leave enough strength to prevent recapture while securing dispatch. | Cr. 12,000 fixed for a two-to-three-compound prototype; no infinite reinforcement grind. |

These are authored repeatable offers at the owning contact's office, subject to
ordinary access and deployment checks. The branch unlock is durable; it does not
require farming a new personal-standing score. Use the existing repeatable offer
cadence instead of promising an evolving network of simulated trade routes.

Do not balance by cash alone. Recovery may yield less cash per job but should
also support a shorter sortie and different risk to the company's troops.
Measure time, company casualties and replacement cost before fixing the repeated
rates. Freight's larger one-time fee must not imply a permanently superior
income path. Neither unlock supplies free personnel, equipment, manufacturing
discounts or bonuses to every future mission.

## Failure, retries and persistence

The committed branch never changes after defeat, Retreat, reload, canceled
launch or delay. Junction and Relay Nine remain retryable after ordinary defeat;
payment and recurring access arrive only with a qualifying successful settlement.
A failed selected branch leaves its own objective unresolved; it does not undo
the other crew's already-issued withdrawal order or mark both missions failed.

**Accepted evacuation retry policy:** rescued and
confirmed lost people persist. A later attempt concerns only the still-stranded
members, using stable campaign cohort identities mapped to new battle entities.
Already rescued people cannot be rescued or paid for twice; known dead people
never respawn. A live person not recovered before withdrawal is recorded as
unrecovered, not automatically described as killed. This requires distinguishing
unrecovered from the existing rescue tracker's broader terminal "lost" bucket.

Retry is available while recoverable people remain. When none remain, close the
branch with the actual result, including an unsuccessful or partial ending if
there was no qualifying victory. Do not create a victory or payment merely
because a prior defeated sortie rescued somebody. If a later attempt wins, its
single chapter settlement may use the persisted cumulative rescued count for the
promised payment; no attempt or Results revisit pays that count again. Emergency
Retreat remains the standard defeat action, not a disguised successful extraction.

The arc owns one versioned record: frozen target/site identity, chapter,
commitment, per-attempt outcome identities, rescue accounting, final route status
and reward-access facts. Settlement consumes frozen outcomes once; it does not
inspect an ended battle or infer choices from cash, roster, display strings or
current UI selection. Unknown branch values fail closed. Older saves with Wayline
complete begin uncommitted; newer partial records never unlock both branches.

The completed dossier records the chosen operation and measured result, the
alternative as not undertaken, payment and recurring access. HQ shows pending
work until closure, then replaces the arc prompt with the appropriate contact
work. Reading correspondence is not the authority that grants an unlock.

## Decision presentation and story hooks

Show both requests together after Junction: Iona's portrait and a crew/boarding
symbol opposite Mara's portrait and a relay/freight symbol. Each panel presents
mission shape, recommended/ready force, employer support, cash and the exact
opportunity cost. Hover carries detail. Do not express this as a good/evil meter.

Inspection, Back and opening a briefing never commit. **Commit support to Rill**
or **Commit support to Relay Nine** names the selected operation and permanently
withdrawn alternative. Persist that action before enabling launch. Use the
existing briefing and fitted-boat manifest afterward, with 1920×1080 / 100% review.

Proposed one-shot hooks use the existing story-beat history. Exact keys are
reserved at implementation; the following are semantic requirements:

| Beat | Required confirmed fact | Presentation use |
|---|---|---|
| Junction held | Territorial victory, not entering Dispatch | Mara introduces the two requests |
| Support committed | Persisted branch transition | Iona or Mara acknowledges the explicit cost |
| Crew contacted / released | Local escort established / cohort actually moving | "We're with you. Keep the road ahead clear." |
| First / final boarding | Actual tracker boarding; final line checks counts | Iona acknowledges recovery without inventing survivors |
| Militia hold established | Actual allied hold assignment and arrival | Explain who protects the company's rear |
| Compound retaken | Confirmed player-visible territorial reversal | Call attention to the route being cut |
| Route reopened | Frozen successful territorial outcome | Mara confirms the commercial resolution |
| Defeat / withdrawal | Actual terminal outcome | Loss-aware correspondence; no success unlock |

Hidden wave releases and reserve commitments stay diagnostic until a separate
player-observed fact permits an announcement. Sound generation and playback stay
outside the simulation and share the radio voice budget. Narrative remains the
comms officer's fact-bound report with attributed correspondence; it does not
invent omniscient cutscenes. A wiped militia unit cannot deliver a later voice line.

## Implementation dependencies and acceptance

The Wayline map and fitted-boat passes now have headless evidence; their linked
live campaign acceptance remains open. This plan does not mark that gate complete.

1. **Small territorial profile.** Author explicit dimensions, compound count,
   force and arrival budgets for Junction. Current Conquest still has the large
   siege floor and structural/airfield assumptions. This needs an intentional
   mission-scoped exception, not lower risk or a renamed siege. Preserve capture,
   reversal, resolved rooms and terminal territory law. Disable inherited
   open-ended production and free auto-garrison cycles for these finite jobs.
   Coordinate with `mission-tier-nouns.md`; do not silently change generic Conquest.
2. **Junction prototype.** Prove the three capture rooms and one recapture threat
   with two- and four-squad forces and no named officer. Record capture order,
   hold abandonment, company/employer losses, actual boat identity and duration.
3. **Branch battle prototypes.** For Last Lift, combine cohort escort/boarding
   with conventional interdiction and explicit employer pickup capacity. For
   Relay Nine, prove hold/assault duties and the one finite road reserve. Use
   larger maps only where traversal creates decisions rather than empty walking.
4. **Campaign record and settlement.** Implement branch/target persistence,
   the accepted persistent rescue accounting, one-time payments and
   separate recurring access. Preserve the existing loss and Retreat boundaries.
5. **Choice and correspondence.** Integrate broker/HQ choice, completed history
   and contact offers. Add confirmed hooks; audio assets are later content work.

Acceptance must cover:

- Guaranteed discovery after Wayline, including old completed saves; no retired
  story mission reappears and neither branch blocks unrelated contracts.
- Inspect/cancel/reload before commitment; double-click commitment; reload after
  commitment, defeat, Retreat and victory; unavailable target and unknown save values.
- Last Lift: full rescue, partial rescue, no rescue, escort loss, pickup delay,
  boat loss, withdrawal with people still stranded and a retry that cannot
  duplicate people or payment. Result text distinguishes dead from unrecovered.
- Relay Nine: first capture, reserve arrival, a recapture while the company pushes,
  restoration of the lost position, and victory with surviving raiders elsewhere.
- Player fitted-boat identity, shared-apron clearance, one slower boat versus
  multiple boats, acting-NCO leadership and two-/four-squad force controls.
- Both resulting contact offers are distinct repeatables with new job identities;
  no branch epilogue, chapter-completion fact or first-time bonus repeats.
- Extend existing evidence selectors; duplicate traces remain deterministic and
  timeouts remain unresolved. Review renders at 1920×1080 / 100%, then complete
  campaign/save and live mission acceptance.

Prototype numbers remain adjustable after evidence. Last Lift's persistent
civilian outcomes and retries for remaining people are accepted design. Implement
and verify that accounting before enabling campaign progression for the branch.
