## Placement and employer

Unlock after **A Cold Account** is won and its aftermath has been acknowledged,
regardless of the earlier evacuation/freight choice. This is an optional new
contract, not an automatic launch or a timed demand while the company is depleted.
Mara makes the introduction. Wayline remains a trusted former employer; this
contract belongs to someone with a different business and a different stake.

**Basin Reclamation Cooperative**, an Independent association of local repair
shops and industrial operators on **Asharu**, wants its inland recovery corridor
working again. **Tamsin Vale**, its yardmaster and contract signatory, is a working
engineer responsible for people, pumps and equipment stranded in the basin. She
counts useful machinery before saleable scrap and dislikes promises that leave
her crews exposed. She can arrange permission for this recovery operation; she
does not speak for Asharu's government or the Independent faction as a whole.
The cooperative, Vale, basin and ship are mod-authored local fiction.

The basin was a working **shipbreaking and reclamation yard** before the fighting.
Sorted hull sections, cutting beds, machinery stores and a rear haul loop made
it a place where damaged ships became useful parts. Recent fighting overhead
scattered fresh wreckage across those older work sites. Torn sections and shallow
impact trails interrupt the organized stock; they are not more recoverable ships.
The overhead engagement is mod-authored local history, not a named vanilla battle
or a claim that the player's campaign simulation caused it.

The Shepherd **Morrow** was the cooperative's working salvage tender. Caught in
that fighting, its crew brought the damaged ship back down to its own receiving
cradle. It made a controlled emergency landing, not an intact fall from orbit.
Its workshop survived, but flight controls, power distribution and drone servicing
need work. The crew shut down the damaged systems and evacuated through the
receiving apron. Surviving crew members return with Vale's repair party; recovering
the ship is not a search for people sealed inside it. The exact original complement
and casualty count are not yet authored and must not be invented by dialogue.

Armed **scrappers** moved in after the evacuation and began stripping machinery
from the yard and route. The familiar salvage livelihood is shared with Vale's
legitimate crews; the opposition is this armed outfit occupying her claimed yard,
not all scavengers or the Independent faction. Their presence reads through
cutting stations, parked collection vehicles and removed components rather than
another generic military base. They are opportunists exploiting the gap, not
another secret arm of Sable Wake. There is no capital-ship rescue waiting offscreen.

The cooperative already holds the hull's recovery claim. Castle negotiates its
transfer as payment in kind for completing all three operations: the cooperative
supplies engineers and replacement machinery, gets its corridor reopened and
retains the fixed installations; the company keeps the repaired Shepherd and its
drone workshop. The agreement is explicit before the first drop. Securing the
wreck alone grants a protected claim, not a functioning ship in the fleet.

The emotional turn after Garnir is practical independence. We cannot match an
Onslaught, but we can stop asking a larger power to hold every position for us.
This should feel like acquiring a workshop and crew through work together, not
being gifted another escalation in firepower.

After successful Working Passage and actual fleet handover, Vale can refer the
company through Mara to Baronet Rusk on existing Agreus in nearby Arcadia.
`a-small-war.md` owns that optional five-chapter follow-on and its recovered suit
rewards; `agreus-latchkey.md` owns the local setting and cast under Ko Combine
administration. The referral requires completed departure, not the
claim or Cold Start repair settlement, and remains unimplemented with that arc.

## The basin

`ShepherdBasin` authors a **304 × 176** map with one persistent geography and three
visits. Its production roads and terrain are editable catalog plans. Asharu uses
the actual world's captured palette at campaign launch; the review uses ARID.

**Salvage Rights and Cold Start are night operations.** Low ambient light retains
terrain readability. Occupied camp interiors and their entrances are lit;
Morrow's warm emergency fixtures illuminate the service corners. Open ground
remains dark instead of acquiring scattered decorative light pools. Cold Start begins on
those surviving circuits; restoring pump power should visibly restore the broader
yard floodlights without changing the time of day. Working Passage is the following
day's departure. Environmental lighting is presentation, not a new stealth rule:
it does not change either side's sight, targeting or navigation.

The west receiving apron contains four elongated berths on connected concrete,
with clear western approaches. The worn road curls around the recovery cradle,
past the pump bend, through the eastern narrows and into a climb-out yard. Northern
cliffs and southern spurs explain the bends without making a single corridor for
infantry. A secondary service loop passes behind the wreck and workshop. Southern
haul and eastern quarry roads provide legible enemy approaches from beyond view.

The northern salvage racks face the rear service road, with working aisles
between torn hull and drive sections. Southern cutting beds feed the receiving
yard. Fresh wreck fragments lie along diagonal impact trails east of the cradle
and below the machine store, outside the road and suggested construction plots.
Scraped concrete and failed shore equipment lead into Morrow's cradle; medical
supplies and an abandoned muster point sit beside the receiving store. These
signs persist between visits, including after the ship leaves. Larger wreck
sections are ordinary solid scenery with cover; surface scars alone are shallow
visual damage, not a hidden movement blocker or extra salvage economy.

Varied workshop, depot and dispatch stamps have actual doors and usable interiors.
Parked haulers, spare machinery and cargo sit in working yards, outside circulation.
Two open construction plots overlook the pump bend and narrows, with separate
infantry maneuver space around them. Plots are good suggested locations, not the
only legal construction cells. They do not pre-place free guns or force the tender
to ignore threats while its builders work.

The first two visits contain an inert grounded Shepherd using the base-game hull
resource and a matching solid **24 × 20-cell footprint**. This explicit grounded
scale reflects the installed hull's 15 minimum crew, capacity for 30, 100 cargo and
drone bay; it is deliberately larger than compressed overhead flight scale.
The source art and footprint annotation remain with the ordinary asset exporter.
Cold Start adds servicing equipment to
the yard. Working Passage replaces the static hull with one live low-hover tender;
the emptied cradle remains visible behind it. Her moving body retains the same
24 × 20-cell size until climb-out, with swept terrain clearance for mission,
remote and piloted travel. Repair completion does not shrink her to overhead scale.

## 1 — Salvage Rights

**Promise:** take a useful wreck, not a pile of loot. Teach clearing the ground
around an asset and handing a secured position to allied holders.

**Briefing — Vale:** “Those marked rows were our stock before the shooting started
overhead. The scattered pieces came later. Morrow's crew got her back to the
cradle and got out. Then the scrappers moved in. The engines are cold; the workshop
is not a total loss. Clear the receiving stores, get my
people into the repair shop, and hold the hull access while they inspect it. The
road to the pumps is still occupied. We are recovering a ship, not conquering the
basin in an afternoon.”

Beat order:

1. Actual company boats land on the west apron. The receiving store screens the
   first advance; a northern foot approach lets a small force turn its defenders.
2. Secure the repair workshop. Militia move up only after the company establishes
   the gain and hold the workshop access; they do not race to the wreck.
3. Secure the cradle service point. A finite raider team approaches from the south
   haul road, trying to recover its salvage. Inspection requires living company
   presence nearby and an uncontested work area. Killing the original guards alone
   does not finish it.
4. Engineers confirm that the drone bay is recoverable. Company can withdraw;
   Vale's people hold the secured yard between missions. Persistence records the
   recovery claim, not a free fleet member.

**Castle, hull secured:** “Put the registry number in the agreement. We bring your
   crews back to work; when this ship flies, it flies with us.”

**Vale:** “When it flies. I need the pump road open before either of us owns a
   working future.”

**Vale, first sight of the hull:** “Her crew put her down there with half the
controls dead. Some of them are on the repair team. They know what can be saved.”

**Castle:** “Then we keep the cutters away from her until they get a look.”

These are text-only story hooks and a written Castle/Vale recovery agreement.
No new recordings are included. The paid claim closes this chapter; failed attempts
retain confirmed inspection casualties and send a replacement party on a new attempt.

## 2 — Cold Start

**Promise:** restore the tool under pressure. Teach defending a work site and
covering fragile builders; avoid an idle repair countdown.

**Briefing — Vale:** “Our replacement distribution rig is at the receiving apron.
Morrow's surviving hands will meet it at their old workshop. Escort it there,
then reconnect the pump station. We can wake the drone
bay on shore power. I will not send repair crews into a running firefight. Give
them room to work, and we can put the first gun on the road before the raiders
come back in strength.”

Beat order:

1. The yard is visibly serviced and militia hold the earlier gain. Escort one
   repair carrier along the clear service lane, not through the grounded hull.
2. Clear and hold the pump station while an engineer connects power. Raiders
   approach in small finite detachments from the south road and eastern quarry.
   Trigger them from escort progress and power work, not a long fixed silence.
   Successful connection restores the yard-light circuit around the pumps and
   cradle immediately; emergency lights remain on throughout. A later mobilization
   retains completed shore power.
3. Restored power exposes **Build Vulcan Turret** in the ordinary command tray.
   Choose its card, then clear exterior ground near Morrow. Target cancellation uses
   the normal command interface. The first builder deployment uses the real Tender construction path, limited
   to the grounded ship's safe working reach. A suggested gun position protects
   the cradle approach. Contact interrupts unsafe work under ordinary tender rules;
   no special invulnerable tutorial drone flies through fire.
4. Hold the repair yard and pump approach while the crew finishes a finite repair
   task. Field-completed milestones persist on a retry; unfinished work remains.
   A stopped task resumes only while its living crew and location are safe.

**Castle, first gun online:** “That is a position we do not have to fill with
   another squad. Keep the crew covered. Let the machine do its job.”

**Vale, repairs complete:** “Drone bay answers. Pumps hold pressure. Tomorrow we
   find out whether she still remembers how to leave the ground.”

## 3 — Working Passage

**Promise:** carry your own means of holding ground. The payoff is a coordinated
advance by infantry, militia and workshop ship, not a capital gun run.

**Briefing — Vale:** “Morrow can hover, but I will not trust the repaired drives
to a climb inside this basin. Walk her out past the pumps and through the narrows.
Clear a position, let her builders harden it, then bring the company forward.
Our militia will hold what you leave behind. At the eastern yard we have room
to test the drives and clear her for ascent.”

Beat order:

1. A live Shepherd hovers from the now empty cradle. Its short move to the first
   assembly point is visible; the player is not teleported to a different map.
2. Clear the pump bend. Pause the tender behind the company's forward position,
   establish a gun, then hand the hold to a separate militia detachment.
3. Push through the narrows. A quarry-road attack threatens the side of the route;
   a south-road party pressures the previous gain. Stagger their commitments so
   the player has a decision about where to retain a squad. Do not stack all
   allied squads on one exact objective coordinate.
4. Secure the climb-out yard and hold the last approach through 55 seconds of safe
   drive work. Hostile pressure, absent company cover or an out-of-position hull
   pauses the work. Earlier constructions and militia holds contain pursuit.
5. Morrow climbs out. Castle confirms the registry transfer with Vale. The actual
   named fleet hull is awarded once, and later eligible missions can select it
   as fleet construction support.

**Castle, departure:** “Registry accepted. Morrow is ours. Hull, workshop, and a
   very long list of maintenance jobs.”

**Vale:** “The corridor is ours again. Keep her working, Castle. That is what
   those ships were built for.”

The transfer includes an explicit berth offer to willing working crew sufficient
for the awarded member's actual minimum complement. They join fleet crew, not marine
squads; no named engineer award is authored. The fleet receives an actual Shepherd
named Morrow only after confirmed physical departure, with no cash payment. The
permanent member receipt prevents another award after debrief replay, reload, sale
or loss. Campaign support still requires a currently owned eligible source.

## Remaining battle and campaign acceptance

### Battle contract and remaining acceptance

Cold Start is a **6–8 minute target**, subject to playback. Start with the earlier
workshop held and the receiving store clear. The repair carrier waits at the apron
until the company advances; it stops before hostile contact rather than charging
through it. Escort arrival commits one four-person road team. Beginning the pump
connection commits two four-person teams, staggered between the south haul and
quarry approaches. These are finite authored commitments, not an endless spawn timer.
The cooperative's militia keep separate workshop and carrier holds.

The connection requires a technician physically at the pump and company cover.
Interruption pauses the current task. Completing it permanently records shore power
and changes the yard lights immediately; a retry starts with that circuit restored.
Then expose one suggested gun position through the existing Tender construction
interface. The first completed gun commits the last road team, so the player sees
the tool working before the repair is signed off. No completion by enemies merely
aging out offscreen. The ship remains grounded, with the large landed body and
ordinary vulnerable builders. No fleet ownership is granted in this chapter.

Persist completed repair milestones and confirmed party losses. Replacement crews
are explicitly new cooperative personnel; incomplete task seconds reset on a new
sortie. Losing the carrier before delivery requires a new delivery; losing every
available technician ends the attempt. Completed shore power is not revoked. The
campaign settlement must reject duplicate receipts before payment or milestone writes.

Working Passage is a **7–10 minute target**, subject to playback. Morrow's advance
is gated by cleared assembly positions, not a timed dash independent of the company.
The player clears the pump bend, shelters the tender behind it and places a defence;
one militia team inherits that hold. A quarry team then pressures the narrows while
a smaller southern team tests the previous gun and militia. The final yard is a
short defended spool-up, with a finite pursuit along the road the player just secured.
Keep each team physically committed to a named approach and avoid simultaneous
unreadable attacks on all three positions.

The tender's critical disable aborts departure rather than awarding a replacement
hull. Preserve the recovery claim and completed repairs; a new sortie begins with
Vale's explicit recovery/refit of the same grounded ship. The debug fixture may
start with that work complete, but the campaign may not silently respawn it inside
the current battle. Successful climb-out grants one named fleet member, tracked by
persistent identity; replaying the debrief cannot add another. Subsequent sale or
loss ends access to its construction support until the player owns another eligible
ship. Critical disable at or below 20% hull strength aborts the attempt, as do hull
loss and failed departure. Confirmed militia losses and the failure remain on the
account while the claim and completed repairs survive. These are implemented rules;
the duration targets remain tuning goals until measured playback and live review.
