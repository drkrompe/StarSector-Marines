Use case: sketch-to-render, seamless tileable texture. Asset: the exposed internal structure of a battered starship hull where its outer armour plating has been torn away, seen straight down from directly overhead, for a top-down tactical game. Image 1 is the authoritative geometry guide and edit target (1024x1024). Reskin it into a finished painted texture, keeping the layout of every element: the light vertical bars are heavy hull ribs (I-beam frames with flanges, rivet rows and weld seams); the lighter horizontal bars are longitudinal stringers; the mid-grey irregular patches are shreds of torn deck and inner plating still clinging to the frames, their edges ragged, buckled and bent; the short pale bars are snapped and twisted rib ends; the near-black between everything is deep shadow inside the hull, with hints of cable bundles, pipes and lower decks far below. Scorching and soot around the torn edges, a few small dull ember-orange heat marks, chipped grey-brown industrial paint, bare dark gunmetal where the paint is gone. The image must be SEAMLESSLY TILEABLE: whatever leaves the left edge continues on the right edge and whatever leaves the top continues at the bottom, with no border, no frame, no vignette and no lighting gradient across the image. Fully opaque, no transparency. Straight overhead orthographic camera, no perspective, no horizon, no cast shadow direction stronger than soft ambient occlusion. Desaturated, medium-dark values overall (it will be tinted to each ship's colours at runtime), but strong local value contrast between bright frame edges and dark gaps so it reads at small size. Hand-painted Starsector-like game sprite finish, gritty grounded industrial science fiction. No text, no letters, no labels, no logos, no watermark, no UI, no people, no fire or flames, no glowing neon.
