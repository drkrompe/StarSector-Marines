DAMAGE STATE: REMAINS. This is what is left on the ground after the structure in the identity reference has been destroyed: the same structure, dead. Keep its layout and identity (the same places for its plates, tusks, sacs, slabs and roots, the same sun from the upper left), but it is a corpse now: the carapace has gone dull, dry and ashen, greyer and paler than the living painting, with the gloss gone; plates have slumped flat and some are torn away, leaving dried sinew sockets; tusks and spines are snapped to stumps; roots are withered and shrivelled. For a bore: the shaft has caved in, its throat fallen in on itself and choked with churned soil and rubble where the guide shows brown mounds, so it reads as a collapsed, filled-in hole, not an open pit. For a spore colony: every sac has burst and deflated into a wrinkled, torn, empty skin around a dried crater. For a ground colony: its sinew arms have shrivelled flat and dry against the ground, their spines snapped to stumps, its core plates slumped and split, the amber eye gone dark. No amber glow anywhere, no wet highlights: everything is drained, dry and dead, like a husk. It must read at a glance as the dead remains of that structure.
