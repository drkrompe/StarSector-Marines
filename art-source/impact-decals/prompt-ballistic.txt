A sheet of sixteen bullet and shell-strike decals for a top-down tactical ground-combat game: the marks that
kinetic rounds leave where they hit the ground or a wall.

Rows, top to bottom (each row is one kind of mark; the four columns are variants of it):
1. Small-arms strikes in the ground (rifle, SMG and light machine-gun rounds). Each round disc is a small, ragged dark
   pocket kicked into the surface, not a neat round cup, with a broken ring of disturbed pale dust; the scattered dots are flecks of spat
   dust and grit thrown out around it. Shallow, no rubble.
2. Small-arms strikes on a hard wall. Each star shape is a bullet pock in concrete or masonry: a small near-black
   hole at its centre and a ring of pale chipped spall around it, its jagged edge the flaked-off surface.
3. Heavy-round gouges in the ground (anti-materiel rifle, heavy machine gun, autocannon). The round touched down at
   the thin left end and ploughed to the right: a furrow torn into the surface, narrow and shallow at the left,
   deepest and darkest near the right end, with a raised lip of torn material along both edges. The dots at the
   right end are clods and chips thrown forward.
4. Heavy-round strikes on a hard wall. The central shape is a deep, dark punched crater with broken pale spall
   round its rim; the thin lines are cracks radiating out through the surface.

Layout: reference 1 is the exact layout. It is a 4 x 4 grid of equal square cells on a transparent canvas, and each
flat grey shape is the footprint of one decal (blurred grey means a soft, feathered edge). Fill every grey footprint
with its decal, keeping its position, size, outline and orientation. Do not move, merge, add or remove marks. Keep
every cell's empty space fully transparent. Marks that point sideways point along +x (to the right): the round
travelled left to right.

These decals are stamped at random onto every kind of ground and wall in the game: earth, grass, sand, concrete
paving, metal deck, brick, cast concrete and olive-painted plate. So their colours must be neutral and work over
any surface: soot and scorch near-black to charcoal, cavities dark, freshly exposed dust, chips and spall pale
neutral grey, a faint warm brown only in soot falloff. Every outline is rough, ragged and organic, like real damage: never a regular geometric star, flower or
perfect circle, even where the grey footprint is close to one. Never paint the surrounding ground or wall itself, and never
tint a mark green, brick red or sand yellow.

Style: painted, slightly gritty realistic game art, seen from straight overhead, lit from the upper left, matching
reference 2. Read clearly at small size: strong dark centres, crisp light rims where material is chipped.

Output: a true transparent background, including every gap between and inside marks; no ground, no floor, no wall
tiles, no cast shadow slab, no grid lines, no border, no text or labels. Each decal must stay inside its own cell.

Reference 2 is material and style only: blast damage overlays from the same game. Do not copy its layout or shapes.
