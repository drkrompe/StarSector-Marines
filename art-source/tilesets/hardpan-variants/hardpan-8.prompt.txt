A seamless ground material texture for a top-down tactical game: the bare
ground of an irradiated, airless dead world, seen from directly overhead.

Material: scorched, bleached, glassed hardpan. Pale bone and ash-white to pale
grey-beige baked crust, broken into a polygonal desiccation crack network: flat
plates separated by clearly legible dark grey cracks, 2 to 4 pixels wide,
so the network still reads when the image is shrunk to a quarter of its size. Plates are irregular polygons,
each roughly 1/12 to 1/16 of the image width across, so there are many of them.
Harsh and washed out, very low saturation, nothing grows. Average colour close
to RGB (182, 178, 164): pale and slightly warm grey, lighter than grey stone,
greyer than desert sand, warmer and duller than snow.

Variant feature: larger glassed areas: about a third of the surface is a fused, bubbled, pale olive-green trinitite glaze, slightly glossy, in irregular patches distributed across the whole image, with cracked bleached hardpan between them.

Rules:
- Square image, the whole frame filled edge to edge with this one surface.
- Flat, even, overhead lighting. No directional sun, no cast shadows, no
  vignette, no gradient across the frame, no highlights larger than a few pixels.
- The feature is repeated and spread evenly across the ENTIRE image, so that
  any crop of the middle third still contains it. No single central subject.
- Low contrast, fine detail. No objects, rocks, debris piles, craters, rubble,
  plants, footprints, vehicles, text, borders or frames.
- Photographic orthographic material scan, not an illustration.

The reference image is for format only (an opaque, flat, fine-grained ground
tile seen from overhead); do not copy its colour or its texture.
