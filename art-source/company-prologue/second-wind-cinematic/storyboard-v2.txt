SECOND WIND ARRIVAL - STORYBOARD V2
Status: DRAFT for discussion; surface-yard setting selected by the user.

Story
After nearly a decade of asteroid impacts and a desperate recovery, Second Wind
reaches Jangala. The yard can take her weight; the damage is still far beyond the
crew's means. The arrival ends before the berthing conversion begins.

Setting
A surface industrial spaceport: open landing apron, fitted support cradle,
perimeter service gantries and a sheltered operations booth. The sky, distant
port activity and ground contact establish the location. Leave the approach and
descent volume clear. No enclosing hangar doorway is required for this beat.
The ship retains accepted V3 component placement and the heavy-arrival skin.

Twelve-second edit: three selected moments of a longer landing procedure

0-4 seconds - REACHING THE YARD
Wide view from the ship's port quarter, low enough for the yard structures to
give scale. The vessel is already lined up with the cradle's long axis and
moves slowly along a straight approach. Show the destination in the same frame.
Keep the ship level; no bank, yaw or lateral slide into a narrow doorway.

4-8 seconds - WHAT IT TOOK TO GET HERE
Closer port-side view at hull height. Track the same direction of travel across
the cratered forward shoulder, welded backing plates and makeshift repairs.
Yard structures passing behind the hull demonstrate slow relative motion.
The ship decelerates. Keep the ship's guns, side pod and thrusters consistent.
This view should make the patches look load-bearing and desperate, without fire
or an open pressure breach. No need for a new mechanical failure or sparks.

8-12 seconds - THE YARD TAKES THE WEIGHT
Cut to a steady side-wide view on the same side of the approach axis. Fore-aft
motion has stopped. The final short vertical descent brings the hull onto a
fitted cradle. Supports meet established structural hardpoints under the hull,
clear of damaged plating, the side pod, shuttle opening and engine bells.
Only once supported do retaining arms secure it. End on a quiet, stable berth
with the old damage plainly visible. Arrival is not a repair/restoration receipt.

Continuity rules
- The edit contains time ellipses: the whole approach and landing is not forced
  to occur in twelve seconds of physical time.
- Choose one screen direction in blockout and preserve it through the cuts.
- Keep all cameras on the port side of the approach axis; no unexplained reversal.
- Derive berth dimensions from the accepted ship model and Hermes scale. Do not
  invent an absolute ship length from this concept sheet.
- The cradle stays level and the hull's longitudinal axis matches it.
- Show a credible load path and actual clearance around every support/gantry.
- Occupied walkways remain outside the landing/exhaust safety volume. People
  loading aboard belong in the later sheltered dock sequence.

Unresolved before scene animation
Ground touchdown is now the selected staging, but the accepted hull reference
does not establish a landing-lift system. Blockout must resolve whether the
final descent uses established ventral lift/control hardware or explicit yard
assistance. Rear-facing engine bells alone cannot explain a controlled vertical
lowering. Do not quietly add a new thruster layout or hide this with dust.
If a credible descent cannot preserve the ship design, revise the board to a
ground transport cradle carrying the already-landed hull into its service berth.

Production sequence after storyboard agreement
1. Reuse the accepted V3 hull and create one shared Blender scene for the apron,
   cradle, gantries and cameras. Grey geometry, no detailed texture generation.
2. Animate the complete maneuver and cut a greybox animatic. Review support,
   clearance, scale, screen direction, pace and touchdown before surface work.
3. Keep real scene geometry for perspective, parallax, occlusion and shadows.
   Apply generated material/texture work to the actual meshes or controlled
   camera projections. Independently painted cutouts are limited to distant
   backdrop elements, not the ship/cradle interaction.
4. Render beauty, shadow, depth and object masks from the same cameras. Composite
   restrained lighting/atmosphere effects and later sound on those stable passes.

The existing orbital movie is retained only as a compositing technique study;
its hangar and approach were rejected. No V2 Blender scene or new painted scene
has been produced yet.
