Purpose
Bring the people from Harker's Claim back to a populated world. Begin with the
experience of a tired civilian aboard the ship, let Jangala become a place beyond
the campaign's planet portrait, then step outside to show what carried them here.
Relief comes from reaching people who can help. The ship is still badly damaged.
The arrival ends before Nessa's inspection and the paid berthing conversion.

Campaign continuity

The production CompanyPrologueScreen and active strings resolve exact sequence
and current text. The eight-page intro begins on the bridge, recalls the Claim's
decline, abandonment and salvage, then returns to the crew, Nessa and a yard plan.
This cinematic continues that journey; it does not retell all eight pages.

The crew includes miners, cargo hands and families. Sabine and her twelve are
already aboard but are a distinct escort section. Do not turn every passenger
into a uniformed marine, give the camera a new named protagonist, or identify the
viewpoint with the Commander. Castle speaks to the Commander elsewhere aboard;
an overheard conversation need not address the person beside the window.

Nessa must be selected as the destination and her estimate received before entry.
Her relationship with Castle is established; Mara, Wayline's later briefing,
company formation and usable shuttles are not outcomes of landing.
Arrival buys a chance at repairs, not a restored ship or a ready mercenary company.

Lore grounding
Jangala is a Hegemony world in Corvus with a dense native biosphere, seas and
severe weather. Large agricultural clearings and organized industry sit beside
dangerous native growth. Surface exposure calls for biofilters and decontamination.
These facts support a beautiful exterior seen from a closed, worn cabin, followed
by managed port infrastructure and controlled disembarkation.
The named Jangala Station is orbital. Our destination is an authored surface yard
on the same world, not a relocation of that station or a newly named canonical city.

Viewpoint and geography
A sealed, pressure-safe crew passage or improvised passenger space has one modest
reinforced side viewport. Its actual location must be reconciled with the accepted
V3 hull and damaged compartment plan in blockout. No panoramic glass bridge.
Start close to a seated or braced passenger: a work sleeve, harness or hand and
a worn window seal are enough. Keep the person off-screen or partly framed.
The viewport opens a wide field as the camera settles near the glass. A separate
wide landscape cut may follow, but do not float the camera through a solid hull.

Use one shared terrain plan: cloud break -> broad forest/river catchment ->
managed land and freight routes -> industrial port -> Nessa's yard apron.
Choose a single approach heading, port-side window and consistent sun/storm
orientation. Later exterior cameras remain on that side of the flight path.
The world moves aft outside the window; do not reverse that flow between interior
cuts. The exterior view looks back toward the hull from outside: track the same
world heading, rather than blindly matching the ship's motion to scenery motion.
Altitude changes take time. Cloud transitions and editorial ellipses bridge the
long descent; the whole voyage is not performed physically inside one minute.

Proposed cut: about 65 seconds, flexible until voice takes are measured

01 / 0-6 seconds / ENTRY GLOW
Inside the worn cabin at the end of atmospheric deceleration. Orange light beyond
the glass dims, vibration eases, and the dark window begins to show cloud.
Keep the cabin contained and functional; patches and rattles suggest strain
without inventing a fresh failure. Do not begin with a triumphant exterior flyby.
Sound: low structural rumble and cabin ventilation, with a possible tail of the
preceding Castle line only when the edit is continuous. No loud new exposition.

02 / 6-18 seconds / FIRST OPEN SKY
Remain beside the window as cloud thins. Give the landscape a broad, held reveal:
canopy extending to distant water and a storm bank far off the approach corridor.
The warm entry glow gives way to green-blue daylight on the cabin and sleeve.
Hold several seconds without speech. Let the passenger and audience simply look.
The framing should reveal distance and weather, not resemble a low forest tunnel.

03 / 18-30 seconds / A WORLD PEOPLE LIVE ON
An explicit time cut from shot 02 keeps the window as a familiar frame but lowers
the viewpoint into the approach. Continue looking sideways. Foreground cloud wisps and nearer terrain pass faster
than distant hills. Managed clearings, field divisions and freight corridors
gradually establish human scale. The same river or ridge carries through the cut.
Allow a restrained passenger reaction: a hand loosens on the frame, or a quiet
shift toward the glass. No assigned player face or compulsory spoken reaction.
Optional voice: begin Castle's existing Nessa memory, if moved here from the
voiced prologue rather than played twice.

04 / 30-42 seconds / THE PORT COMES INTO VIEW
Stay inside. Roads lead into cargo districts, processing sheds and a working
spaceport. Yard cranes, queues and controlled approaches make civilization
visible. Retain vegetation and weather at the city's edges.
The port district passes abeam while the landing berth remains farther ahead.
Do not put a forward destination into a purely lateral window. This view can
show perimeter booths and sheltered transfer routes; the exterior shot establishes
the actual apron, approach centerline and cradle together.
Optional voice: finish the Nessa memory. Her remark about opinions on this ship
sets up the exterior cut. Keep the first surface reveal free of this dialogue.

05 / 42-52 seconds / WHAT BROUGHT THEM HERE
Use a second explicit time cut to the final aligned approach. First clear exterior
view, on the same side of the approach axis, showing the ship and its destination.
Second Wind's
cratered bow, emergency backing, welded seams and heavy industrial volumes now
read against the scale of the yard. Show it level and aligned with the cradle,
slowing along an unobstructed approach. The passenger's window location must
agree with the prior interior view.
Use the accepted V3 component placement and heavy-arrival skin. Do not relocate
the pod, turrets or engines between cameras. No new damage blooms during flight.
Sound: perspective changes from cabin vibration to exterior machinery and air.
This cut is the reveal of the ship's condition, not another entry burn.

06 / 52-65 seconds / SOMETHING ELSE TAKES THE WEIGHT
A steady side-wide view first establishes a hold above the cradle with fore-aft
drift arrested, then the final short descent and load transfer.
Supports meet suitable frame points, leaving the side pod, boat opening and
engine bells clear. Retaining arms secure the hull only after support.
Let the flight noise subside. A brief return to the window may show the passenger's
hand relax; the ship has stopped shaking, and ordinary yard activity continues.
The same viewport still faces the apron: check its real seated view after landing,
including any cradle/building occlusion, instead of reusing the airborne vista.
End with the ship still visibly scarred. Keep its hatch sealed until the yard's
environmental and arrival clearance. Cut into Nessa's inspection/yard review.
