This tile: 870 m wide by 580 m tall, so one pixel is about 57 cm. It is the east end of Greywater Down's public landing field, seen from high up: the passenger ferry's berth, the arrivals hall, the freight terminal, then the fenced private repair yard Kilo Nine and the jungle beyond. Paint it like a real high-altitude aerial survey photograph of a hard-used frontier spaceport at the jungle's edge.

What the regions are:
- The big flat mid-grey area on the left: the port apron, a huge hardstand of dark weathered concrete in large cast panels with a faint grid of joint lines, rain-darkened, with patch repairs, tyre-worn paths, oil and fuel stains. Keep it fairly plain and even.
- The large pale grey disc on the left: the ferry's berth pad, a raised concrete slab about 68 m across, scorched and sooted from its centre outward where ships' drives fire. Keep every yellow marking exactly as drawn (the dashed ring, the long centre line and the cross bar), as worn, faded paint. The short dark line with a small square beside it: a steel service tower and its arm, seen from above.
- The small grey disc with a yellow ring at the bottom left: a small shuttle landing pad, concrete with a ring of yellow edge blocks.
- The grid of small coloured squares (orange, green, white, blue) and the cluster beside the pad: stacked cargo modules, painted steel roofs; the rust-brown bars: bundles of steel stock; the tiny tan squares: crates; the pair of white discs: the tops of small round fuel tanks.
- The thin yellow lines at the far left edge: the rails and girders of a gantry crane at the edge of the heavy berth. Keep them exactly as drawn.
- The grey rectangle at the top with a dark road leading to it: the freight terminal shed, a big corrugated grey metal roof with rust patches and skylight panels; the road is worn asphalt.
- The long thin white lines forming an L: the roofs of covered walkways from the ferry pad to the arrivals hall, white painted metal.
- The white square at the bottom with the orange band, orange disc and white bar: the roof of the arrivals hall, clean-ish white membrane roof with an orange stripe across it and the Hegemony crest painted large in its middle: an orange disc with a white bar across it. Keep the crest's shapes exactly as drawn, as painted roof markings.
- The dark grey bands along the bottom: the port road (asphalt) and, below it, the asphalt loading strip; the short dark strip going up from the port road: the asphalt apron at Kilo Nine's gate. The pale grey area at the very bottom left: the container yard's concrete. The brown-grey area at the very bottom right: bare dirt yard ground.
- The fenced brown-grey rectangle in the middle: Kilo Nine, a private ship repair yard. Its ground is compacted gravel and dirt with tyre ruts, oil stains and puddles; its fence is the thin dark outline, a chain-link fence seen from above (keep it thin and straight). Inside it: the large pale grey disc with yellow markings and a ring of yellow dashes is its landing pad (scorched concrete; keep the markings); the darker grey strips are its haul roads of packed dark gravel; the rust-orange square is a hangar roof (rusty corrugated metal); the large dark grey rectangle at the top is a fabrication hall roof; the orange-brown rectangle with white stripes is a stack of plate stock under a crane, the two yellow lines are the crane's rails; the grey blocks and the grey jumble near the pad are hull sections and salvage plates; the small coloured squares are stacked cargo modules; the white discs are small fuel tanks; the pale tan bars by the pad are scaffold decks; the small green-striped patch is a vegetable garden.
- The dark green area across the top and the whole right side: the jungle, dense dark green wet undergrowth and broad-leaved plants; the round darker green blobs: the crowns of jungle trees seen from above, dense leafy canopy; the brown patches along the yard's east fence: bare red-brown earth and burnt clearings with stumps.

- Vehicles in this tile: the guide now draws parked trucks, cars, carts and cargo dollies where they stand. They are part of the place, so the rule against vehicles above means only: do not invent any. Paint each drawn one exactly where it is, at its size, as the roof of that vehicle seen straight down (a cab, then a long trailer roof in its colour; a car roof; a small cart), crisp-edged, no shadow.
- The pale grey lumpy mass, where the guide has one, is the jungle's tree canopy (it is drawn grey only in the guide): paint it as dense dark green broad-leaved jungle canopy, exactly like the style reference.
- The dark, irregular, elongated blotches scattered over the apron: shallow rain puddles lying in ruts and sunken panels. Paint them as still water reflecting a pale overcast sky: dark blue-grey with a soft lighter sheen, wet darkened concrete around their ragged edges. Never solid black, never round.
- The long thin pale line along the port road's north edge with a yellow line beside it: painted apron edge lines. The thin yellow lines along both sides of each cargo lane: painted lane edges. Keep them thin, worn and exactly where drawn.
