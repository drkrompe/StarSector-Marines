This tile: 144 m wide by 96 m tall, so one pixel is about 9.4 cm. It is part of Kilo Nine, a private ship repair yard run by one tough salvage engineer, fenced, at the edge of the port, with the jungle beyond its east fence. This is the north-west corner: the turning ground for heavy trucks, and the parts store.

What the regions are:
- Brown-grey ground everywhere between things: the yard's surface of compacted gravel and dirt. Tyre ruts, oil and fuel stains, damp patches and shallow puddles, scattered grit, a few weeds and grass tufts where nothing drives.
- The dark grey vertical strip at the far left: the yard's haul road of packed dark gravel with patches of old asphalt, deep tyre tracks, mud at the edges. It turns along the top.
- The large dark grey rectangle at the top: the turning ground, a wide hardstand of packed dark gravel and broken asphalt with sweeping curved tyre tracks, oil stains and puddles.
- The grid of small blue, green, orange and white rectangles: the roofs of stacked cargo modules, ribbed painted steel with rust streaks, scuffs and stencil-free panels. This is the parts store.
- The long thin yellow straight lines running north-south: the rails of an overhead plate crane, heavy steel rails on a concrete ground beam painted safety yellow, worn.
- The grey rectangles at the right: stacked steel plate and salvaged hull sections, grey primer and bare steel, weld scars, rust.
- The green blobs at the very top: the crowns of trees beyond the north fence.
