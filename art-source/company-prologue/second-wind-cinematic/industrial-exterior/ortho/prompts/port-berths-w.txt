This tile: 1008 m wide by 672 m tall, so one pixel is about 66 cm. It is the west half of Greywater Down's public landing field, seen from very high up: a vast apron with a row of freighter berths, small shuttle pads, a fuel farm and the walled heavy berth, the jungle beyond it at the top and the edge of the industrial belt at the bottom. Paint it like a real high-altitude aerial survey photograph of a busy, hard-used frontier spaceport.

Registration matters more than anything here. Much of this tile is plain apron, so it is easy to let things drift: they must not. The jungle's edge stays about one third of the way down the frame, the berth pads stay where they are in the lower half, and the roads and the container yard stay at the very bottom, every one on exactly the same pixel rows and columns as in the guide.

What the regions are:

Keep it fairly plain and even; it is very large. Keep its overall tone a medium grey like the guide's, not darker: the tile beside it paints the same apron.
- The five large pale grey discs in a row: freighter berth pads, raised concrete slabs about 68 m across, each heavily scorched and sooted from its centre outward where ships' drives fire, with oil rings and patched panels. Keep every yellow marking exactly as drawn (the dashed ring, the long centre line and the cross bar), as worn, faded paint.

- The small clusters of coloured squares beside the pads (orange, green, blue, white): stacked cargo modules, painted steel roofs; the rust-brown bars beside them: bundles of steel stock; the tiny tan squares: crates. The pairs of white discs: the tops of small round fuel tanks. The little groups of thin coloured bars below some pads: shipping containers set down on the apron, ribbed roofs in their own colours.
- The dark grey strips running down from the pads and along the bottom: asphalt roads and cargo lanes, worn and patched, the long one across the bottom is the port road. No painted road lines.

Keep every yellow line exactly as drawn. The thin coloured bars below it: shipping containers staged for an incoming ship. The small coloured squares cut by the right edge: more stacked cargo modules.

- At the bottom: the dark grey band is the asphalt loading strip at the port's edge; the pale grey area below it is the container yard's concrete; the brown-grey patch at the bottom left is bare dirt yard ground.
