Shot plate: the YARD layer for a grounded, photographic science-fiction film. Outside, ground level, late morning on the humid jungle world Jangala, at a private repair cradle in a working spaceport yard. The camera stands on the yard's perimeter walkway, about four metres up. 16:9, the widest landscape canvas.

What the clay stands for:

The dark steel blocks on it are the cradle's contact pads, clamp slides and the low rails between them: massive, scuffed, oily steel.
- A large ship is about to stand in this cradle, but the ship is a SEPARATE LAYER: do NOT paint any ship or hull. The cradle stands empty. Paint the area between and around the pads as ground in deep, soft shade, as if a big hull overhead were shading it (the slightly darker zone in the clay).
- The lighter grey ground in the foreground is the yard apron: dry, stained, patched concrete.
- The yellow frame at right is a sheltered crew gantry: a small roofed steel shelter with a yellow-painted frame, a corrugated roof, a mesh back screen, a few equipment lockers and gas bottles under it, and a slim jib hoist on a mast at its left end with a steel cable hanging straight down from the jib head to an empty hook at about head height.
- The long building behind is a weathered repair hall with warm cream cladding, a sawtooth roof and big roller doors; the square tower is the yard's control tower with a dark glazed cab; the low buildings at right are offices and stores. Faded teal tanks or service units may appear by the walls.
- The bottom boxes are, from left: a low yellow-and-grey concrete yard barrier row, and at bottom right a heavy cable reel and a battered service cart.
