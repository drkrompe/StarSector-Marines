A finished matte painting for a film: a photoreal view from low orbit, 250 km up, looking
forward and down across a desert world to its curved horizon. Wide 16:9 landscape canvas,
50 mm lens, flat morning light from the left and behind.

Image 1 is the exact layout authority, a rough projection of this very ground through the
shot's camera. Keep everything exactly where it is, at the same size and in the same colours:
the horizon line and its curve, the pale sand sea on the left, the dark canyon-cut rock massif,
the olive basin at right, the pale salt pan, and the three settlements and their roads. Do not
move, add, remove or rescale anything, and keep the framing pixel for pixel.

Repaint it at full photographic detail, the way an astronaut's photograph from orbit looks:
- Near ground sharp: dune ridges with a consistent wind grain, sharp canyons with pale floors
  and dark walls, mesas and talus. Detail falls off with distance into a soft, thin, pale-blue
  atmospheric haze that thickens toward the horizon, and a thin bright limb of atmosphere
  along the curve.
- Above the limb: black space, no stars, no moon, nothing else.
- The settlements stay small, as seen from 400-700 km: the walled city at the canyon mouth on
  the edge of the sand sea, the strip mine on the massif's far flank, the spaceport's grid of
  pads on the salt pan, faint road lines between them.
No ships, plasma trails, clouds, lens flare, text, labels or border.
