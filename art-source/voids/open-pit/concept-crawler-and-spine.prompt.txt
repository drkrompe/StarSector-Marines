Concept art for a top-down tactical science-fiction game: one finished concept frame, 16:9 landscape.

Image 1 is the layout authority: the game's own overhead view of an open-cut ore pit on a desert world, terraced in six benches down to a floor with a murky sump, a haul road spiralling down the benches, a dark conveyor leaving to the upper right toward the processing works off-frame. Images 2 and 3 are the setting's own illustrations of a mine and an industrial megafacility: material and design language only (Domain-era spacefaring industry: heavy, worn, ribbed and plated hulls, lattice gantries, cable rigs, thick pipework, rust, grime, sodium-orange work lights, hazard striping). Not layout, not camera.

This is a starfaring colony's planetary megaproject, not a modern Earth quarry: no recognisable modern trucks, excavators or bulldozers. Machines are the size of buildings, read clearly from far above, and look old, repaired and hard-used, never clean or glossy. No people, no text, no labels, no UI, no border.

The ore's path must make plausible sense and be easy to follow by eye: from the face where the ore is cut, through the machine that takes it, up and out of the pit, toward the works at the upper right edge of the frame.

Option A, "Crawler and spine": on the pit floor a crawling cutting-head excavator the size of a city block, on four heavy track units, a rotating bucket wheel / cutting drum biting into the foot of a bench face, a long discharge boom swinging back. Behind it a tracked mobile crusher station receives the ore, and from it the SPINE starts: a heavy covered conveyor gallery on lattice trestles, one straight bold line climbing the benches on its own incline cut, over the rim through a transfer tower, then running on across the ground toward the works at the upper right. Two or three tracked ore crawlers (low armoured hauler hulls, no cabs like Earth trucks) carry ore from an upper working bench down the haul road to a hopper on the spine. A drilling gantry straddles one upper bench, a row of drill masts hanging from it, ready for the next blast.
