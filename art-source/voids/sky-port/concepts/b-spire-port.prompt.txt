Concept frame for a top-down tactical battle game (1920x1080 landscape). The
battle is fought on the roof deck of a colossal sky port megastructure that
rises out of an endless, hazy megacity hundreds of metres below. The camera
looks STRAIGHT DOWN (true overhead, no tilt, no horizon, no sky). The
attached render is the camera and projection authority: the roof deck
occupies the upper-left of the frame, seen flat from above, and the
structure's walls fall away from the deck's edges in steep perspective,
converging toward the centre of the frame, down to the city far below. Keep
that framing, projection and vanishing point; replace everything else.

The roof deck must stay readable as a flat, walkable battlefield: landing
pads, apron markings, cargo, low roof plant and parapets, at the scale of
soldiers about 1 m across in a 60 m wide view. Below the deck's edge,
everything shows real depth: the structure's own lower tiers, neighbouring
megastructures with their own roofs at different depths, haze between
layers, and the city floor glowing deep below.

Painterly-realistic, grounded, game-art finish; no text, no UI, no labels,
no border, no vignette, no people drawn larger than 1 m.

Direction B, spire sky port (Star Wars city-planet): the deck is the crown
of a tall, elegant spire. Round landing platforms on slender arms jut out
from the structure at several levels below the deck, each with its own
markings and parked craft. Ribbed buttresses run down the faces. Streams of
air traffic flow in lanes between neighbouring towers at different depths.
Bright, pale stone and steel, warm afternoon haze, the city a sunlit
blue-grey sea of towers far below.
