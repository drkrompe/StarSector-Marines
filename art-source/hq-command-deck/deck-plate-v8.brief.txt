Use case: sketch-to-render illustration, background plate. Asset type: wide landscape establishing illustration for Starsector Marines, a grounded mercenary ground-operations game; it becomes a layer of a banner across the top of the company's headquarters screen. Game screens and the view outside are drawn behind this painting, through holes keyed in it.

Reference 1 is a grey 3D blockout rendered from the exact camera of this shot. It is the geometry and layout authority: keep every object where it is, at its size, with its silhouette, perspective and horizon. Paint over it; do not move, add, remove, resize or re-angle anything, and do not change the camera.
Reference 2 is an earlier painting of this room. It is colour, brushwork and figure-painting reference only: take its neutral gunmetal and its restrained red-orange window light, and ignore its layout, its heavy rust, its rivets and its blue screens.

KEY COLOURS, the most important instruction. Two surfaces in reference 1 are flat key colours, and the painting must keep them as flat, uniform, unlit fill, like a green screen in a film:
- Every magenta surface in reference 1 is a screen. Fill it with pure solid magenta, exactly #FF00FF, edge to edge, up to its bezel.
- Every green surface in reference 1 is a window. Fill it with pure solid green, exactly #00FF00, edge to edge, up to the pillars, sill and crown. No stars, no planet, no space, no reflections, no glass effect: only the flat green.
No gradient, texture, shading, glare, vignette or painted detail inside either key colour. Neither colour lights anything: no magenta or green glow, reflection or tint on faces, hands, desks, seats, pillars, sills or floor. Use magenta and green nowhere else. Anything in front of a key surface in reference 1 (a person, a seat, a pillar, a microphone, a rail) is painted normally on top of it with a crisp edge.

Scene: the working bridge of a military starship, seen across the ship with the bow off to the left. A moulded, purpose-built late-1990s science-fiction starship interior, in the manner of a practical film set: smooth faceted grey composite and painted-metal panels with crisp seams, chamfered edges, flush access plates and small stencilled-looking markings with no legible text. Surfaces are moulded and clean-edged, not riveted plate. Big raked window openings run along the far side and wrap to the left, between heavy leaning pillars with lit bands. Overhead, faceted soffits and beams with recessed light troughs, and one monolithic cowl hung from the deck head. In the middle, a large rectangular plotting table. Shell seats on thick deck pedestals face raked consoles dense with keypads, small lit keys and gooseneck microphones.

The console on the back wall at right is built into the bulkhead, moulded in one piece from deck to head height, with its two screens let deep into a raked face, rows of small lit keys let into the deck below them and a brow with a light strip above. It is 1990s embedded military computer hardware. Paint it nothing like office furniture or a modern flat panel: no stand, no drawers, no separate keyboard. Beside it, a plain moulded counter with a paper ledger, a clipboard, binders and a mug, under a roster board with pinned cards. Further right a wheeled pressure hatch, and a few exposed pipes above: the working end of the bridge. Wear is light and only where hands go: scuffed deck plate, rubbed paint on seat edges and console nosings, a little grime in seams. A mercenary company has owned this ship for years, so it is clean and maintained but not new. Almost no rust.

Style: detailed late-1990s/early-2000s science-fiction strategy-game illustration, matte-painted cinematic realism, visible restrained brush texture, the same painted family as the game's character portraits.

Palette and light: neutral gunmetal and cool steel grey, near-black shadow, cool white from the overhead troughs and pillar bands, and a thin, dim red-orange line in the troughs along the windows only, far more restrained than in reference 2. Tiny amber and pale indicator keys. Cool pale blue light falls softly on hands and faces at the consoles. Deep shadow under the consoles and round the deck, faint haze in the air.

Paint the plotting table top as a flat, evenly lit, pale blue-grey surface with only the one faint ring and cross the reference shows.

The crew: the smooth mid-grey mannequins in reference 1 are the only people in the painting. Each one, and every one, becomes a person in the exact seat and pose of its mannequin: the two near the camera, and the helm and navigator on the raised platform at left. Men and women of the ship's company dressed as naval officers of a late-1990s science-fiction film's starship: pressed, fitted mid-grey fleet tunics with a high standing collar, a plain metal collar pin and clean shoulder seams, worn buttoned and correct, hair short or pinned up in a neat bun, slim headsets. Disciplined, upright posture, backs to us, hands on their consoles, faces turned away. Not fatigues, not rolled sleeves, not armour. A seat without a mannequin stays empty: the two seats at the consoles just left of the plotting table are empty, and must show their bare seat backs with nobody in them. Nobody stands anywhere.

Constraints: no text, lettering, numbers, logos, insignia other than the plain collar pins, HUD elements, frames or watermark. No holograms, no floating screens, no weapons. Opaque, edge-to-edge painting. Use the widest landscape canvas available, as close to 3:1 as possible, matching the reference's framing edge to edge.
