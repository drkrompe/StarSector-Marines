Concept sheet: four small Tri-Tachyon corporate battlefield COMBAT DRONES, each about a metre long, built as LIFTING BODIES. Each is a single flat blended hull whose whole planform is its lift surface, held up by a gravitic lift plate on its belly (invisible from above except for a thin cyan underglow at the hull edge) and steered by tiny thruster ports at its tips. Clean, flat, manta- or arrowhead-like planforms, smooth composite plating with panel lines, no protruding propulsion.
- ESCORT: a broad arrowhead with a pulse-laser emitter set into the leading edge on the centreline and a small sensor eye behind it.
- SENSOR: a wide thin crescent flying-wing whose leading edge is a long strip of dark sensor glass with a cyan scan line.
- POINT-DEFENCE: a compact delta with one small low laser turret dome on its back and two emitter lenses at the wing tips.
- STRIKE: a narrow swept dart lifting body with a brass warhead nose and a cyan hazard band, the smallest and sharpest of the four.
Tri-Tachyon high-tech livery and material: pearl white and pale grey composite with fine graphite seams, restrained cyan lights; image 2 is livery and material reference only, not layout or shape.
Strict 90-degree overhead orthographic view, no perspective tilt, lit from the upper left, every drone nose UP (toward the top edge), the four drones evenly spaced in one row across a wide landscape canvas, each drawn large and with a small plain caption beneath it naming its role (ESCORT, SENSOR, POINT-DEFENCE, STRIKE). Plain flat dark-grey background, no ground, scene, shadow slab, border or grid. Rendered as finished top-down game sprites in the manner of the attached approved game sprite: grounded, believable, subtly weathered, crisp and readable when shrunk to 40 pixels, not glossy concept art.
BANNED SHAPES, do not draw any of these anywhere: exposed fans, fan blades, rotor rings, rotor discs, propellers, ducted props, shrouded lift fans, round fan ducts, grilles over circular ducts, quadcopter arms. No drone may be built around a circle. The outline must come from what the drone does (its weapon, sensor or warhead and how it is steered), never from a lift fan.
