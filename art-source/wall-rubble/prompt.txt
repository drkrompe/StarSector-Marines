A sheet of sixteen pieces of building rubble for a top-down tactical ground-combat game: what a shot-up or collapsed
building wall leaves lying on the ground at its foot.

Rows, top to bottom (each row is one kind of debris; the four columns are variants of it):
1. Heaps. A long, low pile of broken wall heaped against where the wall stood, along the top of its cell: broken
   blocks, plaster slabs, splintered timber and bent rebar, piled highest along its top edge and thinning toward the
   bottom, where loose chunks have rolled off. Clearly a three-dimensional heap with lit tops and shadowed undersides.
2. Slabs. One or two large broken pieces of wall lying flat: a chunk of masonry or cast wall with ragged broken
   edges, a few courses or a reinforcing bar showing at the break, chipped corners.
3. Clusters. A handful of mid-sized broken blocks and wall fragments lying together, each with a small contact shadow.
4. Scatter. Small fragments, grit and pebbles thrown out across the ground, over a faint pale dust patch (the blurred
   grey) that fades out to nothing at its edges.

Layout: reference 1 is the exact layout. It is a 4 x 4 grid of equal square cells on a transparent canvas, and each
flat grey shape is the footprint of one piece of debris (blurred grey means a soft, feathered dust edge). Fill every
grey footprint with its debris, keeping its position, size and outline. Do not move, merge, add or remove pieces.
Keep every cell's empty space fully transparent.

Colour: the game tints each piece by the wall it fell from (plaster, brick, timber, concrete, ceramic panel), so
paint every piece in neutral, light warm greys: broken faces pale, the old wall surface a little darker, cracks and
undersides dark charcoal, rebar and timber only slightly darker grey. No strong hue anywhere: never red brick, green,
sand yellow or blue.

Style: painted, slightly gritty realistic game art, seen from straight overhead, lit from the upper left, matching
reference 2. Read clearly at small size: strong light-and-shadow on every chunk so it reads as solid.

Output: a true transparent background, including every gap between and inside pieces; no ground, no floor, no wall,
no cast shadow slab, no grid lines, no border, no text or labels. Each piece must stay inside its own cell.

Reference 2 is material and style only: rubble from the same game. Do not copy its layout, shapes or colours.
